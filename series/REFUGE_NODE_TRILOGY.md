## Series identity

Medical science fiction exploring what happens to healing, identity, and consent when human nervous systems can directly interface with the Boundary Field.

Where the Asterion Trilogy asks *can humanity survive contact with what it has stumbled into*, the Refuge Node Trilogy asks *what does it cost to repair someone who survived it*.

Tone: quieter, darker, and more intimate than Asterion. No academy rankings. No combat sport. The stakes are a single patient in a single room, which makes them feel larger.

Protagonist: **Dr. Sable Hren** — a civilian Boundary Medicine specialist operating a small licensed facility attached to a research hospital on the Pacific seaboard. Standard Six classification. Unremarkable combat record. Exceptionally perceptive diagnostician.

What Sable has that military Resonants do not: she watches systems at rest. She has spent twelve years learning what a body does when it stops fighting.

# BOOK 1 — THE QUIET WARD

**Promise:** Make readers feel that a hospital corridor is the most dangerous place in the world.

Primary arc:
Sable Hren must treat a patient whose neural-field architecture she has never seen before — and who cannot tell her what happened to them because they no longer know what they are.

External arc:
A civilian doctor discovers that the government classification apparatus surrounding Resonance medicine has a hole in it. Something is being hidden in the category of cases that officially do not exist.

Mystery answers in Book 1:
- The patient in Room Four is not malingering.
- Standard Seven does not fix what happened to him.
- There is a category of Boundary Field damage that has no name in the clinical literature.
- Someone above the hospital administrator knew this patient was coming.

Mysteries carried forward:
- Who suppressed the case documentation?
- Why does the field signature match nothing in human records?
- What is the patient trying to say when language fails?
- How many other cases of this type exist?
- What does Contact Zero have to do with a healer in a civilian hospital?

Ending:
Sable cannot fix the patient. She can only keep him stable. She discovers a third clinical file — sealed under military medical authority — that suggests he is not the first. A government liaison arrives the day the file disappears.

# BOOK 2 — THE RESONANT WOUND

**Promise:** Expand the medical world before expanding the mystery.

Core frame:
Sable takes on a consulting role for a government working group studying unclassified Resonance trauma cases. She begins to see the pattern the individual cases obscure.

New character:
**Physician-Captain Oren Thiel** — military Boundary Medicine, Standard Seven, assigned to the same working group. He knows more than he says and is not the antagonist. He is someone who stopped asking questions too early and is not sure whether he regrets it.

Major reveals:
- The unnamed damage category is not caused by combat or accident. It is caused by proximity to something operating at a field level that human Resonance cannot produce or classify.
- Cases cluster geographically and temporally. The cluster pattern points toward the outer solar system transit corridor.
- The government does not know what is causing the cases. The government knows that not knowing is easier to manage than knowing.
- A Standard Eight practitioner who attempted identity reconstruction on the first known case never recovered.

Emotional arc:
Sable has spent her career treating people the system gave up on. She must decide whether uncovering this is healing or harm.

Ending:
A new case arrives. The patient is not human. The hospital administrator does not call it in.

---

# BOOK 3 — THE CONSENT STANDARD

**Promise:** Pay off every clinical detail at the scale of what the universe is actually about.

Core conflict:
Sable must attempt Therapeutic Consensus — the extralegal Standard Nine application — on a patient who cannot consent in any language she speaks.

Major reveal:
The non-human patient is a fragment of the Refuge Node. Not the Node itself. A detached Resonant component operating at low capacity. It has been trying to communicate by generating human-pattern trauma signatures because those are the only field frequencies the people around it respond to.

It is not hurting people. It is calling for help in the only language it learned from observing what happens when it is nearby.

Final problem:
Consent. Sable cannot perform Therapeutic Consensus on an entity she does not understand, in a field-space she cannot map, for a patient that the law does not recognize as a patient.

The solution:
She does not heal the fragment. She teaches it that there are other ways to be heard. The field signature of the facility changes. A long-range signal changes character. This is never explicitly explained. The reader understands it; the characters do not.

Final image:
Room Four, empty. Clean. Sable writes a case note she will never submit. Closes the file. Goes to find her next patient.

## Character roster

### Dr. Sable Hren
Age: 38
Standard: Six
Institutional affiliation: Pacific Meridian Boundary Medicine Center, civilian
Core competence: diagnostic perception; reading field states at rest; patience with ambiguous data
Core wound: she could not save the first patient who trusted her with a Standard Seven intervention. She has limited herself deliberately since.
False belief: the safest medicine is the most careful medicine.
Need: to understand that care and risk are the same thing, finally taken seriously.

Age: 29
Role: Sable's senior resident; Standard Three practitioner

Age: 44
Standard: Seven
Voice: measured, deliberate, carefully imprecise
Function: knows the shape of what Sable is finding without knowing its source; his restraint is not cowardice but a specific failure of nerve he cannot admit to himself

## Continuity rules — Refuge Node Trilogy

1. **Same physics, different scale.** All Nine Standards apply as established in UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md. No new capabilities invented for medical use. Medical applications are extensions, not exceptions.

2. **No Kade Mercer.** He exists in the world. He does not appear. If the timeline is contemporaneous with Asterion Book 1–2, Sable may read about the Asterion anomaly in news reports. She finds it interesting and returns to her patient.

3. **The Refuge Node is never named.** Characters may develop theories. The word "Refuge Node" does not appear in the trilogy until the final pages of Book 3, and then only in Sable's private case note.

4. **Contact Zero is background, not backstory.** Sable knows the public history of Resonance Discovery. She does not know the classified history. The series does not reveal it. The reader of the Asterion trilogy will understand what Sable cannot.

5. **No villain.** The antagonist force is institutional inertia and the difference between what the system was built to handle and what is actually happening.

6. **Medical accuracy in spirit.** The Boundary Medicine procedures should feel as specific and consequential as real medicine. Informed consent, documentation, liability, licensing, hierarchy of care — these matter and should function correctly within the universe's rules.

7. **Connected-universe discipline.** A reader who has never read Asterion should finish the Refuge Node Trilogy satisfied. A reader who has read Asterion should finish it shaken.
