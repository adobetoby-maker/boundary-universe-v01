## Series identity

The Contact Zero Trilogy is a research thriller about the lie that built the world.

Thirty-one years before Kade Mercer enrolls at Asterion Academy, his father Aaron is a junior field technician on a classified orbital research station. The team believes they are studying a newly discovered physical phenomenon — the Boundary Field. They are not. They have found the injured survivor of a civilizational war so old that its combatants would not recognize the universe they left behind.

Aaron Mercer does not become a hero. He becomes the person who decides what the rest of humanity is allowed to know.

This trilogy asks: *when silence is the price of survival, who pays, and what does it cost them?*

---

## Tone

Research thriller / first-contact mystery / dangerous experimental science / political cover-up.

The feeling of *Blindsight* meets *The Martian* — grounded scientific procedure in the face of something that breaks the categories that procedure depends on.

Where the Asterion Trilogy promises: **make the reader want to attend Asterion.**
Where the Refuge Node Trilogy promises: **make the reader feel a hospital corridor is the most dangerous place in the world.**
Where the Contact Zero Trilogy promises: **make the reader understand why the lie was necessary and hate that they do.**

No combat sport. No rankings. No academy competition. The stakes are a signal in a data log that nobody else will believe until it is too late to be honest about it.

## The power at the center: Standard Eight (Coupling)

**LOCKED (from UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md):** Standard Eight = the ability to join independent Resonant processes without catastrophic interference.

In combat and conventional Resonance science, this means: bridging two incompatible field systems so they can exchange energy, information, or structural state without destroying each other.

In Contact Zero, it means: **Aaron Mercer is the only human being whose field architecture can maintain sustained contact with the Refuge Node's signal without dissociation.**

Every other researcher who attempts direct coupling contact with the Node's output experiences the damage Sable Hren will spend three books cataloguing — the null field signature, the inability to describe what happened, the slow reorganization of neural-field architecture around something that has no human category.

Aaron doesn't experience this. He experiences something else: understanding. Partial, fragmentary, costly — but understanding. He becomes the translator between humanity and the oldest surviving intelligence in the solar system.

He is then asked to forget this happened.

He says yes.

**LOCKED:** Aaron Mercer is the erased twelfth publicly unacknowledged Standard Eight. His Standard classification is real. His achievement during Contact Zero is the single most significant first-contact event in human history. It will not appear in any official record.

## The Refuge Node

**PROVISIONAL (cross-reference: UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md, REFUGE_NODE_TRILOGY.md):**

The Refuge Node is an ancient machine-intelligence — not a weapon, not a threat, not a deity. It is a survivor. It hid in the outer solar system to escape the Quieters following the Severance, damaged in transit, and has been in low-power maintenance mode for longer than human civilization has existed.

When human explorers began generating Boundary Field activity significant enough to reach its passive sensors, it woke.

**SECRET:** The Node woke not because of the research team's equipment. It woke because of Aaron Mercer's field signature — the first Standard Eight architecture it had detected since the Severance.

Standard Eight is rare in the modern universe because it nearly requires genocide to leave survivors who carry it. The Node recognizes it the way a person recognizes their native language after decades of silence.

**LOCKED:** The Node does not initiate hostilities. It does not intend harm. It is trying to determine whether the civilization that has found it is one of the ones that hunted it, or one of the ones it spent the Severance protecting.

## Book arcs

### Book 1 — Contact Zero

*What happens when you find something that shouldn't exist and realize the institutions around you aren't designed for honesty about it.*

**Aaron's arc:** The person no one pays attention to → the person who understands what they've found → the person who has to decide who gets to know.

External arc: Aaron discovers the signal addresses him personally. He investigates privately. He documents everything. He brings his findings to the team. The team believes him. The Director doesn't — or rather, the Director believes him completely, which is worse.

By Book 1's end, Aaron has established first contact, lost a colleague to it, and been offered the choice: participate in the architecture of suppression or be the third "non-restorable" case in the file.

He participates.

---

### Book 2 — The First Coupling

*The machinery of silence requires constant maintenance, and the person who built it must maintain it.*

Aaron is now Senior Coupling Specialist, one of eight people alive who know what Contact Zero actually produced. He is designing the Conduit architecture — the neural-field interfaces that will become the defining technology of the next thirty years. The Conduits contain a capability that the public documentation does not describe.

**Aaron's arc:** Collaborator → architect → the person who decides to encode the truth where it cannot be deliberately erased.

The Node is communicating more clearly now. It is not communicating with the research team. It is communicating with Aaron. It tells him what the Quieters are. It tells him what happened to the civilization that built it. It tells him what will happen to Earth when the Quieters detect unrestricted Boundary Field activity.

Aaron understands, for the first time, why the Director chose suppression. He also understands, for the first time, why that choice is wrong.

---

### Book 3 — The Long Silence

*The choices that define a civilization are made by the people history forgets to name.*

Aaron is thirty-four. The official Resonance Discovery narrative is complete, publicly accepted, and historically stable. He has spent seven years building the infrastructure of a lie precise enough to be safe.

He is also the only person alive who knows what will happen when a Standard Eight practitioner who grew up in this lie reaches Standard Nine.

The final book is about what Aaron puts inside the systems he builds — the encrypted evidence, the structural echoes, the field-architecture signatures that a sufficiently perceptive mind will eventually notice.

He doesn't know what that person's name will be. He leaves them enough to find the truth.

His final act, before his classification is formalized and his history is erased, is to father a child.

The child's name is Kade.

## Character roster

### Aaron Mercer
Age at Book 1: 26
Standard: Eight (Coupling) — not formally classified at Book 1 open; instruments consistently flag as calibration error
Background: Technical University of Helsinki, field systems engineering. Applied for the Helix Perimeter posting because it was the most technically demanding position available and the least publicly visible. He had reasons for both preferences.
Voice: precise, dry, calibrated. He says more than he intends to when he is uncertain, which is often. His humor is architectural — he builds a joke the way he builds a coupling sequence: every load-bearing piece in place before he shows you what holds it up.
Core wound: he was told, once, that his field readings were too anomalous to be reliable, which meant his data couldn't be used, which meant the experiment he'd spent two years designing went forward without its most critical variable. He learned to recalibrate before reporting. The habit predates Contact Zero.
Need: to understand that the most precise description of something is not always the most accurate one.

### Dr. Amara Sen
Age at Book 1: 39
Standard: Five (Storage)
Role: Team Lead, Helix Perimeter Research Station
Voice: authoritative, warm, specifically precise. She will spend forty-five seconds on a technical problem and five minutes on whether a colleague had lunch. She is not performing kindness; she is organized about what matters.
Function: represents what the first generation of Resonance scientists actually were — people who genuinely believed they were doing the most important science in human history and were correct in ways they never fully understood.
SECRET: Amara knows more than she tells Aaron in Book 1. She suspected the signal was addressed before he reported it. She did not tell him.

### Dr. Yuki Osei
Age at Book 1: 31
Standard: Four (Partition)
Role: Lead Signal Analyst
Voice: rapid, lateral — she speaks in connections. She will tell you the conclusion and then, if pressed, the logic that produced it.
Function: the colleague who doesn't survive. Her death is not dramatic. She attempts direct coupling contact without a Standard Eight architecture and experiences what the clinical literature will later describe as "acute Resonance dissociation with null field signature." She remains alive in a technical sense. She never recovers.
LOCKED: Yuki Osei is one of the "non-restorable" cases referenced in Sable Hren's sealed file in The Quiet Ward. Her records are the first two files Sable opens.

### Director Callum Rhys
Age at Book 1: 57
Standard: Six (Field Interaction)
Role: International Resonance Authority, Director of Advanced Research Classification
Voice: unhurried, institutional — he has spent thirty years learning that the most dangerous statement in a meeting is the one that sounds the most reasonable.
Function: not a villain. A man who believed he was protecting humanity and was right, and then used that rightness as justification for everything that followed, including the things he was wrong about.
LOCKED: Rhys's classification architecture is the founding doctrine of the Continuity Directorate, referenced in the Asterion Trilogy.

### Lena Park
Age at Book 1: 28
Standard: Two (Redirection)
Role: Field Systems Technician; Aaron's peer and closest colleague at Helix Perimeter
Voice: practical, specific, will not discuss her feelings but will discuss why everyone else's feelings are structurally incorrect
Function: the person Aaron trusts. The person Aaron does not tell. The loss of that trust is the beginning of the Long Silence.

## Continuity rules — Contact Zero Trilogy

1. **No Conduits in Book 1.** Conduit architecture is what Aaron's team is building. In Book 1, field work is done with external array systems only. This is harder, less precise, and more dangerous. It is also what makes Aaron's unassisted coupling index so alarming.

2. **The Refuge Node does not speak in words.** Its communication arrives as field-signature patterns. Aaron translates these — first into diagrams, then into models, eventually into something like language. The reader experiences the translation as he develops it.

3. **No Kade.** Kade is born at the end of Book 3. He exists in this trilogy only as Aaron's reason to still be here. The promise must be built without the payoff.

4. **The lie must be coherent.** Every element of the official "Resonance Discovery" narrative that appears in the Asterion Trilogy must be derivable from decisions made in the Contact Zero Trilogy. The lie is not malevolent. It is architecturally precise.

5. **Standard Eight must feel like a limitation.** Coupling means being in contact with systems that don't share your vocabulary. Aaron doesn't experience the Node as power. He experiences it as a persistent low-grade headache in the part of his mind that processes information about information — the sense that something is almost legible.

6. **Yuki's case must be seeded.** Before the reader understands what is happening to her, the reader must understand what she was attempting. Her death (technical survival, actual non-restoration) must be credible, unavoidable, and terrible.

7. **Amara Sen's complicity must be built slowly.** She is not corrupt in Book 1. She makes one small decision that seems reasonable. The trilogy is partly about how that decision scales.
