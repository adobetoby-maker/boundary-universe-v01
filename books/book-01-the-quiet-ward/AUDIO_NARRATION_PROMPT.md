# AUDIO NARRATION PROMPT — THE QUIET WARD

Single female narrator, natural audiobook performance. Contemporary American accent, Pacific seaboard register. Read with clinical restraint — this is a medical thriller, not a drama. The performance should feel like a very good doctor explaining something complicated with measured precision.

## Character direction

**Dr. Sable Hren** — Thirty-eight. Intelligent, dry, precise. Narration is internal and observational, never emotional in the melodramatic sense. She notices things. She catalogs them. Dialogue is one sentence longer than necessary when she is uncertain. Her uncertainty is the warmest thing about her.

**Ruan Sela** — Quiet, careful, slightly detached from his own story in the way of someone who has told it too many times to people who did not believe it. He speaks in complete sentences. He does not dramatize. He is more frightening for it.

**Fenn Ash** — Faster, slightly higher register, nervous energy concealed by preparation. His humor arrives at the wrong moment because he uses it to fill silence.

**Administrator Priya Colm** — Warm, institutional, unhurried. She is not a villain. Read her with genuine benevolence, which makes her more unsettling.

**Dr. Vey Orlan** — Older male voice, careful, each word placed deliberately. He knows more than he says and the pacing should carry that weight without announcing it.

**The Liaison** — Neutral, controlled, professionally pleasant. No affect. The specific flatness of someone who has learned to give nothing away.

## Performance rules

Narration should be precise and compressed — shorter sentences than Asterion Book 1, more white space between observations. Let the silence carry. This is not an action novel.

Medical and technical language (Standard Six, BM-1, field diagnostics, null reading) should be read naturally, as if these terms are as ordinary as *blood pressure* or *CT scan* — which, in this world, they are.

When instruments return null, read *null* with the same flatness as any other clinical term. Not ominous. Factual. The ominous quality emerges from the accumulation.

Let Ruan's dialogue land quietly. He should never sound distressed. He should sound like someone describing a car accident from three years ago — factual, outside the emotion of it, with the specific flatness of someone who has already used up their fear.

When Sable says *I don't know* — and she says it more than most practitioners — let it be direct rather than apologetic. It is not weakness. It is the most accurate sentence available.

Chapter endings should be still. The final line of Chapter 1 should land like a door closing in an empty corridor.

## Voice selection for synthesis

Recommended: en-US-Studio-O (female, long-form narration)
Alternate: en-US-Studio-M

Reason: Studio tier accepts SSML. The manuscript's line-breaking carries performance information that must be encoded as explicit `<break>` tags. Text-only voices (Chirp3-HD) cannot receive this direction.

Rate: 0.93 (slightly slower than Asterion — this is quieter material)
