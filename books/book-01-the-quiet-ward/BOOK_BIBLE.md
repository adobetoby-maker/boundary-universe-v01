## Story promise

A reader should finish Book 1 feeling that a hospital is a more dangerous place than a battlefield — because in a hospital, the thing that kills you is the absence of a category.

## POV

Close third person, Sable Hren throughout.
No alternate POV in Book 1. This is a diagnostic novel. We stay inside the one person doing the diagnosis.
Sanctioned exception: Chapter 12 (Fenn) — single-chapter close-third on Fenn Ash, retroactively approved at canonization review.

## Dr. Sable Hren

Age: 38
Standard: Six
Licensing: BM-1 through BM-6 civilian; BM-7 suspended pending review from a prior case.
Facility: Pacific Meridian Boundary Medicine Center, civilian, 44-bed hospital attachment
Core competence: reading Boundary Field states in biological systems at rest; patience with data that does not resolve
Core wound: seven years ago she attempted a Standard Seven trauma interrupt on a patient in acute crisis. Contraindicated by his field state, which she misread as stable. He survived the intervention with permanent personality alteration. She has treated her own caution as a form of penance ever since.
False belief: the safest medicine is the most careful medicine.
Need: to understand that care and risk are the same act, done badly or done honestly.

Her voice: precise, dry, one sentence longer than is strictly necessary when she is uncertain. She says *I don't know* more readily than most practitioners and has noticed that colleagues find this unsettling.

## Fenn Ash

Age: 29
Standard: Three
Role: Senior resident, Pacific Meridian Boundary Medicine Center
Voice: nervous humor, overprepared, fills silence with explanation. He is not wrong about things; he is wrong about which things matter.
Function: represents what Sable was at 29 — technically excellent, emotionally armored. She is harder on him than she should be because she recognizes herself in his mistakes.

## Ruan Sela — The Patient in Room Four

Age: 34
Prior occupation: Resonance field technician, civilian research contractor
Current presentation: admitted with diagnosis of acute Resonance dissociation syndrome. Insurance documentation in order. Prior facility transferred care citing *unresolvable field instability*.

Actual state: coherent enough to be present. Not coherent enough to explain what happened. Standard classification instruments return null. Not a reading error. The null is stable. Whatever his field architecture is doing, it is doing it consistently.

What he wants: to be believed.
What he cannot do: provide a history that clinical categories can accommodate.
What he remembers: a location. A sound that was not a sound. Something that looked at him.

He is not dangerous. He is not delusional. He is a man standing on the other side of an experience that has no documentation, trying to describe it to someone whose vocabulary does not yet contain it.

---

## Administrator Priya Colm

Age: 52
Voice: kind, unhurried, trained institutional warmth
Function: the system's face, and genuinely its best expression. She is not obstructing Sable out of malice. She is doing what the system trained her to do when cases exceed category. Her tragedy is that the training was not designed for this.

## Story structure

### Act I — Admission (Chapters 1–6)

Sable receives the transfer case. Ruan Sela arrives. She runs standard diagnostics. The instruments return null. She runs them again. She does not tell Colm about the null. She tells herself she will when she has a theory.

Act I closes when Fenn suggests discharging the patient on a psychiatric referral. Sable realizes she is not certain he is wrong, and that her uncertainty about whether he is wrong is itself data.

### Act II-A — Differential (Chapters 7–14)

Sable begins working through what Ruan is not. Not any documented dissociation pattern. Not a Standard misclassification. Not trauma of any mechanism she recognizes. She calls in one specialist consult, off the record.

She begins sleeping less. Not from obsession — from the specific anxiety of a diagnostician who has ruled out everything benign and has not yet found a malignant answer.

She gives Ruan a field journal. He draws the same shape, every day, that he cannot name.

Act II-A closes when she requests access to his prior facility's records and is told by Administrator Colm that the records were transferred. She asks where. The answer is: they weren't transferred here.

### Act II-B — Investigation (Chapters 15–19)

Sable contacts the prior facility. She is told the file was closed on military medical authority. She is given a name to call. The name is not reachable. She calls the field technician licensing bureau instead. Ruan Sela's license was suspended eighteen months ago following an incident report she cannot access.

Meanwhile Ruan improves slightly. Not toward resolution — toward legibility. His field architecture is not dissociating. It is reorganizing. It is doing something she does not have a word for.

He asks her, one evening, whether she has heard a signal she was not expecting. She says no. He says: *you will*.

### Act III — Arrival (Chapters 20–24)

A government liaison arrives. He does not identify his department. He asks about the patient in Room Four. Sable says Room Four is occupied. He says he knows. He asks whether she has found anything unusual in the field diagnostics. She says standard care only. He thanks her and goes to speak with Administrator Colm.

Sable searches the sealed file system that Colm uses for government-referred cases. She finds three files under military medical authority. She opens one. It is a case history with the same null field signature. The patient's name is redacted. The outcome reads: *non-restorable*.

The second file. Same signature. Outcome: *non-restorable*.

The third file is Ruan Sela. Outcome: pending.

The liaison leaves. Two days later, Ruan's file disappears from the system.

Sable writes everything she knows in a document she saves to a personal device and does not connect to the hospital network. She goes back to Room Four. Ruan Sela is still there. She sits down. She says: *I need you to tell me what you saw, and I need you to start from the beginning, and I am not going anywhere*.

The chapter ends before he answers.

## Ability limits — Sable

Standards she holds:
- BM-1 through BM-6: licensed
- BM-7: suspended (voluntary; following the prior case)

What BM-6 actually allows her to do:
- Real-time field diagnostics at range within the facility
- Passive field monitoring of up to six patients simultaneously
- Telemetric field state transmission to offsite consult (licensed use only)

What she cannot do without BM-7:
- Any trauma interrupt procedure
- Any procedure involving direct field-state alteration of a patient's nervous system
- Standard Seven applications for any purpose

She will not attempt Standard Seven work in Book 1. The suspension is not the only reason. She does not yet understand the patient well enough to risk it, and she knows that is what the prior case should have taught her.

## Diagnostic checklist — required every 3–4 chapters

Each block should include at least two:
- An actual clinical procedure with field mechanics described
- A conversation between Sable and Ruan that moves but does not resolve
- A conversation between Sable and Fenn that reveals character under institutional pressure
- A moment of Sable alone with her own data
- Something that happens in the hospital that is not related to Room Four (the world continues)
- A question the reader does not yet know to ask

Never allow four consecutive chapters to be pure diagnostic process without a human scene.

---

## What Book 1 must accomplish

Establish that:
- Boundary Medicine is specific, consequential, and rule-bound
- Ruan Sela is real and has been through something real
- The institutional machinery is not villainous, which makes it more frightening
- Sable is neither reckless nor safe — she is honest, which is something else entirely
- Something is being managed at a level above Administrator Colm and she does not know what it is

Must leave the reader wanting:
- To know what Ruan saw
- To understand the null field signature
- To see what Sable does when she finally runs out of cautious options
