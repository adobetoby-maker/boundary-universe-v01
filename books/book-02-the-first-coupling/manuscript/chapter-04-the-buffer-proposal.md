# Chapter Four: THE BUFFER PROPOSAL

## Day 2,273

The proposal reached Aaron at 05:52 and contained sixty-three pages.

Amara had attached the raw models.

This distinguished it from most proposals that reached his queue, which attached a summary of the models, a statement confirming that the models existed, and a request for Aaron to certify conclusions he could not reproduce. Her package included the Conduit replay configuration, Soren's de-identified telemetry, three candidate storage geometries, the rejected alternatives, and a four-page list of assumptions labeled by the probability that each assumption concealed a problem.

The title occupied two lines.

**TRANSIENT STORAGE MEDIATION OF NON-COUPLING PRACTITIONER CONTACT LOAD**

**PRELIMINARY DIAGNOSTIC PROTOCOL**

Aaron opened the assumptions first.

The first concerned the event in Room Two four days earlier. The source-side modulation had reduced the relational complexity presented to Soren without changing Aaron's carrier, the monitoring aperture, or the energy state of any local system. The proposal treated that reduction as evidence that contact load could vary independently from contact depth.

Accurate.

The second assumption stated that a Standard Five practitioner might hold the excluded portion of a field-state sequence in a stabilized intermediate architecture long enough for a Conduit to translate the remainder.

Unproven.

The third stated that the holding operation could remain separate from interpretation. A Storage practitioner would not need to understand the sequence, join its incompatible systems, or expose a secondary practitioner to the full structure. The practitioner would accept a bounded transfer into a defined resonant state, maintain it for no more than four hundred milliseconds, and release it into the Conduit's dissipation lattice under limiter control.

Optimistic.

The fourth assumption stated that Aaron could couple all participating systems without allowing the stored state to treat the practitioner as an available reservoir.

The document marked that assumption **REQUIRES PRIMARY-OPERATOR DETERMINATION**.

Aaron appreciated accurate routing.

He moved to the models.

The building remained quiet around him. Geneva's secure facility occupied three connected structures built in different decades and persuaded into one institution through enclosed bridges, classification doors, and a shared inability to maintain consistent floor levels. Aaron's office sat in the oldest structure. Its stone exterior kept the room cool before sunrise and released the previous afternoon's heat after the temperature outside no longer justified it. Beneath the window, a brass radiator supplied heat according to a schedule last revised before the Boundary Field possessed a public name.

At 05:58, it clicked six times and became warm.

No station designer would have permitted a thermal system with mood.

Aaron loaded the first simulation.

Amara's team had taken the one hundred thirteen milliseconds between the source's response and the Conduit's automatic gain reduction and expanded the interval into a derived exercise. Soren's seven interaction bands entered from the left. Aaron's calibration carrier formed the central boundary. The source sequence arrived from the right as a set of constraints rather than an energy transfer: relationships among permitted systems, prohibited exchanges, and implied structures the local model could detect but not include.

In the actual session, Soren's architecture had tried to enclose those implications inside a larger context. Instability followed in the leftmost band, spread across two adjacent bands, and armed the severance timer.

In the simulation, a third structure opened between Aaron's carrier and Soren's translation layer.

It did not absorb the sequence. That language implied matter or energy going somewhere. Instead, the modeled Storage architecture held the difference between two permitted states: the full set of source relationships on one side, the reduced set presented to Soren on the other. The Conduit supplied a resonant lattice with known charge, thermal capacity, and saturation limits. The practitioner maintained the state separation. Aaron's Coupling architecture defined which systems could exchange structural information across it.

Nothing came from nothing. The lattice paid the electrical and thermal cost. The Storage practitioner paid in attention, metabolic load, and whatever happened when a human nervous system spent four hundred milliseconds holding a boundary condition derived from something older than its species.

The simulation resolved Soren's instability in eighty-one percent of runs.

Aaron opened the failure set.

In nine percent, the storage state saturated before the secondary translation stabilized. In six, the held structure leaked back through Aaron's carrier and increased his coupling load beyond the validated band. In three, the Conduit mistook the storage envelope for an extension of Soren's architecture and granted him access to relationships the protocol intended to withhold.

The remaining one percent terminated because the model reached a condition its designers had not defined.

The summary called those runs *non-convergent*.

Aaron called them the reason to read summaries last.

He replayed all of them.

Then he replaced the modeled practitioner with Amara's certified baseline.

The proposal left the participant field blank, but the Storage geometry carried her calibration habits. Another Five might define a held transfer by duration or reservoir limit. Amara defined it by release certainty: every stored state in the model included two independent paths to a known local condition and a third path that destroyed the useful pattern while preserving its physical accounting. Aaron had watched her build experiments that way on Helix Perimeter. She designed the exit before deciding whether the entrance justified one.

Her most recent certification exercise involved a loaded resonant composite under asymmetric strain. The test lattice held thirty-eight kilojoules of elastic energy distributed across two sections that could not release simultaneously without breaking the sample. Amara's architecture had captured the first section's permitted transition for 1.6 seconds—not its energy, which remained in the strained material, but its access to the lower-energy state—while the second section discharged into the mechanical sink. Then she released the first transition. The sample returned to rest. The lattice temperature rose by 0.7 degrees from control work and ordinary loss.

The operation resembled the proposal only from a sufficient distance.

In the certification exercise, every state had a human definition. Strain, release, sink, fracture. Amara knew what she held, the Conduit knew what it translated, and the material lacked an opinion about how the systems related. The source replay offered ordered constraints with no complete local representation. A Storage practitioner could not hold an unknown state by naming it. She needed the interface to specify its boundaries without pretending to specify its contents.

Aaron opened the translation stack.

Current Conduits generated sensory output by comparing live practitioner telemetry against an authorized operation model. The interface never put a raw field state into a human mind. It mapped divergence into pressure, distance, tone, balance, temperature, or whichever internal vocabulary produced the lowest attention cost for that operator. When Soren reported increased distance between his hands, the room dimensions had not changed. His Conduit had translated boundary extent into the sensory relationship his nervous system resolved most reliably.

Storage mediation required the inverse problem.

The interface would receive a source-derived differential that Amara could not interpret, represent its limits in her private field vocabulary, and let her hold the defined difference without converting the contents into sensation. Too much translation invited her nervous system to organize the pattern. Too little left no stable boundary around the operation. The useful point lay somewhere between comprehension and contact, a phrase no medical authorization board would accept without numbers.

Aaron added four required measurements.

First: translation fidelity at the storage boundary, measured independently from Amara's subjective report. Second: whether the stored differential preserved practitioner identity markers from Aaron's carrier. Third: whether release returned every participating system to its pre-operation set of permitted exchanges. Fourth: whether any part of the pattern remained in Amara's baseline after the lattice cleared.

The proposal already required a clean post-operation scan. It did not define *clean* at the resolution the source demanded. Ordinary Conduit protocols tolerated transient sensory echoes, limiter afterimages, and low-order lattice correlation for up to ninety seconds. None indicated injury. None mattered when the operation involved characterized human systems.

Aaron changed the threshold to instrument zero.

The model rejected it because instrument zero did not exist. Thermal noise, attention variation, and the minor field changes associated with breathing guaranteed a residual.

He replaced it with the facility's minimum distinguishable correlation across three independent baselines and required the measurement for twenty-four hours.

The model accepted the language. Models accepted any language that left them something to calculate.

He checked the authorship record. Amara had opened the document eleven hours after Soren's session and created the first geometry at 03:14 the next morning. She had added the participant field during revision six, left it blank, and never entered a candidate search.

Aaron did not need to infer whom she intended to propose.

He needed to determine whether knowing changed the model.

At 07:18, Amara sent a message.

**You opened it.**

Aaron checked the document telemetry. The package reported his active review state to its owner. This did not qualify as inference.

**The undefined termination condition occurs in thirty-one runs,** he replied.

Her answer arrived eight seconds later.

**Good morning. Yes. Materials Lab Three at 08:00.**

**That room is not approved for live Conduit work.**

**Good. This is a proposal review.**

Aaron returned to the failure set.

He did not write *caution* into the notes.

The word remained where he had put it four days earlier: in no report, attached to no trace, available only in the private translation his coupling architecture had supplied when the Node reduced itself around Soren. Amara knew the modulation came from the source. She knew it selected the secondary layer. She knew it preceded their controls. She did not know Aaron had understood the sequence as something adjacent to care.

That omission did not alter the equations in her proposal.

It altered the person reading them.

Aaron marked the fourth assumption for discussion and continued.

---

Materials Lab Three had once stored mineral samples.

The room occupied the administrative building's lower level, where exterior stone gave way to a foundation wall nearly a meter thick. Two sealed cabinets still carried faded inventory codes beneath their classification locks. The original workbench ran along the north wall, its surface darkened by eighty years of solvents and equipment feet. Amara's research group had placed three current-generation field displays above it. Their mounting rails followed the bench rather than gravity, producing a visible rise of seven millimeters from one end to the other.

Aaron noticed because the model horizon remained level.

Eleven people occupied the room. Amara stood at the bench with her tablet connected to the central display. Jo Varga sat nearest the door, representing Conduit operations and the medical cutoff team. Two field-modeling specialists, a neural translation analyst, a storage-lattice engineer, and five members of Amara's expanded research group filled the remaining chairs. Nineteen people reported to her. She had selected the ones whose work could proceed inside Soren's authorized compartment.

Nobody except Amara and Aaron knew what the external structure actually represented.

Scientific collaboration under classification required every person to understand their portion completely and the whole incorrectly.

Amara closed the door at 08:00. "Dr. Mercer has reviewed the failure set."

The storage-lattice engineer glanced toward Aaron. "All of it?"

"Thirty-one undefined terminations," Aaron said. "Nine storage saturations that the summary assigns to limiter timing but the raw trace assigns to envelope collapse. The labels require correction."

The engineer opened his notes.

Amara nodded once. "Then we can start there."

No ceremonial introduction. No defense of the proposal before criticism. She put the first failed run on the display and asked the room what the model claimed had happened.

For the next forty-seven minutes, the group dismantled it.

The initial geometry treated storage capacity as a volume. That approximation worked for engineered lattices, where the available resonant state scaled predictably with material, charge distribution, and cooling. Human Storage architecture did not. A Five held transfers by maintaining a stable distinction between what a system currently expressed and what it remained permitted to express later. Capacity depended on complexity, duration, the practitioner's attention state, and whether the held transfer matched any pattern the nervous system already knew how to represent.

A new pattern cost more.

A pattern with no human category cost an unknown amount.

The translation analyst separated the source replay into constraint families. The lattice engineer assigned each family an estimated load. Varga added medical termination bands at half the proposed thresholds and refused to move them when the model lost performance.

"At those limits," one of Amara's researchers said, "the buffer closes before the secondary layer stabilizes in forty-three percent of runs."

"Then the operation fails safely in forty-three percent of runs," Varga said.

"It may never demonstrate benefit."

"Lack of benefit is among the results available to research."

Aaron watched Amara suppress a smile.

The second geometry removed the human practitioner from direct alignment with the source-derived sequence. It placed an engineered storage lattice between Aaron's carrier and the proposed Five, allowing the lattice to accept the defined physical load while the practitioner maintained only its release boundary.

The model failed immediately.

The Node's sequence contained no transferred energy for the lattice to receive. Its information existed as ordered changes in which local systems could count as related. An engineered lattice could hold charge, strain, thermal displacement, or a field configuration impressed upon it. It could not decide that two incompatible descriptions referred to the same temporary system.

Aaron could make that decision through Coupling. A Storage practitioner could hold the resulting state after he made it. The lattice could pay the measurable cost. None could substitute for the others.

Amara froze the model at the moment the storage envelope opened. "The interface currently translates in one direction. Practitioner state enters the Conduit, the Conduit maps it against the authorized geometry, and the resulting sensory model returns to the practitioner. For mediation, we need two translations with a stable interval between them."

She drew the sequence on the display.

"Aaron establishes contact and defines the full observation boundary. The Five receives a bounded representation of the relationship differential—not the external systems, not the primary carrier, only the portion excluded from the secondary layer. Storage holds that differential. The Conduit renders the reduced layer to the Seven. On closure, the secondary layer resolves first, the Storage practitioner releases into the local lattice, and Aaron closes the source boundary last."

One of the modelers enlarged the middle interval. "Maximum duration?"

"Unknown," Amara said.

"The proposal says four hundred milliseconds."

"The proposal needed a value before we could test whether the model objected to it."

"Did it?" Aaron asked.

"Repeatedly."

The modeler reduced the interval to one hundred milliseconds. Success fell below thirty percent. At two hundred, storage load crossed Varga's revised medical threshold in twelve percent. At three hundred, the source replay developed a feedback path through Aaron's carrier unless the geometry kept the storage envelope topologically separate from both practitioners.

"It can't remain separate," Aaron said.

The room quieted.

Amara turned from the display. "Show us."

Aaron stood and moved to the bench. He selected the common-boundary layer and removed the color coding. Three structures remained: primary practitioner, Storage practitioner, secondary practitioner. The proposal depicted Aaron between the other two, each joined to him through a separate permitted exchange.

He collapsed the model to the instant the Storage practitioner accepted the differential.

"Before transfer, these boundaries can remain independent. During transfer, I have to join the source-derived state, the Conduit lattice, and the Storage envelope. She has to define the same held state from inside her architecture. For that interval, the system contains two active practitioner signatures participating in one boundary."

The modeler said, "A joint signature."

"An unresolved one," Aaron said. "No validated Conduit protocol supports it."

"Paired clinical operations do."

"Paired operations synchronize practitioners against one known geometry. They do not let both architectures define different functions inside the same exchange."

Amara studied the simplified model. "So the diagnostic cannot start with Soren."

"No diagnostic involving a secondary practitioner can tell us whether instability originated in the buffer or the secondary layer. The variables remain coupled."

The word produced no amusement in the room. Aaron had not intended any.

Varga leaned forward. "Then what does a valid diagnostic require?"

Aaron restored the source replay, removed Soren's layer, and left two practitioner signatures inside the boundary.

"Primary contact. Storage accepts one defined differential for no more than the shortest interval that produces measurable telemetry. No secondary translation. No attempt to reduce load for anyone else. We establish whether the two architectures can share an operational boundary, whether the Storage practitioner returns fully to baseline, and whether the source-side state changes in response."

The last condition brought Amara's attention to him.

"You expect it to change," she said.

"It changed when Soren's signature entered the observation geometry."

"That's evidence that it can, not an answer about what you expect."

Four days earlier, she had asked whether the modulation expressed intent. Soren had asked whether the source noticed him. Aaron had given each the defensible portion and kept the translation.

Now eleven people waited while he selected another true sentence.

"A new practitioner signature constitutes a new input," he said. "Treating the source as invariant would make the protocol unsafe."

Amara held his gaze long enough to identify the edge of the answer. Then she returned to the display.

The omission required less than a second.

The cost continued after it.

---

By 10:36, the proposal had become twenty-one pages longer and substantially less encouraging.

The revised model required three stages before any buffered session with Soren. First, a synthetic shared-boundary exercise using two fully characterized local systems and no source-derived geometry. Second, a passive carrier exercise with Aaron and a Storage practitioner in separate Conduit rooms, no active aperture. Third, if both baselines remained clean, a source diagnostic limited to one bounded differential and an initial hold target of eighty milliseconds.

Every stage required independent medical stop authority, a cold-state lattice reset, and twenty-four hours of post-operation telemetry. Any residual pattern in either practitioner or the interface canceled progression.

The synthetic exercise used two known field processes that resisted direct combination for opposite reasons. One redirected a calibrated mechanical impulse through a suspended mass. The other stored charge in a resonant ceramic lattice whose safe state excluded motion above a narrow threshold. Aaron would establish a temporary shared boundary permitting the impulse to resolve through the mass without entering the ceramic as vibration. The Storage practitioner would hold the ceramic's access to that vibrational state while the impulse crossed, then release it after the mass returned below threshold.

The experiment had no practical purpose. A physical divider could protect the ceramic more cheaply and without a medical team.

Its uselessness improved it. Nobody could mistake a favorable result for permission to deploy the technique before they understood it.

Stage two replaced the source with Aaron's passive calibration carrier. The carrier contained his practitioner signature but no open aperture and no external field-state sequence. If Amara's baseline changed during that exercise, they could attribute the change to joint practitioner geometry without involving the Node. If it remained clean, they would have learned only that the simplest version had not failed.

Aaron added that sentence to the protocol.

Amara read it over his shoulder. "You expect someone to quote the clean result without the limitation."

"I expect someone to place it in an executive summary."

"More specific."

"More probable."

The protocol also required a Storage practitioner.

The participant field remained blank.

Amara dismissed the group after assigning the revised models. Varga left last, taking the medical threshold table with her and informing Aaron that "shortest measurable interval" would become a number before she authorized a chair.

"That is why I used the phrase," he said.

"I know." She closed the door. "It is why I object to the phrase."

Then only Aaron and Amara remained in the old room.

Morning had reached the narrow windows near the ceiling. Their glass sat partly below courtyard level, so the light arrived through the lower branches of shrubs planted against the building. Moving leaves projected soft distortions across the foundation wall. The current field displays threw harder rectangles over them.

Amara disconnected her tablet. "You haven't rejected it."

"The model has not yet earned rejection."

"From you, that approaches enthusiasm."

"It approaches continued modeling."

She set the tablet on the scarred bench. "Could it work?"

Aaron returned to the shared-boundary frame. His architecture, Amara's, the Conduit lattice, the source-derived differential. Standard Eight could join incompatible processes. Standard Five could hold a transfer in stabilized form. The proposal did not ask either capability to become something else. It asked them to perform different parts of one operation without confusing participation for equivalence.

The physics permitted the question.

Physics did not owe them a favorable answer.

"Yes," he said.

Amara nodded, accepting the exact size of the statement. "Then enter me in the participant field."

Aaron did not move.

She touched the blank line on the display. "Standard Five. Current baseline clean. Forty-two months of continuous field telemetry in the Geneva system. Source compartment authorized. Program Scientific Lead."

"The last item creates a conflict."

"The last item creates paperwork."

"You designed the protocol."

"My team designed the protocol. You just removed most of its attractive properties."

"You control the research authorization path."

"Rhys controls live-source authorization. Medical controls practitioner clearance. You control the contact boundary. I control whether I volunteer."

Aaron's threat-response indexing rose with no source aperture open and no instrument present to give the increase a useful number.

The proposal required a first human test. A Storage practitioner would occupy a Conduit chair inside an unvalidated joint geometry and accept a field-state differential no human architecture had held before. Amara understood each clause. She also knew the clinical record attached to trying the wrong operation near the Node.

Yuki remained alive in a facility whose forms used present tense and whose physicians no longer used recovery projections.

"We can recruit a Five with higher measured capacity," Aaron said.

"Without telling them what the source is?"

"With the authorized risk description."

"You mean accurately and incompletely."

He heard no accusation in the phrase. That made it more accurate.

"Yes."

Amara folded her arms. "Then no."

"Capacity matters."

"So does comprehension. I know what the external structure is. I know what happened to Yuki. I watched all five of your Helix sessions and the one four days ago. I can distinguish my Storage response from a limiter artifact without waiting for a control room to tell me which trace belongs to me."

"Proximity to prior failures does not reduce risk."

"It reduces ignorance."

The sentence remained between them.

Two years earlier, Aaron had learned that Amara recognized the address pattern before he reported it and chose not to tell him. She had made the decision from care, caution, uncertainty, and an authority relationship that let her decide what information he needed about his own exposure. The motives had not canceled the choice. Understanding why she made it had not returned the missing days in which he might have known.

Now she had brought him the models before the protocol existed. She had included the failures, labeled the assumptions, and left the participant line blank until the risk acquired a shape precise enough to choose.

This did not erase the first decision.

It gave the next one a different structure.

"Why you?" Aaron asked.

Amara considered him with the specific patience she reserved for questions whose answer the speaker had arranged not to know.

"Because the program is going to ask someone to hold this," she said. "Before I approve asking anyone else, I need to know what the request means from inside it."

"That is not a capacity argument."

"No. It's a responsibility argument."

"Those perform poorly in medical review."

"Then you can add my capacity data."

Aaron turned to the display. Her baseline file sat one compartment above the meeting model. He had reviewed it annually as part of senior program clearance. Strong Five architecture. High retention stability. Low involuntary-release rate. No dissociation history. No persistent lattice imprint after clinical or research operations. She qualified more cleanly than any external practitioner they could approach without expanding the Contact Zero compartment.

He could reject her because the proposal carried too much unknown risk.

The same rejection would end the buffer research. No other fully informed Five existed inside the program. Recruiting one meant giving a person an authorized description designed by people who had spent years making incompleteness sound like context.

Aaron knew how to do that. The ease with which he knew argued against using the skill.

Amara waited.

He still had not told her *caution*.

The private translation pressed against the decision. If the Node had reduced load for Soren deliberately, then presenting Amara's signature might invite a similar adjustment. Or a different one. Or none. Care observed once did not constitute a safety system. Telling her the word might offer reassurance the evidence could not support. Withholding it kept her consent technically complete about measurable risk while excluding Aaron's most consequential interpretation of the source they proposed to contact.

He had become practiced at finding the boundary where disclosure turned into judgment.

Practice did not tell him where honesty belonged.

"I think the source distinguished Soren from me," he said.

Amara's expression did not change, but her attention narrowed. "The report says the modulation isolated his layer."

"The report describes the instrument result. My interpretation is that the distinction concerned the practitioner, not only the geometry."

"Intent?"

"Possibly."

"Protective?"

There it stood: the word adjacent to the one he had withheld, offered by her instead of him.

Aaron checked the evidence again because evidence provided the only acceptable place to put his resistance.

"The response reduced danger to Soren," he said. "I don't know whether protection describes its purpose."

Amara absorbed that without relaxing. "Does it change your risk estimate?"

"It increases uncertainty. The source may respond to your architecture in ways our replay cannot model."

"Then that belongs in the consent record."

"Yes."

"Add it."

He did.

Not *caution*. Not *care*. The complete interpretation he could defend: source discrimination among practitioner signatures; adaptive response potentially directed toward a practitioner's capacity; result and intention unresolved.

The act did not remove the cost of the four days before it. It prevented the cost from extending into the chair.

Aaron entered **SEN, AMARA — STANDARD FIVE** in the participant field.

He did not authorize a source session. He authorized protocol development, synthetic validation, and independent review. Live contact remained contingent on results they did not yet possess and signatures neither of them could supply alone.

Amara read the authorization language. "This permits us to discover that the proposal doesn't work."

"That is what research authorization means."

"Kessler's office uses a more aspirational definition."

"Kessler's office does not control my boundary."

"Noted."

She signed the volunteer declaration.

The display recorded her name, classification, baseline reference, and the exact unknowns she had accepted. Beneath them, the shared-boundary diagram still showed two practitioner signatures inside one unresolved geometry. No session number. No predicted outcome. No language that could convert willingness into safety merely because both fit in adjacent fields.

Outside the high window, someone crossed the courtyard. For three steps only their shoes passed through the visible strip at ground level, dark against wet stone, then disappeared toward the Conduit wing.

The building held the old room around the new proposal without needing to understand either.

Aaron saved the protocol.

For the first time, the program had a defensible way to ask whether someone else could carry part of the contact load.

It also had the name of the first person who would try.
