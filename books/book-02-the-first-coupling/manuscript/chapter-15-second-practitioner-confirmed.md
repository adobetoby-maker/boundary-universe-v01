# Chapter Fifteen: SECOND PRACTITIONER, CONFIRMED

## Day 2,359

The aggregate monitor arrived in Geneva as three locked equipment cases and a disagreement about cable.

The cases contained phase-preserving acquisition hardware, a timing reference disciplined to the lunar network, and enough optical isolation to prevent the monitor from becoming part of any field relationship it measured. The disagreement occupied the west service corridor. Facilities wanted the new fiber routed above the suspended ceiling. Source security wanted it inside the original stone wall. The building had acquired the wall in 1898 and the ceiling in 1976. Neither system recognized the other's claim to permanence.

Aaron stood beneath an open ceiling panel while a facilities engineer explained that drilling the wall required heritage approval.

"How long?" Aaron asked.

"Six weeks if the review board accepts the existing survey. Twelve if they request a new one."

"The active-session room is twenty-three meters away."

"The building remains historically significant at twenty-three meters."

The statement contained no technical defect.

They routed the fiber through the ceiling.

By 10:14, the monitor could reconstruct every characterized Conduit operation inside Geneva's local envelope at forty-megahertz resolution, distinguish measured phase from inferred phase, and issue a hard closure request through a path independent of the session controller. The lunar timing reference reached the basement through an antenna on the roof, two security transitions, and a conduit shaft whose original drawings described it as a dumbwaiter. Permanent buildings did not eliminate improvised systems. They gave the improvisations addresses.

Aaron ran three synthetic exchanges through the empty lattice. Each test shifted a bounded mechanical impulse from the drive mass into the calibrated reservoir, held the relationship for the authorized interval, then returned the unresolved fraction as heat into the local sink. The energy ledger closed. The phase record survived reduction. The monitor detected a deliberately injected unclassified relationship on the second sampling interval and sent its closure command before the local controller reached its own threshold.

He signed the commissioning result at 11:03.

Soren's fifth buffered session could proceed.

The operations room occupied the building's lowest secure level, below the courtyard and beside foundations thick enough to make wireless communication a theoretical service. Its walls carried newer damping panels over old poured concrete. Coolant lines crossed the ceiling in labeled pairs. A floor channel returned waste heat from the Conduit lattice to a water loop that also served the archive climate system, an arrangement whose efficiency depended on nobody asking why a classified first-contact interface and nineteenth-century paper records shared a pump.

Soren sat inside the practitioner frame. Eight contact pads rested along his neck, upper spine, and forearms. The Conduit did not amplify his field or supply an otherwise absent capacity. It translated the prospective relationships his Standard Seven architecture formed, compared them against the authorized geometry, limited which systems could exchange momentum, charge, or heat, and forced every permitted exchange through an accountable return path.

Amara occupied the buffer frame two meters to his left. Her Standard Five architecture enclosed the relational differential between Soren's prospective nesting and the source-derived state. She would hold that differential without converting it, then release it along the path the Conduit logged.

The method did not make Soren an Eight.

It made his being a Seven survivable at a boundary designed for something else.

"Local aggregate coverage confirmed," Aaron said. "Independent closure path confirmed. Session Five, authorized depth two, interval fifty-two milliseconds. All stop authorities identify."

Varga answered from medical. Amara answered from the buffer frame. The independent technical officer answered from the rear console. Soren answered last.

Six weeks earlier, he would have added something to demonstrate ease. Now he gave his identifier, authority scope, and release phrase without decoration.

Aaron started the lattice.

The Conduit resolved its empty geometry first: reservoir, practitioner boundary, buffer envelope, source-derived carrier, local return. No energy appeared from the system. A drive mass below the floor supplied the initiating impulse. The lattice changed which defined systems could receive it and for how long. Loss entered the cooling loop. Every other description belonged either to shorthand or funding.

At twelve milliseconds, Soren's seventh interaction band formed a prospective relation around the carrier.

Amara's envelope accepted the differential.

The source reduced two paths before the limiter classified either as unstable. Aaron watched the ordered attenuation cross the raw trace. The private translation remained care. The operational record received source-origin adaptive attenuation, which stated what the instrument established and preserved less comfort than the alternative.

Soren held depth two.

Twenty-nine milliseconds. Thirty-seven. Forty-four.

His prospective nesting index rose to 0.031, flattened, then declined while Amara's Storage load increased by the corresponding relational measure. No unlicensed system entered the boundary. The aggregate monitor drew one continuous Geneva-local sequence and no relation beyond it.

At forty-nine milliseconds, Soren said, "Release transition B is late by two."

The delay remained inside tolerance. He reported it before the controller flagged it.

"Continue to scheduled closure," Aaron said.

The Conduit closed at fifty-two.

Amara released the held differential through the destructive-randomization path. Soren's seventh band returned to baseline. The drive reservoir recovered seventy-eight percent of its initiating impulse; the remaining twenty-two resolved as measured heat, controller work, and acoustic loss. The accounting closed within 0.6 percent.

Soren kept both hands open on the frame until the medical status changed from amber to white.

"Baseline," Varga said.

"Buffer clear," Amara said.

The aggregate monitor finished its first live record and classified the operation as one bounded relationship with complete local closure.

Aaron examined the raw phase trace before he let the status word enter his own conclusion.

Stable.

Five sessions did not certify a practitioner. They did, however, make five.

---

The next three operations occupied twenty-four days.

Session Six repeated depth two after a cold lattice reset. The technical team depowered the Conduit, discharged every resonant element into the thermal sink, destructively randomized the source-derived carrier geometry, and rebuilt the authorized state from signed specification. A warm reset preserved too much of the previous phase relation to establish that Soren's stability belonged to the protocol rather than residual alignment. The cold procedure took nine hours and produced no useful result except the removal of an explanation.

Aaron had developed an institutional appreciation for negative achievements.

Soren's normalized instability reached 0.028 and returned to baseline in eleven seconds. Amara released completely under every declared measure. Her acquired four-state topology persisted beneath active-load threshold, unchanged within measurement variance at six and twenty-four hours. The operation added no fifth relation and no amplitude growth.

Session Seven moved to depth three, the upper edge of the characterized authorization. Soren requested a pause during fitting when the derived carrier presented a phase offset 0.7 degrees larger than the signed pre-run model.

The offset fell inside hardware tolerance. The model did not contain it.

Six people waited through a forty-minute calibration review while procurement's schedule system continued counting the room as active capacity. The review traced the offset to a temperature-compensation table loaded during the aggregate-monitor installation. The table corrected a real sensor drift but had not entered Soren's signed packet.

Aaron canceled the operation and rescheduled it for the following morning.

Nobody praised Soren for stopping. Praise would have turned compliance into an exceptional act when it needed to become ordinary procedure. Aaron entered the discrepancy, corrected the packet, and asked him to authenticate the new model.

"Yesterday you would have called that conservative," Soren said.

"Yesterday I had not found the table."

"And if you hadn't found it?"

"The session would still have stopped. We do not reward instruments for being right after ignoring them."

Soren read the corrected phase terms. "People aren't instruments."

"It would simplify the staffing model."

The next morning, depth three held for sixty-four milliseconds. Soren reported uncertainty at the twenty-one-millisecond transition, waited for confirmation, and did not reach toward the source's reduced path. His instability peaked at 0.033, below Session One's 0.041 and below Aaron's early external-array mean of 0.052. Comparing the numbers required two interface qualifications, one age correction, and a note explaining that Aaron's early sessions lacked a Storage buffer, a Conduit limiter, and most modern concepts of professional self-preservation.

Session Eight began on Day 2,383 after the second cold reset.

By then, the session protocol occupied eighty-two pages. The active interval required sixty-four milliseconds. Preparation required eleven hours across two days. Recovery monitoring would continue for seventy-two hours, followed by weekly low-load baselines for Amara. The ratio offended administrators and protected practitioners.

The final run used depth one, the lowest authorized contact, because certification required stability across three depths rather than a dramatic conclusion at the highest one. Soren entered the boundary, held the relationship for thirty-six milliseconds, reported every deviation, and released on the first command.

Nothing went wrong.

The absence acquired six hundred gigabytes of phase data.

Aaron reviewed all of it.

Before the operational debrief, Soren completed his practitioner report alone in Interview Four.

The room had once served as a document examination office. A north-facing window admitted daylight through two layers of security glass, and a fixed magnifier still projected from the desk on a brass arm. The current terminal sat beneath it. Every practitioner used the room immediately after a session because Requirement 2.4 placed the unprompted report ahead of discussion, schedule interpretation, or exposure to anyone else's conclusions.

Aaron watched the submission status from the corridor and did not enter.

At 10:52, the status changed to sealed. Soren opened the door.

"I reported a clean release," he said. "Two timing deviations inside tolerance. No unrequested relation. The source reduced the same class of path as Sessions Three, Five, and Seven."

"That matches the technical record."

"I know. You made the raw trace visible."

"Visibility and interpretation remain different operations."

"Then interpret one."

Soren led him into the interview room and called up his report. He had marked the source attenuation at nineteen milliseconds. The sequence removed an available relation between Soren's prospective seventh-band enclosure and the carrier, then restored an equivalent lower-load path after his architecture settled. The limiter did not initiate either transition. Amara's buffer accepted less load because the higher-risk relation no longer existed to be held.

Soren enlarged the interval.

"It does this before I need it," he said.

Aaron read the statement for what it asked beneath what it claimed.

The Node had distinguished Soren on his first supervised contact. It had reduced interaction load around him during the unauthorized deepening. Across eight buffered operations, its attenuation had preceded his unstable response and the limiter's intervention. Aaron's private model assigned deliberate restraint with confidence the formal record could not support. The word *care* remained less precise and more accurate.

He had withheld it when Soren first asked. At the time, the withholding protected a fragile interpretation and prevented hunger from turning the possibility of recognition into an invitation.

Now Soren had supplied eight sessions of evidence and one canceled operation. He no longer treated every limit as a challenge. The original reason had narrowed.

The restriction had not.

Telling him would reveal more than source behavior. It would expose Aaron's confidence that the source made practitioner-specific judgments, part of the interpretive structure still withheld from Rhys and Kessler. Soren's certification did not grant access to that partition. Trusting his conduct could not create authority Aaron had denied his own director.

The practiced answer came easily.

That ease registered as the cost.

"The attenuation precedes your highest-risk prospective relations," Aaron said. "It changes in response to your field state and not only to the Conduit's command sequence. We can establish adaptive source behavior. We cannot establish intent from the trace."

Every sentence held.

Soren kept his attention on the interval. "But you have an interpretation."

"I have several."

"That was not my question."

"No."

Silence extended under the ventilation's low draft. Aaron could have added that the missing answer did not imply distrust. That statement would have directed Soren toward the existence of a classified conclusion more efficiently than a refusal.

"Will the board see this?" Soren asked.

"The raw sequence, the independent reconstruction, and the stated limit on causal assignment."

"Not your interpretation."

"No."

Soren closed the enlargement. Disappointment crossed his face without acquiring grievance. "Then my certification depends partly on something whose reasons I am not cleared to know."

"Yes."

"Does that trouble you?"

"It defines most of my employment."

"That also wasn't the question."

The room's old magnifier divided the display light into a pale circle across the desk. Someone had preserved the instrument because removing it would damage the wood. The building kept obsolete tools visible while the program hid active ones in walls.

"Yes," Aaron said.

Soren nodded. He did not ask again.

The correct boundary remained intact. Aaron experienced no satisfaction from preserving it. Three years of practice had improved the method and removed the excuse of clumsiness. He could now withhold a conclusion cleanly from someone who had earned his respect, state the boundary without evasion, and identify the exact institutional reason.

Competence did not reduce the load. It made the load attributable.

Amara waited in the operations debrief with her buffer record open. Varga had already cleared Soren's immediate medical baseline and begun the seventy-two-hour observation clock. The aggregate monitor displayed complete local closure. No schedule representative occupied the live channel or the room.

Soren presented first. He described the two timing deviations, the attenuation sequence, the absence of unrequested relations, and the clean release. He did not mention Aaron's withheld interpretation. The omission preserved a compartment he did not share rather than pretending the conversation had not occurred.

Amara presented second. Her active Storage load had peaked at sixty-one percent of the characterized limit and returned to zero through the declared path. The acquired four-state topology sat 1.8 percent above the pre-session amplitude, inside variance. No fifth relation, active load, identity correlation, or functional change appeared.

"Do you recommend certification?" the independent technical officer asked Aaron.

"I recommend completing the recovery series and independent reconstruction."

"Assuming both clear."

"Their purpose is to prevent assumption."

The officer accepted the correction with the expression of someone who had expected it and retained enough professional discipline to ask anyway.

Soren removed the contact strip from his forearm. "If they clear, does anything change tomorrow?"

"Your candidate restrictions remain until the board vote."

"After the vote."

"If approved, you receive protocol-specific operating access, an assigned reconstruction queue, continuing supervision, and more forms."

"So the principal benefit is forms."

"The forms are evidence that other benefits have become administratively dangerous."

Amara closed her buffer record. "You also become the second confirmed human practitioner capable of working through a Conduit at the source boundary."

Soren's expression stilled.

Aaron had avoided that construction. It converted a narrow authorization into a historical position, and historical positions exerted pressure on the people inside them. Amara had not called him a second Eight or omitted the buffer. She had stated the scale precisely enough that refusing it would create a different distortion.

"Protocol-specific," Aaron said.

"Yes," Amara answered. "Real things have specifications."

Soren set the used contact strip into the destruction tray. "I'll wait for the reconstruction."

The tray reduced the strip's resonant coating to random thermal motion and printed a disposal confirmation.

It provided one more clean result.

## Day 2,387

At 04:26 on the fourth morning after Session Eight, Aaron completed the eighth reconstruction.

Analysis Two retained the high windows and steel map drawers from its earlier institutional life. Night rain struck the glass above eye level. The room's ventilation supplied a low continuous draft through a grille labeled for a system removed in 1991. On the center display, eight session traces aligned in descending rows.

The first four carried the uncertainty of a new method. The next four carried less visual drama and more evidentiary weight. Two cold resets broke continuity between lattice states. Three authorized depths tested distinct prospective-load ranges. Every operation ended with Soren's seven interaction bands at baseline, Amara's active Storage load at zero, the local energy and thermal account closed, and the aggregate monitor clear of undeclared external relationships.

Aaron reran the instability model without source-origin attenuation.

It failed.

That mattered. The source's behavior contributed to the result. They could not claim that the buffer method alone made Soren safe, could not transfer the outcome to another source geometry, and could not describe his certification as proof that any Seven could use any Conduit through any Five. The surviving claim remained narrower.

Under this source geometry, with this Conduit revision, Amara's characterized Storage envelope, the stated depth and interval limits, independent closure authority, and the full recovery schedule, Soren could sustain and release a practitioner-mediated boundary without unstable recruitment or lasting field deviation.

Eight times.

The claim described a person, not a general technology.

It described a real person doing real work.

Aaron opened the recommendation form.

*Classification remains Standard Seven. No evidence supports independent Coupling capacity or reclassification to Standard Eight.*

He entered that sentence first. Institutions read success more rapidly than limitation. The order needed to survive both habits.

*Recommend confirmation as secondary Conduit practitioner within Protocol SA-5/7, Revision 3. Authorization limited to characterized source geometry, certified Standard Five buffer support, depths one through three, maximum active interval sixty-four milliseconds, independent medical and technical stop authority, and stated recovery schedule. Any change in practitioner pair, source geometry, Conduit revision, depth, interval, or recovery obligation requires separate validation.*

He attached the eight traces, the cold-reset certifications, Amara's recovery series, the independent reconstructions, Soren's pre-debrief reports, and the canceled Session Seven authorization. The cancellation supported the recommendation as strongly as the successful run that followed. A practitioner who could produce a stable trace might possess technical capacity. A practitioner who could stop before producing one might remain alive long enough to use it.

Aaron authenticated the packet.

The terminal asked whether the recommendation represented a provisional or final classification action.

He selected final.

---

The confirmation board met at 13:00 in Archive Conference Two.

Five voting members sat at the long table. Rhys chaired. Varga held medical authority. The independent reconstruction lead joined from Kiruna with 140 milliseconds of secure-channel delay. Amara attended as Program Scientific Lead and recused herself from the buffer-validation vote because the data under review included her own field architecture. Aaron presented the classification recommendation. Soren waited outside under a rule designed to prevent candidates from learning how institutions discussed them before institutions knew what they intended to say.

Kessler did not attend. Her office submitted one question in writing.

*Does confirmation establish a transferable secondary-practitioner class for deployment planning?*

Aaron answered it on the first slide.

No.

The board spent ninety minutes determining the dimensions of that no.

They separated four questions the deployment model had combined. Could Soren reproduce a characterized operation? Yes. Could he do so at a second facility using an equivalent Conduit? Untested. Could another Seven reproduce his result through Amara's buffer? Untested and not implied. Could another Five replace Amara? Neither authorized nor medically justified while her acquired topology remained unexplained.

The distinction reduced the projected program capacity from a new practitioner class to one named practitioner with one available buffer.

It also reduced the single-point failure Aaron represented. Not by half. Capacity arithmetic did not permit that kind of optimism when one operator required a second human system, a narrower depth range, and source behavior no one controlled. Soren could take defined monitoring and translation work Aaron no longer needed to perform personally. He could provide an independent practitioner trace. If Aaron became unavailable, Soren could sustain the characterized contact path long enough to observe, close, and report.

He could not replace Aaron.

He could prevent every source-linked operation from ending when Aaron left the room.

The risk office had asked for redundancy. The evidence supplied resilience instead. Institutions preferred the first term because it fit a percentage. The second described what they had actually built.

Rhys required that distinction in the decision text. Kessler's submitted position accepted it: the controlled-deployment model could count Soren's certified hours only inside the protocol, could assign no transfer multiplier, and could not treat Amara's unscheduled availability as standing capacity. Her office requested quarterly review of the boundary as new evidence arrived.

Aaron supported the request. A limitation that evidence could change needed review. A limitation protected only by neglect eventually became tradition, and tradition possessed unreliable stop authority.

The Kiruna lead confirmed that each source-origin attenuation event preceded limiter action and selectively removed relations most likely to recruit Soren's prospective nesting. Varga confirmed complete clinical and field recovery after every session, with no persistent deviation in Soren. Amara presented her buffer-load records and the limits of every measurement. She described her acquired four-state topology as a monitored baseline finding under separate review, stable through the series and not demonstrated to result from Soren's buffered operations.

She did not minimize it. She did not allow it to absorb evidence it had not earned.

Rhys read the recommendation scope twice.

"Why final?" he asked.

"Because the limitations define the authorization," Aaron said. "They are not unresolved conditions that expire. Soren remains a Standard Seven. His secondary status remains dependent on a qualified Storage buffer and the characterized protocol. More data may support additional authorizations. It will not turn this one into an Eight classification."

"Permanent secondary status," Varga said.

"Permanent unless his measured architecture changes enough to require a new classification. We have no evidence, mechanism, or proposed pathway for that."

The independent lead's reply arrived after the channel delay. "Then secondary does not mean probationary."

"Correct. It describes function."

Rhys turned to the canceled Session Seven record. "Dr. Aldarin stopped for an offset inside hardware tolerance."

"The offset fell outside his authenticated model."

"You found no unsafe state."

"We found an undeclared state and declined to discover its safety with a person inside the interface."

Rhys's expression changed by less than the display resolution would have supported. "Reasonable."

From him, the word carried approximately the emotional emphasis other people assigned to applause.

The votes entered in order: medical, technical, source security, program authority. Amara abstained where required and voted on the operational classification. Rhys entered the fifth vote.

Five approved. No conditions beyond the recommendation. No dissent.

The board record updated.

**SECONDARY CONDUIT PRACTITIONER — CONFIRMED**

Aaron read the line once.

For three years, every success in the program had arrived coupled to a subtraction. Contact established; truth removed. Conduit architecture stabilized; its purpose classified. Source communication clarified; the danger grew clearer with it. Soren had survived his own unauthorized reach, which counted as rescue only because the alternative already occupied a file Aaron could not restore.

This result did not subtract him.

The protocol constrained him. It named his limits in eighty-two pages and several clauses that would irritate schedule staff for years. It required Amara, a Conduit, independent stop authority, and a source willing to reduce the relations most likely to harm him. None of that made the result partial.

Soren could do the work.

He could do it again.

He could stop.

"Bring him in," Rhys said.

---

Soren listened to the decision without moving.

Rhys read the scope in full, including every limitation. Varga read the continuing medical obligations. Amara stated that buffer availability constituted an operational prerequisite, not support to be assumed. Aaron explained that any independent attempt outside the protocol would terminate the classification and likely the person attached to it.

"Understood," Soren said.

Rhys slid the credential across the table.

The card contained no title visible without authentication. Its surface showed Soren's photograph, Directorate mark, and a narrow silver line identifying active technical access. Inside the access system, one field had changed from CANDIDATE to SECONDARY PRACTITIONER.

Soren authenticated it with his field signature. The reader accepted Standard Seven and did not mislabel the result as a lesser version of something else.

"I thought this would feel different," he said.

Aaron's attention sharpened. "Different how?"

"Larger."

"The credential is standard size."

Soren glanced at him, and the delayed smile arrived. "Thank you."

The words offered several possible objects. Aaron selected the one he could answer accurately.

"You did the work."

"You kept letting me."

No. Aaron had stopped him, suspended him, narrowed him to a protocol, and required him to repeat an operation until repetition became evidence rather than appetite. Letting did not describe the process. It might describe the outcome.

"The evidence supported continuation," Aaron said.

"I read your evidence sections."

Amara gathered her copy of the decision. "This may be the closest either of you gets to an emotional exchange. Should the minutes remain open?"

"The board has adjourned," Rhys said.

"Good. Then no one needs to classify it."

They left the conference room together. Soren stopped at the access reader outside the operations corridor and presented the new credential. The indicator turned white. A lock released inside the old wall with a heavy mechanical movement.

Candidate access had required escort beyond that point. Secondary-practitioner access still prohibited him from entering an armed Conduit room alone, changing source geometry, initiating a session, or modifying a limiter. It permitted him to enter the analysis suite without Aaron authorizing the door each time.

A small freedom, accurately bounded.

Soren crossed the threshold, then turned. "Do I have a queue now?"

"Nine reconstruction reviews and a limiter simulation," Aaron said.

"You prepared work before the vote."

"I prepared for an approved outcome. Deleting a queue takes less time than building one."

"And if the vote failed?"

"Your disappointment would have included administrative efficiency."

Soren considered this and went to find his terminal.

Aaron remained by the open door.

The corridor carried the smell of wet stone from the courtyard entrance two levels above and the cooler metallic air of the operations floor. The aggregate monitor ran behind the wall, receiving phase data from a system that now admitted two confirmed practitioners, though only one could couple independent processes and only one would ever occupy the primary classification.

The distinction mattered.

So did the second person.

Aaron let the result occupy his attention without qualifying it. No projection to deployment. No recalculation of the single-point-of-failure model. No estimate of how Kessler's office would use the board record or how Rhys would contain that use. Those questions remained real. For several seconds, he declined to make them current.

Soren had reached past authorization and survived. He had learned where to stop. He had completed eight stable operations. The program had produced a practitioner instead of a case file.

The condition in Aaron's chest did not require technical vocabulary. Technical vocabulary remained available, but applying it would have converted the moment into a report, and the report already existed.

He permitted himself the less precise description.

Good.

Then the door began its automatic closure, having allowed twelve seconds for passage under a facilities standard that predated Conduits by four decades.

Aaron stepped through before it locked him on the wrong side.

The institution had produced one unqualified success.

It still required correct access.
