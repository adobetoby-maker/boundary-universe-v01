## Major improvements by arc

### Chapters 1–5 — voice, family, arrival, belonging pressure
- Chapter 2 gives Elena more emotional and procedural authority rather than using her only to deliver Kade to the plot.
- Zero Protocol exposition is tighter and more adversarial without making Renn/Vale cartoon villains.
- Two subtle Aaron-family clues are now seeded before the explicit second-half reveal.
- Asterion arrival has more sensory scale, ordinary student life, class/status discomfort, and Kade's involuntary desire to stay.
- Kade's last-place baseline is framed as a painful but legitimate measurement of current conventional capability, not institutional cruelty.
- Meridian feels lived-in before it becomes emotionally important.
- Eli, Mara and Sera enter as distinct people with different relationships to Kade rather than a ready-made friend group.

### Chapters 6–10 — earned progression
- The Forty-Seven Second Fight has clearer mechanics and medical consequences.
- Boundary anomalies move existing force; they do not create free energy.
- Headache, tremor, pupil asymmetry and brief temporal disorientation establish a concrete neurological cost.
- The Suppression Order follows directly from the medical event and is plausibly about uncertainty/safety.
- Kade accepts that conditioning, footwork, formal physics and combat fundamentals are genuinely deficient.
- Ladder mechanics are more explicit: challenge bands, recalculation, open challenges and team-event weighting are distinct systems.
- Ordinary school life now appears between progression beats: classes, homework, missed deadlines, calls home, meals and fatigue.
- Mara's role remains technical peer first; romance stays subtextual.
- Vector Class strengthens Consensus foreshadowing through shared-state/coupled-control mathematics.

### Chapters 11–13 — Boundary architecture and rivalry
- Jonah Pike's open challenge remains a legal rank-gap mechanism.
- Kade's decision to fight Pike includes a believable dangerous-curiosity motive without making him intentionally violate Sen's order.
- The broken-floor event has clear pre-existing energy sources and repeat neurological cost.
- Sen's Boundary explanation answers what likely happened while preserving the historical mystery.
- `conventional output` is now an important distinction from `no effect`.
- Sen's consent rule around Eli's model seeds the ethics required for later Consensus.
- Sen's lies are precisely worded enough to become fair later payoffs rather than arbitrary withholding.
- Julian's help is explicitly competitive/self-interested: he wants stronger opposition and wants to understand Kade's improvement curve.
- Julian teaches Kade to remain inside a plan long enough to create second- and third-order options.

### Chapters 14–15 — House Trial / Consensus foreshadowing
- Meridian's eight-person qualifier roster is concrete: Kade, Mara, Eli, Sera, Taren, Priya Shah, Nessa Kim and Anton Bell.
- Plans are built collaboratively and no longer belong solely to Kade.
- Qualifier roles give Eli, Nessa, Anton and Priya observable contributions.
- Kade's elimination is an emotional/thematic success because the team's plan survives him and then improves without him.
- The final makes `coupled scoring` an explicit game mechanic that foreshadows Consensus without duplicating it.
- The Meridian bell finally rings, paying off the House identity introduced in Chapter 5.
- Kade's large jump from #8,159 to #1,904 is explicitly a team-event recalculation driven by previously missing high-quality tactical/team data.

### Chapters 16–17 — false belonging and emotional midpoint
- Fame distinguishes being seen from being known.
- The sponsorship scene makes Kade's public story economically valuable and therefore morally uncomfortable rather than simply annoying.
- Taren receives another subtle access clue through calendar/information availability.
- Aaron is lightly seeded through Elena's memory of his discomfort with cameras and her careful `industrial systems` answer.
- The Northline return gives Kade responsibility for his own prior behavior without accepting institutional revisionism.
- Russell remains flawed but not villainous; Kade accepts `we are proud of you` without allowing `we always predicted this` to become true.
- Ms. Alvarez apologizes for overvaluing fit while also holding Kade accountable for using valid criticisms as permission to disengage.
- Kade deliberately promises to return to Northline rather than using humor to avoid commitment.
- Midpoint thematic state is now explicit: belonging means choosing vulnerability, not eliminating it.
