# Chapter 22 — Midyear: Mercer v. Vey

Kade knew he was in trouble when Mara smiled.

Not a large smile.

That would have been reassuring.

This was the small, precise smile she wore when a problem had finally become interesting.

They met in the quarterfinal arena under lights bright enough to erase the crowd.

Mara entered ranked seventh.

Kade entered with a live tournament projection in the low hundreds.

The announcer spent far too much time on the story.

Zero Boy.

Meridian teammates.

House Trial partners.

Rising anomaly versus established elite.

Kade ignored all of it.

Mara did too.

The signal sounded.

He attacked first.

That was the mistake.

Mara gave him exactly the opening he expected.

He took it.

She was already moving to punish the response she knew would follow.

Kade recovered.

Changed angle.

She changed before he did.

Three exchanges later, he understood.

She was not fighting him.

She was fighting the version of him three decisions from now.

Kade adapted.

Mara adapted to the adaptation.

He felt the arena network at the edge of awareness.

Floor.

Anchors.

Walls.

Transfer paths.

He could reach for it.

He did not.

This was not the place to gamble everyone’s safety because he wanted to win.

Mara saw the hesitation.

Then she struck.

Kade hit the mat.

Point.

He stood.

She did it again.

Not the same technique.

The same principle.

She made him improvise inside a plan she had already built.

Final exchange.

Kade feinted high, rolled under her counter, and almost found the line.

Almost.

Mara trapped his lead arm, redirected his momentum, and put him on his back with such clean control that even Kade laughed.

The buzzer sounded.

**WINNER — VEY**

Mara offered him a hand.

Kade took it.

“You planned all of that?” he asked.

“Most.”

“How far ahead?”

“Far enough.”

“That is deeply irritating.”

“I know.”

They stood too close for half a second.

The crowd noticed before they did.

A wave of noise moved through the arena.

Mara stepped back.

Kade looked toward the stands.

Eli was making a face Kade intended to punish later.

Sera looked delighted by his suffering.

Julian, waiting for his own semifinal, gave Kade a single approving nod.

The recalculation posted that evening.

**KADE MERCER — RANK #211**

Kade stared at it from the Meridian common room.

Mara sat across from him, reviewing her next match.

“You beat me,” he said.

“Yes.”

“I moved up.”

“Yes.”

“This ranking system is absurd.”

“Only when you lose.”

Kade looked at her.

Mara’s eyes stayed on the tablet.

But she was smiling.

Kade smiled too.

Then he remembered his father’s name in a classified archive.

The smile faded.

He had spent months climbing Asterion’s visible ladder.

Somewhere underneath it was another structure entirely.

And he was beginning to suspect his father had once climbed that one.
