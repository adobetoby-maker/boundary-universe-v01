# Chapter 25 — Taren Holt

Taren was the one who knew how to get into places he was not supposed to be.

That should have worried Kade sooner.

The restricted archive entrance sat beneath the old eastern lecture hall, behind a door that required two faculty credentials and one biometric confirmation.

Taren had none of those.

The door still opened.

Eli stared.

“How?”

Taren smiled. “Charm.”

“That was encryption.”

“I’m charming to machines.”

Sera did not laugh.

Kade noticed.

Inside, the archive corridor smelled like cold metal and old air.

Mara carried the search list.

Aaron Mercer.

Continuity Directorate.

Project Consensus.

Asterion precursor site.

They reached the terminal room.

Taren stayed by the door.

Kade logged in using the temporary clearance Sen had reluctantly issued for historical research.

Mara searched.

Eli copied metadata.

Sera watched Taren.

The first useful file appeared under Project Consensus.

Then the lights changed.

Red.

The terminal locked.

The door behind them sealed.

Kade turned.

Taren was standing on the other side of the transparent partition.

His face looked wrong.

Not smug.

Ashamed.

“Taren,” Kade said.

“I’m sorry.”

Sera moved first.

She hit the partition hard enough to make it ring.

“You open this door.”

“I can’t.”

“You just did.”

“That was before.”

Kade stepped closer.

“Before what?”

Taren looked at him.

“Before they knew you were here.”

The answer landed slowly.

Mara said, “Who is they?”

Taren’s jaw tightened.

“The Directorate.”

Silence.

Kade felt something colder than anger.

“How long?”

Taren looked away.

“How long have you been reporting on me?”

“Since before you arrived.”

Eli whispered, “No.”

Taren closed his eyes once.

“I was assigned to observe. That’s all.”

“That’s all?” Kade said.

“I didn’t know what they wanted.”

“You became my friend because someone told you to.”

“At first.”

Kade laughed once.

There was no humor in it.

“At first.”

Taren stepped toward the partition.

“Kade, listen to me. I thought this was intelligence work. Risk assessment. I did not know they were planning anything inside Asterion.”

“Why should I believe you?”

“You shouldn’t.”

That stopped him.

Taren swallowed.

“But you should believe this: get out of that room.”

Eli looked at the sealed door.

“Helpful.”

Taren pressed his palm to the partition.

“I can override it for eight seconds when the next security cycle resets.”

Sera said, “Why help us now?”

Taren looked at her.

“Because this stopped being an assignment a long time ago.”

The red lights pulsed.

A timer appeared over the door.

**SECURITY CYCLE — 00:12**

Taren said, “When it hits zero, run.”

Kade stared at him.

Every roof conversation.

Every joke.

Every warning.

Every piece of campus advice.

All contaminated.

Not necessarily false.

That was worse.

The timer reached zero.

The door unlocked.

Sera went first.

Mara followed.

Eli grabbed Kade’s sleeve.

Kade moved.

As he passed Taren, he stopped for half a second.

Taren said, “I am sorry.”

Kade looked at him.

“I don’t know what that means yet.”

Then he ran.
