# Chapter 14 — House Trial: Qualifiers

House Meridian had never finished higher than ninth in the opening House Trial.

Taren told Kade this like it was a family shame.

“Historically,” he said, pacing in front of the strategy wall, “we are aggressively respectable.”

Eli looked up from his tablet.

“That phrase should be illegal.”

“It is accurate.”

Sera threw a towel at Taren.

“Sit down.”

The House Trial was Asterion’s first major team event of the year: twenty Houses, mixed disciplines, three rounds, and enough public attention that half the campus pretended not to care while knowing every score.

Each team fielded eight students.

Meridian’s projected finish was twelfth.

Their problem was not talent.

It was distribution.

Mara was exceptional.

Taren was excellent.

Sera was solid across multiple roles.

Eli was brilliant with systems and a liability if anyone reached him physically.

Kade was still difficult to categorize.

The remaining three students were competent but unremarkable by Asterion standards.

Taren pointed to the official simulation map.

“First round is relay capture. Three nodes. One mobile objective. Standard scoring favors speed.”

“Then don’t race,” Kade said.

Everyone looked at him.

Taren smiled.

“There he is.”

Kade stood and moved to the map.

“Everybody will send their fastest people straight at the mobile objective.”

“Because it is worth the most points,” Mara said.

“Exactly.”

Kade tapped the two static nodes.

“So these will be under-defended.”

Sera folded her arms.

“You want to give up the big objective.”

“I want everyone else to fight over it while we score somewhere else.”

Eli’s eyes lit.

“Opportunity cost.”

Taren nodded slowly.

Mara studied the map.

“If we take both static nodes early, we force at least one team to peel off.”

“And then?”

Kade smiled.

“Then the mobile objective gets less crowded.”

Mara looked at him.

“You want to make the other teams reorganize around us.”

“Now you’re being flattering.”

“It was analysis.”

“Recovered.”

The qualifiers began at fourteen hundred.

Meridian was placed against House Kepler and House Horizon.

The horn sounded.

Kepler and Horizon did exactly what Kade expected.

Six students sprinted toward the moving objective.

Meridian sent Mara and Sera left, Taren and another student right, Eli to systems control, and Kade nowhere obvious.

His assignment was simple.

See the shape change.

For the first forty seconds, he did almost nothing.

Then Horizon realized Meridian had taken both static nodes.

Two of their students broke away.

Kepler responded.

The center opened.

Kade moved.

“Now,” he said over comms.

Mara abandoned the left node instantly.

No hesitation.

That mattered more than Kade expected.

She trusted the call.

Taren shifted right.

Sera covered the retreat.

Meridian collapsed on the mobile objective from three angles.

For twelve seconds, the other two Houses were reacting to a plan they did not understand.

Meridian captured the objective.

Then gave it up deliberately.

The crowd booed.

Eli laughed over comms.

“They hate us.”

“Good,” Taren said.

Kade called the next rotation.

By the end of the round, Meridian had held the mobile objective for less total time than either opponent.

They had also won by fourteen points.

Taren tackled Kade in the corridor afterward.

Not hard.

Mostly theatrically.

“You beautiful menace.”

“Release me.”

“You have given Meridian hope. This is irresponsible.”

Mara walked past them.

“His third rotation was late.”

Kade pointed after her.

“See? Balance.”

Round two was more difficult.

The event shifted to defensive extraction.

Meridian had to move a weighted rescue capsule through a field while opposing teams could redirect obstacles and attack carriers.

Kade could not use his anomaly intentionally.

Sen had made that explicit.

So he did not.

Instead, he changed assignments every twenty seconds.

The strongest carrier was not always the best carrier.

Sometimes Mara fought.

Sometimes Sera carried.

Sometimes Taren became bait.

Once Eli carried for eight seconds because nobody expected it.

He complained the entire time.

“I am an engineer.”

“You have arms.”

“Debatable.”

They finished second in the heat.

Enough to advance.

The final qualifier was tactical elimination.

Meridian entered projected sixth.

They finished first.

Not because Kade dominated.

He was eliminated halfway through.

That was the part he liked most.

The plan survived him.

From the observation bay, he watched Mara adjust one of his rotations without waiting for permission.

Taren recognized it and changed position.

Sera covered Eli.

Eli disabled a gate at exactly the right moment.

Meridian functioned without Kade at the center.

When the final horn sounded, House Meridian had qualified for the House Trial Final in third place overall.

The common room erupted that night.

Kade stood near the edge of the celebration.

His old instinct returned.

Watch.

Smile.

Leave before anyone notices you care.

Sera appeared beside him.

“You’re doing it again.”

“What?”

“Standing one step outside your own life.”

Kade looked at the room.

Eli was reenacting the rescue capsule carry with wild exaggeration.

Taren was claiming every successful call had been his idea.

Mara was arguing with a second-year about scoring rules.

Kade felt something unfamiliar and dangerous.

Pride.

Not in himself.

In them.

He stepped into the room.

Eli immediately pointed at him.

“There! He can confirm I carried the entire team.”

“You carried a box for eight seconds.”

“Heroically.”

Kade laughed.

House Meridian had not won anything yet.

But for the first time, he understood why people cared about Houses at all.

Rankings told you where you stood.

A House told you who stood with you.
