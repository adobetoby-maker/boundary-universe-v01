# Chapter 15 — House Trial: Final

By noon on final day, nobody in Meridian was pretending the House Trial did not matter.

Even Sera had braided her hair in Meridian’s silver-and-blue colors.

Kade noticed.

She noticed him noticing.

“Say nothing.”

“I was going to compliment your team spirit.”

“You were not.”

He smiled.

The final field was larger than the qualifiers: a three-level arena with moving barriers, capture zones, and a central relay core that could change the scoring geometry mid-match.

Four Houses remained.

Meridian entered seeded third.

Taren stood in front of the team and looked unusually serious.

“No speeches,” he said.

Eli raised a hand.

“That was almost one.”

Taren ignored him.

Mara looked at Kade.

“What’s the plan?”

Kade studied the field.

He had three.

That was Julian’s influence.

One for the opening.

One if the opening failed.

One if everyone did something unreasonable.

He liked the third best.

“First five minutes, we look predictable.”

Sera groaned.

“Your favorite kind of lie.”

“Exactly.”

The horn sounded.

Meridian opened conventionally.

Mara took center.

Sera guarded Eli.

Taren contested the west zone.

Kade moved between lanes as support.

For two minutes, nothing surprising happened.

Then House Atlas committed three students to overwhelm Mara.

Kade saw the rotation.

“Switch.”

Mara moved before he finished the word.

That trust again.

She abandoned center and cut east.

Kade moved west.

Taren dropped low.

Sera released Eli.

The entire formation changed shape in less than three seconds.

Atlas hit empty space.

Meridian took two zones.

The crowd surged.

Eli’s voice came through comms.

“I have the relay core.”

“Can you hold it?” Kade asked.

“That depends on whether anyone touches me.”

“So no.”

“Correct.”

Kade redirected Sera toward him.

The match became chaos.

House Kepler took center.

Atlas attacked east.

Horizon forced a barrier shift that split Meridian’s formation.

Kade lost sight of Mara.

Then saw her on the upper level, fighting two students at once.

She should have retreated.

Instead she looked down through the transparent floor.

At Kade.

One second.

No words.

He understood.

She was buying him time.

Kade moved.

The central relay core was about to change state.

If Eli held it three more seconds, the field would rotate scoring priority from position to coordinated captures.

“Two seconds,” Eli said.

A Kepler student reached him.

Kade was too far away.

Sera was engaged.

Taren was down.

Mara was still above.

Kade saw the whole field at once.

Not force.

Relationships.

Who occupied what space.

Who depended on whom.

Which actions mattered because of everything around them.

The sensation from the broken floor flickered at the edge of his awareness.

He did not reach for it.

Sen’s warning remained clear.

Instead he shouted, “Mara, drop!”

She did.

Without asking why.

Mara stepped off the upper platform.

The crowd gasped.

She fell one level, caught the side rail, swung beneath it, and kicked the Kepler student away from Eli at the exact instant the relay core changed state.

**COUPLED SCORING ACTIVE**

Eli laughed so loudly the comm clipped.

“Now!” Kade yelled.

Meridian moved.

Not eight students waiting for orders.

One team.

Taren recovered and took west.

Sera broke center.

Mara climbed back to the upper platform.

Eli shifted the barriers.

Kade filled whichever gap appeared.

For ninety seconds, Meridian scored faster than every other House combined.

Then Atlas adapted.

Their captain targeted Kade.

Smart.

Kade was knocked out of the match with four minutes remaining.

From the observation bay, he watched the score tighten.

Meridian lost west.

Then center.

Eli was eliminated.

Taren went down.

Thirty seconds remained.

Meridian trailed by two.

Mara and Sera were still active.

Kade gripped the rail.

Mara looked at the score.

Then at the field.

Kade knew that expression.

She had seen something.

Mara abandoned the obvious capture zone.

Sera followed instantly.

They moved to the relay core.

Atlas ignored them.

The core was worth nothing by itself.

Unless two active teammates synchronized contact during coupled scoring.

A rule buried in the technical appendix.

Eli had found it the night before.

Mara touched one side.

Sera hit the other.

The horn sounded.

For half a second, the scoreboard did not change.

Then:

**MERIDIAN +5**

The arena exploded.

House Meridian had won.

Kade did not remember leaving the observation bay.

One moment he was behind glass.

The next he was on the field with Eli hanging off one shoulder and Taren yelling directly into his ear.

Sera actually hugged him.

Briefly.

Then denied it happened.

Mara stood two steps away, breathing hard.

Kade looked at her.

“You dropped off a building because I asked.”

“It was one level.”

“You are impossible.”

“You called the timing correctly.”

“So you trusted me.”

Mara’s eyes held his.

“Yes.”

One word.

No joke arrived.

Kade realized he did not want one.

The ranking recalculation came thirty minutes later.

House Trial performance carried individual weighting.

Kade’s tablet vibrated.

He looked down.

**KADE MERCER**

**RANK: 1,904 / 10,482**

Eli screamed.

Actually screamed.

Taren picked Kade up off the ground.

“Put me down.”

“Absolutely not.”

Kade looked over Taren’s shoulder at the giant arena display.

His name had appeared among the day’s largest movers.

People were chanting Meridian.

Some were chanting Mercer.

Kade felt the old instinct rise.

Leave.

Make a joke.

Make it smaller before it can matter.

Then he saw Mara.

Sera.

Eli.

Taren.

Even Julian, standing across the arena with House Sol, applauding.

Kade let the moment remain large.

For once, he did not protect himself from it.
