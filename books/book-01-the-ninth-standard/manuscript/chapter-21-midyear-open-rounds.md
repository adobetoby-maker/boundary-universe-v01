# Chapter 21 — Midyear: Open Rounds

By midyear, Asterion had learned what to do with Kade Mercer.

Mostly.

Put him in an arena.

Measure everything.

Try not to let him break the floor.

The Midyear Open began with 2,048 first-years and a bracket large enough to make Eli emotional.

“It’s beautiful,” he said.

“It’s a spreadsheet.”

“It’s destiny organized into columns.”

Kade looked at the first opponent.

Rank #1,611.

He won without using boundary architecture.

That mattered more than the rank jump.

Second round: #1,204.

Kade lost the first exchange, changed his footwork, and won on points.

Third: #883.

He nearly triggered the strange whole-system perception, stopped himself, and won by exploiting timing instead.

Sen’s restrictions still allowed him to compete.

They did not allow him to experiment with the dangerous part of his ability without supervision.

For once, Kade understood why.

That did not mean he liked it.

By the fourth round, the stands had started filling specifically for him.

Not because he was favored.

Because he was unpredictable.

Kade preferred being underestimated to being watched.

Asterion had removed that option.

Mara met him between bouts.

“You’re drifting left when tired.”

“I know.”

“You’re still doing it.”

“That’s different.”

She handed him water.

Their fingers brushed.

Neither reacted.

That was technically true.

Eli reacted enough for everyone.

He turned away with exaggerated politeness.

Sera smacked the back of his head.

The next opponent was #503.

Kade lost.

Cleanly.

The older part of him expected humiliation.

Instead he found himself studying the replay before he left the arena.

“Again next semester,” he told the winner.

She smiled.

“Get in line.”

Kade’s final tournament rank settled at #211 after recalculation, challenge bonuses, and open-bracket performance.

He stared at the number.

Ten thousand four hundred eighty-two had become two hundred eleven.

Fast.

Too fast, maybe.

But not magic.

Every step contained losses.

Bruises.

Homework.

Mara correcting his equations.

Julian dismantling his habits.

Sera calling out excuses.

Eli logging everything.

People.

Always people.

That should have made the achievement feel less his.

Instead it felt more real.
