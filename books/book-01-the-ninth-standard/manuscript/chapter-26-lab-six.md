# Chapter 26 — Lab Six

The explosion happened six minutes after they left the archive.

That was long enough for Kade to begin believing they had escaped.

They were crossing the eastern engineering wing when Eli stopped walking.

Not slowed.

Stopped.

Kade took three more steps before noticing.

“What?”

Eli stared at a maintenance panel built into the corridor wall.

A small diagnostic light blinked amber.

Then green.

Then amber again.

“That sequence is wrong,” Eli said.

Sera looked back.

“How wrong?”

“Not broken wrong.”

Eli stepped closer.

“Watched wrong.”

Kade’s skin tightened.

Taren went pale.

Mara asked, “What does that mean?”

Eli opened his tablet and pulled up a network map.

“I’ve been seeing phantom maintenance pings in Lab Six for weeks. I thought it was a legacy monitor. It isn’t.”

Taren said, “Directorate overlay.”

Eli looked at him.

“You knew?”

“Not specifically.”

“Excellent distinction.”

The diagnostic light flashed red.

Eli’s expression changed.

“Move.”

Nobody argued.

They ran.

The first blast was not loud.

It was pressure.

The corridor floor rose under Kade’s feet as if the building had inhaled.

He hit the wall shoulder-first.

Air disappeared from his lungs.

A second later the sound arrived.

Deep.

Metallic.

Wrong.

Alarms screamed through the eastern wing.

Emergency shutters dropped from the ceiling.

Sera rolled beneath the first.

Mara followed.

Kade slid through as the gap narrowed.

Taren came after him.

Eli did not.

Kade turned.

Eli stood on the other side of the descending pressure door, staring at his tablet.

“Eli!”

“The coolant chamber is venting!” Eli shouted.

“Then get through!”

“If it vents into the upper lab, it takes the whole level!”

The gap beneath the door was less than half a meter.

Eli turned and ran the other direction.

Toward Lab Six.

Kade lunged for the door.

Sera caught him around the chest.

“No!”

“He’s going back!”

“I know!”

“He’s going the wrong way!”

“I know!”

The pressure door sealed.

Kade hit it with both palms.

“ELI!”

No answer.

Mara was already at the emergency panel.

“Manual override locked.”

Taren slammed his revoked access badge against the reader.

Nothing.

The wall shook again.

This time fire flashed behind the small reinforced window.

Kade saw only white.

Then smoke.

Then nothing.

He tried to reach for the whole system.

Door.

Wall.

Pressure.

Structure.

The cold line formed behind his eyes.

Sera grabbed his wrist.

“Kade.”

“I can open it.”

“You don’t know that.”

“He’s in there.”

“I know.”

“I can—”

“You are standing beside a damaged thermal lab full of unstable field hardware.”

Her voice cut through him.

“If you guess wrong, you make it worse.”

Kade hated her.

For three seconds.

Because she was right.

Emergency teams arrived in fifty-four seconds.

Kade counted every one.

Responders in sealed armor flooded the corridor. Sera immediately began giving them the information she had: number of people, last known position, lab hazards, the direction Eli had run.

She became someone else under emergency lights.

Not calmer.

More precise.

This was what she wanted to do with her life.

Kade understood that now.

A response officer shoved a portable map toward her.

“Which route?”

Sera pointed without hesitation.

“Service spine C. Do not enter from the west stair. Pressure differential will trap your team.”

The officer looked at her identification.

“You Response Corps?”

“Candidate.”

He nodded once and followed her recommendation.

Kade stood uselessly against the wall.

That was almost unbearable.

Mara stayed beside him.

Not touching.

Close.

Taren disappeared during the confusion.

Kade noticed.

He hated himself for noticing.

He hated Taren for making noticing necessary.

After seventeen minutes, the first rescue team emerged with two injured engineering students.

Neither was Eli.

Thirty-three minutes.

A faculty technician.

Not Eli.

Forty-one.

A responder came out alone.

His helmet was scorched.

Kade knew before the man spoke.

“Lower lab is compromised. Field-stabilized coolant flooded the chamber. Thermal sensors are unreliable.”

“Navarro?” Mara asked.

The responder looked at the list.

“Not located.”

Not located.

Kade held onto those words.

Not dead.

Not located.

The official casualty notice posted two hours later.

Five injured.

Two critical.

One missing, presumed dead.

**ELIAS NAVARRO**

Kade read the name until it stopped looking like language.

No body recovered.

The lab collapse had damaged the thermal chamber and flooded the lower level with coolant designed to remain stable under field stress. The same properties that prevented a larger explosion made normal recovery impossible until the system cooled.

Technical language.

Clean language.

Merciful language.

Presumed dead.

Kade sat on the stone outside Meridian House because going inside meant seeing Room 814.

Mara sat beside him.

Sera paced ten meters away, still answering questions from response supervisors because she had somehow become useful enough that nobody told her to stop.

Julian arrived after evening training had been canceled across half the island.

He stood in front of Kade for a while.

Then sat.

Rank One on the pavement.

No one cared.

Kade stared at the ground.

“If I hadn’t gone to the archive—”

Julian cut him off.

“No.”

Kade looked up.

Julian’s face was tired and angry.

“You do not get to make yourself the center of every bad thing that happens.”

“They targeted us.”

“Probably.”

“Eli was there because of me.”

“Eli was there because Eli made decisions.”

Kade almost snapped at him.

Julian continued before he could.

“You can be responsible for your choices without claiming ownership of everyone else’s.”

The sentence sounded like something Eli would have turned into a spreadsheet column.

That made it worse.

Kade looked away.

Across the courtyard, Sera finally stopped moving.

She put both hands on the back of a chair and bowed her head.

Mara reached over and took Kade’s hand.

Not romantic.

Not careful enough to pretend it was accidental.

Kade let her.

He had spent months learning that needing people made life larger.

Nobody had explained the other side.

Larger things left larger holes.

His tablet buzzed.

A message from Elena.

**I heard. Call when you can. I love you.**

Kade stared at it.

For once, he did not answer with a joke.

He typed:

**I love you too. I can’t talk yet.**

Then he put the tablet down.

Inside Meridian House, someone rang the old bronze bell once.

Not a victory ring.

One low strike.

The sound moved through the courtyard and out toward the ocean.

Kade closed his eyes.

Eli was missing.

Presumed dead.

And Kade had no system large enough to make that fact go somewhere else.
