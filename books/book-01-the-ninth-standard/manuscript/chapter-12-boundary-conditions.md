# Chapter 12 — Boundary Conditions

Sen did not begin with an answer.

She began with a line.

On the wall display, a simple box appeared around a glass of water.

“What is the system?” she asked.

Kade looked at her.

“Is this a trick?”

“Probably.”

“The glass and water.”

Sen drew a second box around the table.

“And now?”

“The table too.”

A third box enclosed the room.

Kade sighed.

“I understand boundaries.”

“No. You understand that they can be chosen.”

She erased the display.

“Most Resonants interact with transfers inside a stable definition. They redirect momentum. Store energy. Partition force. Alter pressure. The system is assumed.”

Kade thought of the hidden exam.

**REDEFINE.**

“And me?”

Sen folded her hands.

“You appear to question the assumption.”

“Appear?”

“You have done it twice at meaningful scale.”

“Three times,” Kade said.

Sen’s eyes sharpened.

He told her about Eli’s tracking model.

The minor events.

Blocks moving strangely. Impacts resolving wrong.

Sen listened without interrupting.

When he finished, she said, “You will give me a copy.”

“Eli built it.”

“You will ask him.”

“He’ll say yes.”

“Then ask anyway.”

Kade noticed the distinction.

Consent.

It felt unexpectedly important.

Sen brought up the arena fracture.

“What happened here was not amplification. You did not generate additional force.”

“So where did it come from?”

“It was already there.”

Pike’s momentum.

Kade’s stance.

The floor.

The building.

“You changed where it counted,” he said.

Sen nodded once.

“Possibly.”

“Possibly is doing a lot of work.”

“That is science.”

Kade leaned back.

“Boundary architecture.”

“A provisional term.”

“You already had a term.”

Sen went still.

There.

Mara had been right.

Kade sat forward.

“You said that too quickly yesterday.”

Sen did not answer.

“Has someone done this before?”

A pause.

“No.”

Kade watched her.

The answer was clean.

Too clean.

“Never?”

“Not in any verified modern program.”

That was not the same answer.

He felt it immediately.

“Modern.”

Sen’s expression did not change.

Kade almost pressed harder.

Then he remembered the phone call at Northline.

The valid Zero route from three decades ago.

“Is this connected to Zero Protocol?”

Sen stood.

“This session is over.”

“That means yes.”

“It means the session is over.”

“Director—”

“Kade.”

Her voice sharpened for the first time.

“Curiosity is useful. So is survival.”

He stared at her.

Sen took a breath.

When she spoke again, her voice was quieter.

“We are going to teach you carefully. We are going to learn what you can do without assuming that novelty makes you invulnerable.”

“I never assumed that.”

“Good.”

She looked at his hands.

“Because whatever you did to that floor also happened through you.”

The headache felt suddenly more significant.

Kade stood.

At the door, he stopped.

“One question.”

Sen looked tired again.

“Did you know my father?”

The question came from nowhere.

Or maybe it had been waiting since Northline.

Sen’s face did something almost too small to see.

Then it was gone.

“No,” she said.

Kade nodded.

He left.

In the hallway, he stood still for several seconds.

Sen had lied twice in one meeting.

He was almost certain of the first.

He was completely certain of the second.

He did not know why.

But for the first time, the mystery around Asterion felt personal.
