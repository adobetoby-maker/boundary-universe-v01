# Chapter 30 — Siege of Asterion

The first thing Kade learned about real violence was that it did not wait for the arena signal.

The second was that nobody looked heroic while running from falling glass.

Asterion’s eastern transit spine went dark at 03:12.

Three minutes later, the Directorate seized the sublevel control rooms.

By 03:19, House Meridian was awake.

By 03:23, Julian Cross was giving orders.

Kade noticed because everyone listened.

Not because Julian was rank one.

Because he was calm.

“Meridian teams one through four, evacuation routes. Five and six, med support. Nobody engages unless necessary.”

Sera said, “You sound annoyingly competent.”

Julian nodded. “I practice.”

The building shook.

Students froze.

Julian did not.

“Move.”

They moved.

The campus Kade had spent months learning became something else.

Dining halls became triage stations.

Training corridors became barricades.

Rankings vanished from the walls, replaced by emergency maps.

Nobody cared who was #211.

Kade found that strangely comforting.

The first Directorate operative he saw wore academy maintenance gray.

That frightened him more than a uniform would have.

The man fired a field pulse down the corridor.

Mara redirected it into the ceiling.

Sera pulled two younger students behind cover.

Kade felt the structure around them.

Walls.

Floor.

Supports.

People.

He did not reach for the whole system.

Not yet.

He used what he knew.

Timing.

Angles.

Trust.

They forced the operative back without seriously injuring him.

The victory felt nothing like an arena win.

Kade’s hands shook afterward.

Mara saw.

“So do mine,” she said.

He looked.

They did.

That helped.

A message reached them from Sen.

**SUBLEVEL NINE BREACHED. CONTAINMENT AT RISK.**

Eli went pale.

“What’s in Sublevel Nine?” Sera asked.

Sen’s next message arrived.

**THE REASON ASTERION EXISTS.**

Julian read it once.

“Of course it is.”

They headed down.

At the junction above Sublevel Nine, Taren appeared from the smoke.

Sera raised her guard instantly.

Julian moved between them.

Taren lifted both hands.

“I know how this looks.”

Kade said, “You have no idea.”

Taren looked exhausted.

“They’re opening the chamber.”

“Why?” Mara asked.

“They think there’s a weapon inside.”

“Is there?”

Taren looked at Kade.

“No.”

“How do you know?”

“Because they told me what they wanted after it was too late to pretend I was still only observing.”

Kade stared at him.

Taren said, “I can get you through the security lock.”

Sera said, “And then?”

“Then you can decide whether to hit me.”

Sera considered that.

“Reasonable.”

Taren opened the door.

They descended together.

Trust had become an ugly, imperfect thing.

Kade was beginning to understand that maybe it always was.

Sublevel Nine opened beneath them.

A chamber the size of an arena filled the center.

Inside, something impossible glowed beneath layers of containment fields.

And beside it stood a man Kade knew from one photograph on a hallway wall at home.

Older.

Thinner.

Alive.

Aaron Mercer looked up.

He saw Kade.

And every field in the chamber failed at once.
