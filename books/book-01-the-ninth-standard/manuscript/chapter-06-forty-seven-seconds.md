# Chapter 6 — Forty-Seven Seconds

Kade’s first official combat assessment lasted forty-seven seconds.

The academy would replay the final six for weeks.

He spent the first forty-one discovering how thoroughly a trained Resonant could rearrange his understanding of pain.

The assessment arena was smaller than the broadcast stadiums, built for instruction rather than spectacle. White composite floor. Transparent barrier walls. Two starting circles twelve meters apart.

One hundred first-years sat in tiered seats around the perimeter.

Kade stood inside the left circle wearing a gray training suit and a sensor collar that felt too expensive to sweat in.

Across from him stood a girl named Priya Shah.

Rank 6,204.

Standard One confirmed.

Kade’s current combat rating remained blank.

Priya looked at the board, then at him.

“Sorry.”

Kade frowned.

“For what?”

She lifted her hands into a ready stance.

“This is going to look bad.”

Kade glanced toward the observation booth.

“Which one of us?”

Priya smiled.

“I like you.”

“That makes one person.”

The instructor’s voice came through the arena speaker.

“Assessment rules. Controlled contact. No head strikes. No intentional joint injury. Field output capped at twelve percent of measured safe maximum. Match ends on pin, surrender, boundary exit, or instructor stop.”

Kade raised a hand.

The instructor paused.

“Yes, Mercer?”

“What if my measured safe maximum is zero?”

Several students laughed.

The instructor did not.

“Then I recommend creativity.”

Kade lowered his hand.

“Finally, sensible advice.”

In the front row, Eli covered his face.

Mara sat beside him, arms folded.

Sera stood behind them against the wall.

Taren Holt had somehow acquired a seat three rows higher despite not being assigned to the session. Kade had met him on the Meridian roof the previous night and learned three things quickly.

Taren talked easily.

Taren knew everyone.

Taren answered questions by giving information adjacent to the thing asked.

Kade had not yet decided whether that made him useful or dangerous.

Probably both.

The signal light changed from red to amber.

Priya bounced once on the balls of her feet.

Kade did not.

Amber to green.

Priya disappeared.

Not actually.

Kade knew that.

But his eyes lost her anyway.

Something struck his chest.

Air left his lungs.

His feet left the floor.

He hit the arena mat on his back and slid three meters.

The display timer read:

**00:03**

Kade stared upward.

“Well,” he wheezed.

Priya stopped instead of following.

“You okay?”

“Excellent hospitality.”

“Get up, Mercer,” the instructor said.

Kade rolled to one knee.

His chest hurt.

Not badly.

The suit had absorbed most of the impact.

What bothered him was not being hit.

It was not seeing how.

Priya had crossed twelve meters almost instantly, but she had not simply run faster.

The floor beneath her starting circle showed faint scuffing.

Momentum.

She had changed hers.

Or borrowed it.

Or redistributed something Kade still could not sense.

He stood.

Priya returned to ready stance.

“Again?” she asked.

“I was hoping we’d move to the written portion.”

She laughed.

Then attacked.

This time Kade watched her feet.

Wrong choice.

Her shoulder struck him from the side.

He spun.

Priya hooked his ankle and put him down.

Timer:

**00:09**

The audience had stopped laughing.

Not because Kade was doing well.

Because the novelty was wearing off.

He knew that feeling too.

At Northline, being bad at something in public was ordinary.

At Asterion, failure had an audience with statistics.

Priya backed away.

“Stay down if you need to.”

Kade pushed himself up.

“I’m beginning to dislike your kindness.”

“It’s not strategic.”

“Even worse.”

He stood again.

The instructor had not stopped the match.

Kade looked toward the booth.

Dr. Park sat behind the glass.

Next to her stood Dr. Sen.

Of course.

No pressure.

Kade rolled his shoulders.

Priya came forward more slowly this time.

That helped.

Not enough.

She feinted left.

Kade followed.

She changed direction with a sharpness no unassisted body should manage.

He barely turned before her forearm touched his shoulder and redirected him toward the boundary.

Kade stumbled.

He recovered one step before the line.

Timer:

**00:18**

Priya stopped.

Kade stared at her.

“You could have ended it.”

“Yes.”

“Why didn’t you?”

“It’s an assessment.”

“So?”

“So they need to see what you do.”

Kade almost said, *Nothing.*

He stopped himself.

Sera’s voice from dinner returned.

*Careful.*

He looked at the arena differently.

Not at Priya.

At everything.

The floor.

The walls.

Her feet.

His own balance.

The faint fractures in the chalky traction coating where students had accelerated before them.

He could not feel Resonance.

Fine.

He could feel consequence.

Priya shifted her weight.

Kade saw it before she moved.

Not the field.

The decision.

Her right foot loaded.

Her hips turned.

The floor beneath her became part of the motion before her body did.

Kade moved first.

Priya’s eyes widened.

He stepped inside the attack instead of away.

Her forearm missed his chest.

Kade caught her wrist.

For half a second, the entire arena seemed to pause.

Then Priya smiled.

“Better.”

She rotated.

Kade went over her hip.

He hit the floor.

Timer:

**00:27**

This time the audience laughed with him rather than at him.

He stayed on his back.

Priya leaned over.

“You almost had something.”

“I had your wrist.”

“That is technically something.”

Kade got up.

His ribs ached.

His left shoulder had begun to complain.

The sensor collar remained green.

He looked at the clock.

Twenty-seven seconds.

It felt like ten minutes.

Priya attacked again at thirty-one.

Kade avoided the first strike.

Not the second.

He took a controlled hit to the ribs, absorbed it, and grabbed her sleeve.

She accelerated sideways.

He let go before she dragged him off balance.

Thirty-six seconds.

She was faster.

Stronger.

Trained.

He could not beat her at the thing she had spent years learning.

That seemed obvious.

So stop doing her thing.

Kade backed toward the arena wall.

Priya slowed.

“You’re cornering yourself.”

“Maybe I like architecture.”

She frowned.

Kade’s heel touched the boundary strip.

One step behind him was elimination.

The wall stood less than a meter farther.

Priya had one obvious choice.

Drive him out.

She took it.

At forty-one seconds, Priya accelerated.

Kade watched the whole system.

Her body.

Her foot.

The floor.

His body.

The boundary.

The wall.

Force had to go somewhere.

The thought was not new.

The sensation was.

A cold line formed behind his eyes.

Not pain.

Clarity.

For one impossible instant, Kade did not see objects.

He saw connections.

Priya’s foot to the floor.

Floor to support lattice.

Support lattice to wall.

Her momentum into him.

His body into the boundary.

A chain.

A system.

And systems only ended where somebody decided they ended.

Priya struck his chest.

Kade should have flown backward.

Instead the wall boomed.

The sound hit the arena like thunder inside a room.

Every light flickered.

Priya stopped dead.

Not gently.

Her momentum vanished from Kade’s chest and resolved somewhere behind him.

The transparent wall flexed outward.

A web of white stress lines flashed through the floor supports.

Kade remained standing.

For half a second.

Then both his knees gave out.

He dropped.

Priya stumbled forward, shocked.

Kade’s vision narrowed.

The instructor shouted, “Stop!”

Timer:

**00:47**

Silence.

Not normal arena silence.

The kind where a hundred people all forgot to make sound at once.

Kade sat on the floor.

His hands trembled.

The cold line behind his eyes became pain.

Priya crouched several feet away.

“What did you do?”

Kade looked at the wall.

The stress lines were already fading as the smart material redistributed load.

“I have no idea.”

That was not entirely true.

He knew what it had felt like.

The test.

The line through the platform.

**REDEFINE.**

His stomach turned.

Dr. Park entered the arena at a run.

Sen walked behind her.

Of course Sen walked.

People who frightened rooms rarely needed to hurry.

Park knelt beside Kade and checked his pupils.

“Headache?”

“Yes.”

“Nausea?”

“A little.”

“Tremor?”

Kade held up his hand.

It shook.

“Very observant.”

Park ignored him.

Sen crouched beside Priya.

“You injured?”

Priya shook her head.

“Just surprised.”

Sen looked at the wall.

Then at Kade.

No smile this time.

Behind the barrier, students had begun whispering.

Eli stood with both hands on the glass.

Mara had risen from her seat.

Taren was no longer pretending he had wandered in accidentally.

Sera watched Kade with an expression he could not read.

The instructor checked the official result.

“Match to Shah. Mercer unable to continue.”

Kade laughed weakly.

Priya looked offended on his behalf.

“I hit him and the wall broke.”

“You remained combat capable.”

“He did something.”

Sen said, “Yes.”

The single word quieted everyone nearby.

Kade looked at her.

“What?”

Sen’s attention remained on the damaged support lattice.

“Not here.”

Kade pushed himself upright.

Park pressed a hand to his shoulder.

“Stay seated.”

“I’m fine.”

“No.”

“People keep getting very comfortable with that word.”

“You are shaking and your pupils are uneven.”

Kade sat.

A maintenance display illuminated on the wall.

**STRUCTURAL LOAD EVENT DETECTED**

**SOURCE: UNRESOLVED**

Eli made a sound somewhere between a laugh and a gasp.

Mara said something to him Kade could not hear.

The arena replay screen activated automatically.

Priya’s final charge appeared in slow motion.

Forty-one seconds.

Her foot loaded.

Acceleration.

Impact.

Then something strange.

The telemetry lines that normally tracked field transfer disappeared for six frames.

When they returned, the wall registered the energy.

Six frames.

Kade watched the clip again.

Priya’s fist touched his chest.

No visible flash.

No field glow.

No dramatic wave.

Just consequence in the wrong place.

The replay froze.

A red banner appeared.

**DATA FLAGGED — RESTRICTED REVIEW**

The audience reacted immediately.

Kade closed his eyes.

“Please tell me that doesn’t mean another government car.”

Sen stood.

“No.”

He opened one eye.

“That sounded promising.”

“It means three.”

Kade let his head fall back against the wall.

Priya laughed.

He looked at her.

“Sorry,” she said.

“For what?”

She smiled.

“This is going to look bad.”

Kade laughed despite the headache.

Outside the arena, somebody had already uploaded the final six seconds.

By the time Park got him to medical, the clip had thirty thousand views inside Asterion.

By lunch, it had a name.

**The Forty-Seven Second Fight.**

And somewhere behind three layers of restricted access, six people replayed the final impact over and over, trying to determine how a student with zero measurable output had moved enough force to bend a wall.
