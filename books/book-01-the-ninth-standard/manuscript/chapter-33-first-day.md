# Chapter 33 — First Day

Three months later, Asterion looked repaired from a distance.

Up close, Kade could still find the scars.

A replacement wall panel near Meridian that reflected morning light at the wrong angle.

A darker strip of flooring outside Lab Six.

New fire doors in the east engineering wing.

A hairline repair across the arena once called the Mercer Fault.

He preferred that.

Perfect repairs would have felt dishonest.

The official story of the siege occupied four pages and said almost nothing.

An unlawful security breach.

Internal extremist activity.

Infrastructure damage.

No public mention of Contact Zero.

No Refuge Node.

No Aaron Mercer.

No explanation for why several governments had quietly sent representatives to Asterion before the smoke was fully gone.

The unofficial story occupied every conversation on campus.

Students knew the Directorate had attacked from inside.

They knew some faculty had been involved.

They knew something had happened below the Spire that caused every high-level field sensor on the island to record the same impossible spike.

And they knew Kade Mercer had somehow been in the middle of it.

Again.

He was getting tired of being historically adjacent.

House Meridian’s bronze bell had been repaired first.

That had been Sera’s demand.

“It is an evacuation signal as well as a victory bell,” she had told facilities.

Facilities had attempted to explain that this was not its documented purpose.

Sera had handed them the siege response report.

The bell was repaired the next day.

She had accepted the accelerated Disaster Response Corps assessment two weeks later.

She did not talk about it much.

Which meant everyone in Meridian talked about it constantly.

Eli’s Systems Lab fellowship application had also been approved.

He claimed the academy had been emotionally manipulated by his temporary death.

His faculty advisor claimed the stabilization design was simply good.

Kade suspected both were true.

Eli now spent three evenings a week trying to reduce the civilian unit cost without accidentally starting fires.

Progress was mixed.

Taren remained at Asterion under restricted status while three separate investigations fought over what to do with him.

His Directorate cooperation had helped stop the siege.

His earlier reporting had helped make the siege possible.

Both facts were true.

Sera had not forgiven him.

Neither had Kade.

But Taren still attended classes under supervision and still sat with Meridian at meals when the supervision rules allowed it.

The first time he returned to the table, nobody spoke for almost a minute.

Then Eli asked him to pass the salt.

Taren did.

That had been the entire reconciliation.

So far.

Julian remained number one in everyone’s head even during the three-month period when official rankings were suspended.

He hated when people said that.

Which encouraged them.

Mara had spent the break dividing her time between training, arguing with her father, and helping Sen’s independent inquiry into precursor records.

She and Alaric Vey were speaking again.

Carefully.

According to Mara, this was different from speaking normally because now they admitted when they were withholding things.

Kade found that darkly funny.

Aaron Mercer was alive.

Almost nobody knew.

The first week after the siege, Kade visited him every day in the secure medical wing.

The second week, every other day.

By the third, they had settled into something less like reunion and more like negotiations between two people who happened to share a face.

Kade had spoken to him eleven times.

Eight conversations had ended badly.

One had ended with both of them silent for twenty minutes.

One had involved Aaron trying to explain why staying in containment had seemed necessary while Kade explained why necessity did not make seventeen years disappear.

The last conversation had gone differently.

Aaron had asked about Northline.

Not Resonance.

Not Consensus.

School.

Kade had told him about Darius and the vending machine.

Aaron had laughed.

Kade had hated how much he wanted to hear the sound again.

That was, according to Elena, progress.

Elena had visited Asterion twice.

The first time she saw Aaron, she stood outside the medical-room door for six minutes before entering.

Kade did not stay for the conversation.

Some boundaries were his to respect.

Afterward, Elena sat with Kade on the seawall and cried without apologizing.

Kade had no idea what to do, so he sat beside her.

Apparently that counted.

The countdown continued the entire time.

Lunar Far-Side Array had confirmed the returned coordinates were outside the solar system.

The sequence translated to just over one Earth year at receipt.

No government publicly acknowledged it.

Every government that knew about it treated the number like a lit fuse.

Asterion students knew only that unusual military traffic had tripled around the island.

The new semester began under clear skies.

Thousands of students gathered in the central forum for the ranking reset.

Kade stood with Meridian.

The repaired Spire rose above them.

The ocean was bright enough to hurt his eyes.

Eli bounced on his heels.

“You nervous?”

“No.”

Mara looked at him.

Kade sighed.

“Yes.”

“Growth,” she said.

“I regret learning things.”

She smiled.

Not the tiny problem-solving smile.

A real one.

Kade noticed.

He did not make it weird.

That might have been his greatest advancement yet.

The reset rankings were not a direct continuation of the old ladder. Asterion recalculated each semester using tournament performance, challenge history, academic field evaluations, team events, instructor scoring, and safety-adjusted capability.

For most students the changes were modest.

For Kade, the academy had spent weeks arguing about what category he belonged in at all.

The boards illuminated.

Names populated from the middle outward.

Kade waited.

#211 disappeared into the recalculation.

Mara’s name appeared.

**VEY, MARA — #4**

Meridian cheered.

Mara looked mildly inconvenienced by being celebrated.

Julian appeared.

**CROSS, JULIAN — #1**

He accepted the obvious with great dignity.

Then Kade’s tablet vibrated.

His name appeared on the main display.

**MERCER, KADE**

**RANK: #37**

For several seconds, he simply stared.

Eli made a sound that should not have come from a human being.

Sera punched Kade lightly in the shoulder.

Taren, standing under supervision at the edge of Meridian’s group, laughed.

Julian called across the forum.

“Still thirty-six places to go.”

Kade looked at him.

“You’ve been saving that?”

“For weeks.”

“Terrible.”

Mara was smiling again.

Kade looked back at the board.

Thirty-seven.

He remembered ten thousand four hundred eighty-two.

He remembered how that first number had felt like every teacher report and every failed class compressed into one public verdict.

He remembered telling Darius to enjoy it while he could.

The strange part was that #37 did not feel like proof that the old number had been wrong.

#10,482 had been true when it appeared.

He had been untrained.

Unprepared.

Terrible at footwork.

Still terrible at some notation.

The number had described something.

It simply had not described everything.

Kade liked that better.

Then the board flickered.

A second line appeared beneath his rank.

**STANDARD: IX**

The central forum went silent.

Not quiet.

Silent.

Standards did not use Roman numerals on public ranking boards.

There was no public Standard Nine.

The academy system had apparently disagreed.

Kade looked toward Sen on the administration platform.

For once, she looked surprised.

Eli whispered, “Did you know they were going to do that?”

“No.”

Mara said, “Neither did she.”

That was not reassuring.

Every display on campus changed at once.

Asterion’s emblem vanished.

Rankings disappeared.

The coordinates appeared.

The same coordinates the lunar array had received three months earlier.

Then the countdown.

**00:364:17:42:08**

A collective sound moved through the forum.

Confusion first.

Then fear.

Eli stared.

“That is definitely days.”

Mara looked toward the administration platform.

Sen was already speaking into three secure channels.

Julian’s expression had lost every trace of humor.

Sera automatically scanned exits and crowd density.

Taren looked at the numbers as if they had personally accused him of something.

Kade looked toward the Pacific.

Somewhere beyond the horizon, beyond Earth, beyond every problem he had understood while sitting in Room Four complaining about a physics question, something had answered.

His tablet buzzed.

For one absurd second he expected another classified alert.

It was Elena.

**Proud of you. Also eat something.**

Kade laughed.

Mara looked over.

“What?”

“My mother remains focused on the important threats.”

“What did she say?”

“Eat something.”

Mara considered the countdown.

“Reasonable.”

Another message arrived.

Darius.

**BRO YOU ARE THIRTY SEVEN WHAT**

Then:

**ALSO WHY IS SCHOOL SHOWING SPACE MATH**

Kade typed:

**Long story.**

Darius replied:

**YOU SAY THAT TOO MUCH**

True.

Kade put the tablet away.

The countdown continued.

A year ago, Kade would have looked for an exit.

Not necessarily a physical one.

A joke.

An excuse.

A reason none of this was really his problem.

Now he looked around.

Mara.

Eli.

Sera.

Julian.

Taren at the edge of the group.

Sen on the platform.

His mother eight hundred miles away.

Darius at Northline.

Aaron beneath the medical wing, alive and complicated and not yet forgiven.

People who made the system harder.

People who made leaving more expensive.

People he would not trade for simplicity.

Mara stepped closer.

Their shoulders touched.

Neither moved away.

“So,” she said. “First day.”

Kade looked at the countdown.

Then at his friends.

“Could be worse.”

Eli stared at him.

“Something in space just sent us a one-year countdown.”

Kade nodded.

“Fair point.”

The numbers kept falling.

And for once, Kade Mercer did not care where the exit was.
