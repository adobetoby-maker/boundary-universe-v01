# Chapter 25 — Taren Holt

Kade had a file on Taren Holt, and he did not like owning it.

It was not a real file. It was a place in his head where he had been putting things since October, because they did not fit anywhere else. Taren had known the date of Kade's combat assessment before Kade did. He had known which Kepler runner was eleven seconds slow. He had turned his band face-in against his forearm at the Trial, three times in one evening. He had sent Mara a message about a dinner she had told nobody she was skipping. He had been sitting on the forum steps with a sandwich, in the one place a person leaving the Spire had to pass. Every coffee order was right, every time.

For four months Kade had filed all of it under one heading, which was *Taren is good at people*. Then Sen had put four blank blocks on a wall and let Mara name one of them, and the heading no longer covered the contents.

He took the file up to the Meridian roof three days after the storm. He sat on the parapet beside the bell anchor with the island going down into the dusk below him.

The privacy lasted about four minutes.

"You look suspiciously reflective," Taren said. He had two cans in one hand, as he had the first night, and he held one out.

Kade did not take it. "Why do you know so much?"

Taren's arm stayed out a moment longer and then came down. "About what?"

"About everything. My schedule, before I get it. Where my footage goes when it leaves the arena. Who's hurt. Which lift is shut tomorrow. Which desk closes Thursday."

"People tell me things."

"Why do they?"

"Because I listen. It's a rare skill. You should try it on a slow day."

"That's the answer you always give."

"It keeps being true."

It was, and that was the difficulty. Kade had spent his whole life keeping a closed face, and he had assumed that a person as open as Taren had nothing behind the door.

"What's the Continuity Directorate?" he said.

He had been watching for it, as he watched a shoulder before a punch, and he still nearly missed it. Taren's face did not fall. It stopped. For about the length of a blink there was nobody behind the smile, and then there was again.

"You heard Sen say it?"

"I asked you."

"It's a government planning group. Continuity of government. What happens if the worst happens."

"That's the sentence on the brochure."

"That's the sentence most people get."

"You've got another one."

Taren looked down at the can he had not opened. "Probably."

"Probably?"

"Kade."

"No. Not this time." Kade took the second can out of Taren's hand and set it on the stone between them, unopened. "You say my name like that and I'm supposed to let it go. I've let it go since October."

Taren looked up. The easy face was gone, and the one under it was tired, much more tired than seventeen. His mouth opened.

The roof door banged. Two second-years came out laughing, with a blanket and a bag of something from the late kitchen.

Taren's face came back at once. "Mercer wants to know whether the government keeps secret assassins," he announced, to the roof at large.

"Obviously," said one of them.

"Those are just tax auditors," said the other, and they went off round the bell frame to the west side.

Taren opened his can and drank.

"You did that on purpose," Kade said.

"I did."

"Why?"

"Because not every conversation belongs where other people can hear it."

"I've heard that before. From the Director, mostly."

Taren winced before he could hide it. He looked at the corner the second-years had gone round. When he spoke he was very quiet.

"Don't go looking for Directorate files by yourself."

"That's extremely specific."

"I know it is."

"What aren't you telling me?"

"More than I should be able to." Taren put his free hand in his pocket. "Drink that. It's the kind you like."

He left. Kade sat with the can sweating on the stone beside him. It was the kind he liked.

He did not go after him. Later he would count that among the things he should have done.

---

The plan room was under the Commons, next to the laundry, and it still smelled of warm lint. Taren's column of numbers from the qualifiers was still pinned to the wall, with the 3 somebody had added at the bottom.

Mara put her list on the table display beside it.

AARON MERCER. CONTINUITY DIRECTORATE. COUPLING TRIALS, APPLICATION. ASTERION PRECURSOR SITE.

"Sen's authorization came through this morning," she said. "It's a historical reading permission, in all four of our names, for the restricted stacks under old eastern administration. It covers the precursor technical records. It does not cover anything the Directorate filed."

"Which she will have known when she wrote it," said Kade.

"Of course. That's what it's for. We can read everything around the hole." Mara looked round the table. "We use the permission we've got. If the system refuses us, we stop."

Kade looked at her. "Do you believe that?"

"I expect it of us. Belief is a separate question."

"Good," he said, and meant it.

The door opened, and Taren came in. Nobody had asked him. Sera's face changed first.

He looked at the list. Then he looked at Kade. "You're going into eastern archive."

"How do you know that?"

"Because that's where those collections are kept."

"Most first-years couldn't tell you that."

"No." Taren shut the door behind him and stayed by it. "Your permission's good. It will get you through the front entrance and as far as the inner bridge, and then it will fail. The bridge was built before the academy had students. It wants a faculty biometric from an access model nobody has used in twenty years. It will refuse you, and it will log all four names as refused, and you won't have read a page."

"How do you know what the bridge wants?" said Eli.

"Because it's old."

"That explains the bridge. It doesn't explain you."

Taren's smile started toward them and gave out halfway across the room. Kade had never seen that happen. It was like watching someone reach for a rail that had been taken away.

"Who are you?" Kade said.

"Your friend."

"You know that's not what I asked."

Sera moved. She did not hurry, and she ended up standing between Taren and the door. "You don't come anywhere with us until you've said why you can open it."

Taren looked at the four of them. For several seconds Kade thought he would simply go. Then he pulled out a stool and sat down on it, heavily.

"My family works around international security. My mother's diplomatic protection. My uncle was in continuity analysis."

The word did more than the rest of the sentence. "Directorate," said Kade.

Taren nodded once. "He was. He's retired."

"And he's why you can get in?"

Taren hesitated, and Kade saw it.

"Not exactly," Taren said.

"Then we're finished," said Sera, and put her hand on the door.

"Wait." Taren was on his feet. "There's a maintenance entrance. It was built so technicians could service both sides of the bridge without being cleared to read anything. It checks an infrastructure identity, not a faculty one. I can take you in that way, and nothing with your names on it is touched."

"You don't inherit that from an uncle," said Eli.

"No. You don't."

It was a true thing, said so as to leave a gap, and Kade was tired of gaps. "Tell me now. All of it."

Taren looked away. "I can't. Not in here."

"Then we don't go."

It surprised the room, and it surprised Kade a little. In September curiosity would have beaten caution before he had finished the thought. But Julian had spent a month teaching him to hold a plan still long enough to look at it, and he looked at this one. Mara's father knew phrases he should not. Sen had gone pale at a routing stamp. Taren could open a door nobody his age could open, and would not say why. There was too much he did not know, and all of it lay in the direction they were about to walk.

Something went across Taren's face that looked like relief. Then it looked like fear.

"They already know you found the twelfth name," he said.

Nobody moved. Through the wall a dryer thumped round.

"How?" Kade said quietly.

"I don't know."

"Then how do you know that they did?"

Taren's eyes closed for a moment. "Because somebody asked me what you were looking for."

Sera came a step nearer. "Who asked?"

He did not answer. Mara did it for him. "The Directorate."

Taren nodded.

"You talk to them," he said.

"I do."

Eli had gone completely still, which he never was. Sera's hands had loosened at her sides, as they did before a bout.

"For how long?"

"Too long."

"Give me a number, Taren."

"I will. I'll give you every number I've got. But not in a room with a network jack in the skirting board." He swallowed. "If you want the whole of it, it has to be somewhere they can't hear. The old eastern sublevel is shielded. It was built to be."

"So your safe place to talk about them is their own archive," said Sera.

"Yes."

"That's either very clever or the most suspicious thing I have ever heard," Eli said.

"It's both," said Taren.

Kade ought to have walked out. He knew it then. But the ground had moved since he said *we don't go*. The Directorate already knew what they were looking for. Somebody had asked, and Taren had been standing there to be asked. They were not choosing between safe and unsafe any more. They were choosing whether to be watched while they knew something or while they knew nothing.

He looked at Mara. She looked back and did not decide it for him. He looked at Sera, who hated it and did not bother to hide that. He found both of those steadying.

"Show us the route first," he said. "Every piece of it you expect us to take on your word."

Some light remark about his wounded credit got as far as Taren's mouth. He stopped it there. "All right."

He pushed Mara's list to the side of the display and drew underneath it with a fingertip. The old eastern lecture hall went on top. Two public stairs ran down from it to faculty storage. Below storage he drew a long passage running south behind the archive rooms, to where it met the engineering utility tunnels.

"This is the maintenance spine. The front entrance is here." He marked it in red. "We don't use it. We go in here, off the spine. There's a service door behind a cabinet."

"Whose identity opens it?" said Sera.

His finger stayed on the door. "Mine will."

Sera took over the display. She circled the public stair, and then the hatch where the spine met the utility tunnels. "Two exits. We test both from the inside before anybody opens a file. If either one won't open, we leave by the other. Eli keeps the map, and keeps it off the House network. Mara owns the search. Kade does not touch anything unfamiliar because it looks interesting."

"That last rule feels personal."

"It's based on data."

Eli was already copying the plan and pulling his tablet off the network. "What do we use for comms, if the shielding's that good?"

Taren drew three short strokes along the spine. "Hardline call points, one at each junction. They reach campus security."

"And the Directorate?" said Mara.

He did not lift his finger. "If that layer is live, you should assume every fixed system down there reaches them."

"Including the doors you open," Kade said.

"Possibly."

"You left that out a minute ago."

"I did. I shouldn't have."

Sera made one more mark, where the spine met the archive corridor. "Abort point. We stop there and check the way back again. Nobody separates. If he changes the route and doesn't say why first, he stops leading."

Taren could have argued that every delay made them easier to find. He gave Eli the old infrastructure code for the tunnel hatch and stepped back from the table.

"One more thing you should have," he said. "The alarms down there aren't the campus ones. Three low notes is fire suppression. One long tone is archive security, somebody local. Red lights and no sound at all means the room has been isolated from outside."

"Outer archive only," Kade said. "What Sen's paper covers. The first thing that changes, we walk out."

"Those are the limits," Taren said.

"I reserve the right to hit him," said Sera.

Taren looked at her. "Also agreed."

---

An evening policy seminar was letting out of the old eastern lecture hall as they arrived. Third-years stood about the corridor in knots, arguing about emergency powers with the pleasure of people for whom it was a reading. A faculty assistant held the door for Sera. Taren thanked her by name and kept walking, at exactly the pace of someone with nowhere to be.

Kade stayed two steps behind him. Taren had always crossed the campus like that. People remembered how easy he was and forgot to ask which door he had come in by.

At the bottom of the first public stair Sera said, "Return check." Eli pushed the bar from the inside. It released. He held it while the sensor counted them, and let it close.

"That's one," he said.

In faculty storage there was a steel cabinet labeled LECTURE SUPPORT, ANALOG EQUIPMENT. Kade had taken the two recessed fittings at its feet for screws. Taren pressed them with his heel, one and then the other, and the cabinet rolled aside on a track. The door behind it had no handle.

Taren put two fingers to the frame. Nothing happened. Sera's weight shifted. He tried again, lower, and a blue line ran under his fingertips and a small panel lit. LOCAL IDENTITY ACCEPTED.

The passage behind it was raw concrete, and just wide enough for two. It was also a good deal steeper than the drawing.

Sera went first. Her sole skated on the dust and she caught the wall before anyone could reach her. "Single file. Use the pipe for a rail."

The pipe was cold and it hummed faintly under Kade's palm. He counted the turns as they went down. There were three. When he looked back the service door was still open, a pale upright rectangle a long way up.

At the first junction Eli woke the hardline call point. The little screen said CAMPUS SECURITY AVAILABLE. In the corner under the words a mark flashed once and went out. It was a circle with a gap in it, and three short upright lines standing in the gap.

Taren was looking at the corner where it had been.

"What was that?" Kade said.

The answer came half a second late. "Legacy routing mark."

"Directorate?" said Mara.

"The archive inherited some of their plumbing."

Sera reached past him and killed the call before it could connect. "Does that mark mean the line told them we're here?"

"It means it could have."

"You knew that upstairs."

"I suspected it."

Kade looked up the slope at the pale rectangle. They could still go. Nothing had stopped them. The route had turned up exactly the doubt Taren had said it might, and no more than that. Mara stood at the next turn and waited, and did not hurry him.

He went through it as he would a board between exchanges. The Directorate knew what they were hunting for, and had known before tonight. The fixed systems might be reporting and might not. One exit was proven and the second was forty meters on.

"Second return check," he said. "Then we decide."

The utility hatch was at the south end of the spine. Eli keyed the code Taren had surrendered in the plan room. The hatch opened on a dark tunnel and a smell of wet iron, and he shut it again.

"Two," he said. "Both good. For now."

Sera led from there, and Taren let her. At the abort point she stopped them, sent Eli back to the junction to look up the slope, and did not move until he had come back and nodded.

The archive corridor was nothing like the spine. It was clean, and cold, and the air had been through a filter recently. The seals on the doors were new and so were the cameras. When Taren touched a wall plate the nearest camera turned its face to the ceiling.

Kade slowed. "You've done this before."

"I know the infrastructure."

"That's not what I asked you."

"I know. I keep doing that."

They passed two sealed side doors. On the second there was a new Asterion access plate, and under the edge of it the ghost of something older, etched in the steel. It was a broken circle, closed with three upright lines.

Kade stopped, and so did Taren. It was the mark from the call point.

"That's theirs."

"Yes."

"You didn't need to ask me which mark I meant."

Taren said nothing, and Kade found that he did not need him to.

The reading room was small. There were four terminals on a steel bench, a wall panel by the entrance, and a slot in the ceiling above the doorway that Kade noticed and did not think about. Taren stayed at the threshold, in the corridor, apart from the search he had walked them to.

"You're not reading?" Kade said.

"I'm keeping watch."

"What for?"

"People more responsible than we are."

Sera put herself where she could see both him and the corridor beyond him. Eli bridged his slate to the first terminal and fed it Sen's permission. It took. Mara ran the list.

Most of it came back as index lines with nothing behind them. The fourth search brought up twelve identifiers, and a ring diagram Kade had seen on Sen's wall, and a file header. It should not have opened on a historical permission. It opened.

CONTINUITY DIRECTORATE. CONSENSUS APPLICATION STUDY.

"Consensus," said Eli. "Is that what they called it? The coupling."

"It's what these people called it," Mara said. She read the first paragraph aloud, in the level voice she used for contracts. "'Objective. To determine the feasibility of coordinated multi-operator Boundary control under centralized command authority.'"

Kade's stomach drew tight. "Centralized command. One operator."

"And eleven instruments. Sen said it." Mara scrolled. "'Degradation of operator autonomy is acceptable within operational tolerances.'"

"Say that in human," said Sera.

"They measured how much of a person's own control went missing," Eli said. "And they wrote down how much missing was fine. It's a design variable. It's got a tolerance band." He sounded ill.

Mara opened a later memorandum. It was a single page. "'Recommendation. Prioritize stability of the command node over the subjective continuity of participants.'"

Kade read it on the screen himself, because he did not trust his ears. "Subjective continuity."

"Whether the person still feels like themselves," Eli said quietly. "Afterward."

Kade thought of the Trial floor, and the horn going, and the moment the plan had stopped being his and got better. Eight people had agreed about a problem and gone on being eight people. He had felt what that was like from the inside. Somebody had looked at that same physics and seen a way to make twelve people into parts. His father had stood in a ring and said no to it, loudly, and had not come out.

"They were willing to spend that," Sera said. "People's selves."

"Some of them were," said Taren, from the door.

They all turned.

"You knew," Kade said.

"Not this. I never saw documents."

"You knew what they wanted."

"I knew they thought a commanded version was a survival tool. That's how it's talked about. As if it were a lifeboat."

"Since when did you—"

The screens went black, all four at once.

The lights went from white to red. Kade waited for a tone. He waited for three low notes or one long one. No sound came at all.

He had time to understand what that meant. Then something heavy let go in the ceiling, and a clear partition came down through the slot above the doorway and met the floor with a sound like a vault.

Taren was on the other side of it.

He did not look triumphant, and he did not look cold. Kade had braced for either one. He looked ashamed.

"Taren," Kade said.

"I'm sorry."

Sera hit the partition with the heel of her hand, hard enough to make it ring. "Open it."

"I can't."

"You have opened every door in this building."

"That's the reason." He got it out with difficulty. "Every door I opened for you, I opened as me. I knew it might tell them. I told myself that *might* was a long way from *would*. They know you're here because I brought you."

Kade put his own hand flat on the partition. It was cold, and thicker than it looked. He felt something else, faintly, the thing he was not supposed to reach for. The partition and its frame and the floor it stood on were carrying load together, and he could feel where the load went. He knew what he had done to a floor in November with less than this. He also knew what the floor had cost, and how little he had aimed it. Four people stood in this sealed room with him and a fifth was a hand's width from the barrier. His head would go white and his hands would shake, and the steel would come down wherever it liked. He took his hand away. Nobody saw him decide it, and he found that he did not mind.

The wall panel on Taren's side lit white. It was not an alarm. It was a call coming in.

Taren stepped back from it. It connected anyway.

"Cadet Holt," a woman said.

Her voice was low and exact, and not at all surprised. Taren had gone rigid.

"Your observation status remains recoverable," she said. "Do not touch the partition controls. Proceed north to the staff stair."

A route came up on the panel in green. It led away from them.

"A security team will take custody of the students. You will not be identified in the incident report."

Taren looked through the barrier. Sera still had one hand on it. Mara had moved in front of the dead terminal, between the camera and the screen. Eli had the slate hugged to his chest. Kade stood and waited, and did not help him.

Everything Taren had was on the wall in one green line: his access, his cover, and whatever future his family had built for him. He only had to walk.

"Was this the assignment?" Taren said.

There was a short pause. "This is containment of an unauthorized disclosure."

"You assigned me to Mercer. Was this where it was always going to end?"

"You were assigned to report instability around Mercer. He has entered a restricted collection. The concern is validated."

So there it was, in a stranger's voice. He had not been a friend. He had been an *observation status*. Somebody had given Taren a category for sitting near him before the two of them had ever shared a table.

"He came in because I offered him the way," Taren said.

"Then your corrective obligation is clear."

"You mean leave them in there."

"I mean that you should not compound an error out of personal attachment."

There was no anger in how she said the last two words. Kade thought anger would have been easier to hear.

Taren's hand came up to the panel. For one second Kade believed he was going to take the route, and was surprised at how badly he did not want to watch it.

Taren put his thumb on the call icon. "My attachment isn't your error to correct."

He ended the call. The green line went out, and the panel turned red. FIELD IDENTITY REVIEW INITIATED. Under it was his name, and as Kade watched, the first line of the name grayed and was gone.

Then he turned to face them, and no joke arrived to make it smaller.

"That was them," Mara said. "The Directorate."

"Yes."

Kade waited for his own anger. He had plenty owing. Nothing came, and the absence frightened him more than the red light did.

"How long have you been reporting on me?"

"Since before you got here."

Eli's arms tightened on the slate.

Kade thought of his first night, and a message from a stranger on a roof. *The face is correct. Keep it. Come anyway.* He had thought it was the funniest thing anybody had said to him in a year. "How? How does someone get assigned to a person who hasn't arrived?"

Taren breathed in. "I told you about my mother and my uncle. I grew up in rooms where people said *stability* as if it were the only moral word there is. After my entry ranking posted, somebody came to see me. They said I had the right profile. I ranked high enough to move between groups, and I had no famous surname to make anyone careful. And I was good at getting people to talk." His smile had gone brittle. "It turns out being likable is a security skill, if the wrong people write the job description."

"And you said yes," said Sera.

"I was sixteen."

"You're seventeen," said Mara.

"I know. A year didn't improve it." His voice cracked on the edge of the last word. Kade had never heard that happen. "They said Asterion had a classified admission that needed watching. There was to be no sabotage and no pressure, and nothing outside ordinary student life. I only had to report changes. Behavior, friendships, anything odd in training."

"You reported us," Eli said.

Taren made himself look at him. "I reported all of you."

"My models?"

"That they existed. Never what was in them."

"Is that meant to help?"

"No. It isn't."

Kade stepped up until the partition was all there was between them. "You were my friend because somebody told you to be."

"At first."

Kade laughed, once. It was not a good sound. "At first."

Taren put his palm against the barrier. "I thought it was intelligence work. Risk assessment. I thought the Directorate was paranoid and controlling, and mostly harmless, because every adult I knew called it necessary."

"When did you work out that it wasn't?"

"At the House Trial."

"Why then?"

"Because my handler asked me which of you would follow you, if you left Asterion."

Mara went absolutely still. Sera's hands curled.

"That's not a training question," Taren said. "It isn't even a risk question. They were asking about leverage."

"And you kept reporting."

"I did."

"Why?"

"Because if I stopped overnight, they'd know I'd turned, and they'd put someone on you that you didn't know about."

"Convenient," said Sera.

"It was convenient. I'm not going to tell you it wasn't." He did not defend it further. "I started giving them less. I gave them things they could get anywhere. Rank changes, who sat with whom in public, schedules they already held. I thought I could keep them fed while I worked out what they were for."

"You thought you could manage the system from inside it," Kade said.

"You've been a terrible influence."

Kade did not smile. "What did you tell them after I found my father?"

Taren's eyes closed for a second. "That you were angry with Sen."

"And the night I packed? The shuttle gate?"

"That you came back. That went in."

"That I almost didn't?"

"No. I kept that."

Kade stared at him. Taren held it.

"Why?"

"Because that one was yours."

It hurt more than if he had said all of it was false. A fake friend would have been simple. Kade could have carried a fake friend out of here like a bag. He had no category for this.

The speaker in the reading room came on with a flat recorded voice. "Security response inbound. Remain in place."

"How inbound?" said Eli.

Taren read something on his side. "Under ninety seconds."

"Then open this," said Sera.

"I can't until the security cycle resets. When it does, there's an override window. It lasts eight seconds. I can use it once."

"And then what?"

"And then my access burns. All of it."

"Your Directorate access," said Mara. "If you use it, they'll know you chose."

"They'll know exactly."

Kade's anger arrived then. It had not gone anywhere. It moved over just far enough to let in one more fact. Taren could walk north. The green line was gone, but the staff stair was still there. The voice had called him recoverable, and he could still recover. He was standing at a red panel watching his own name come apart, and he was waiting for a clock so that he could finish the job.

"Why?" said Sera.

Taren looked at them, one at a time. "Because it stopped being an assignment a long time ago. I didn't tell anyone. That part was me."

A counter appeared above the doorway. SECURITY CYCLE, and twelve seconds.

"When this lifts, run east," Taren said. "Not west. The west lift locks on any isolation."

"How do you know?" said Eli.

Taren's face twisted. "Because I helped write the response map."

Kade thought of every useful warning Taren had given him, and every right coffee. None of it was necessarily false. He would have to go back through four months and test each piece, and that was worse than having it all be lies.

Five. Four. Three. Taren laid his palm on the panel. Two. One.

The partition went up into the ceiling.

Sera was through first, then Mara. Eli tore the bridge cable out and came after them with the slate. Kade was last. Taren stood at the open panel with his credential in his hand, and the credential was flashing red. CREDENTIAL REVOKED.

Kade stopped. He could spare half a second, and he spent it.

"I'm sorry," Taren said.

There were boots somewhere in the clean cold corridor, a long way off and coming. Kade thought of fourteen seconds on the composite, with Taren shouting that he'd been abandoned. He thought of coffee turning up after Taren had vanished for nine minutes, and he wondered now what the nine minutes had been for. The gate had been kept back. Other things had not.

"I don't know what that means yet," he said.

Taren nodded. "That's fair."

Then they ran.

---

Nobody had designed the eastern service corridor for five people at a sprint. Pipes crowded the ceiling. Equipment carts pinched the turns. The red light took the depth out of everything, so that every doorway looked as if somebody stood in it.

Taren led. Sera ran at his shoulder, near enough to take hold of him if leading turned into leaving.

At the first junction he went left.

"You said east," Mara said.

"This is east."

"It is north. I know where the sea is."

"The archive grid's rotated against the campus."

Eli was running with the slate held up in front of his face, which should not have been possible. "He's right. Twenty degrees. I hate it."

Sera looked as if she hated it more.

The corridor ended in a service hatch. Taren put his credential to the plate, and nothing happened at all. Kade saw his face change.

"Problem?"

"I'm dead faster than I thought I'd be."

The boots were nearer. Sera dropped back to the corner and looked round it. "Give me an alternative. Now."

"Eli. There's a manual bus under the plate housing. Two contacts, top left."

Eli was on his knees with the cover off. "You know this model?"

"I know the Directorate retrofit."

"That is a deeply upsetting phrase."

"Later."

Eli bridged the contacts with the metal edge of a map tab. The hatch gave. They went through it into a tunnel that smelled of wet iron, and Sera pulled it shut and leaned on it.

The boots went past on the other side. They did not slow down.

For a while there was nothing but five people breathing. Kade's heart was going hard. His hands were steady and his head was clear. He noticed that, and knew he had chosen it, back at the partition.

Taren had his back against the wall. Sera came away from the hatch and stood in front of him. He lifted his chin.

"You reserved the right," he said.

She hit him. It was one slap with an open hand across the face. It was not hard enough to injure him, and it was hard enough to turn his head. Eli flinched. Mara did not.

Taren touched his cheek. "Reasonable."

Sera's eyes were wet, which was worse than anything she could have done with her hands. "You sat at our table."

"I did."

"You sat next to him the night he came back from that gate."

"I did. And I wrote down that he'd come back."

"You knew by then what they wanted it for."

"I knew."

She stepped away from him before she could do it again.

"Who's your handler?" Kade said. "The voice."

Taren hesitated. Kade could see what it cost him to stop hesitating. "Marisol Crane."

It meant nothing to Kade. It meant something to Mara. "Strategic analysis. She used to give public testimony."

"She used to. She doesn't appear anywhere now."

Taren reached under his overshirt. Sera's weight came forward, and he stopped with his hand where she could see it.

"There's a Directorate wafer behind my student credential."

"Take the whole thing out by the edge," Sera said. "Slowly."

He did. It looked like anybody's credential. He pressed one corner, and a second layer slid out from behind the academy seal, black and as thin as a fingernail. He put both pieces down on the tunnel floor and backed away from them.

Eli crouched over them and did not touch. "Is it transmitting?"

"Not since the revocation. It holds contact tokens and cached instructions."

"Does it hold reports?" said Kade.

Taren looked at the black sliver. "Submission headers. And any drafts that were never acknowledged."

"How many?"

"Three."

A number was harder to hear than *some* would have been. Kade kept his voice level. "About what?"

"My Midyear update on you. The night you came back from the gate. And today."

"Were they sent?" said Mara.

"The first two were asked for, and I sent summaries with nothing attached. Today's never went."

"You drew that line yourself," Kade said. "We never agreed to it."

"No. I decided for all of you."

Eli had an antistatic sleeve out of his tablet case. "If I shield it, can they wipe it from outside?"

"They shouldn't be able to."

"Shouldn't."

"I didn't design this one."

Eli lifted the wafer with two plastic map tabs and slid it into the sleeve. As the seal closed, the dead credential on the floor flashed once more. NETWORK UNAVAILABLE. Then it went dark for good.

Mara held out her hand, and Eli gave her the sleeve. He did not give it to Kade. Kade felt the small unreasonable pull of wanting it, and let it pass. Being the subject of the reports did not make them his. There were three other people in those headers.

Mara checked the seal by the light of Eli's slate. "Sen receives this with all of us in the room. We preserve the headers and the drafts. Nobody opens it alone."

"There are phrases in the headers that map to priority codes," Taren said. "I can translate them."

"You can offer," said Sera. "You don't touch it again."

"Understood."

Kade studied him. Handing it over did not prove Taren honest. It proved that he had taken one hidden thing and put it where other people could look at it. That was all, and for tonight Kade was prepared to count it as a change.

"What does Crane want?"

"Consensus."

His mouth went dry. He had learned the word a quarter of an hour ago, off a file header. "She uses that name."

"She used it to me before I'd ever seen it written down."

"How much does she know?"

"I don't know."

"Don't tell me you don't know when you mean you won't say."

Taren looked straight at him. "I really don't know."

Kade believed him. That was getting to be a problem of its own.

"She thinks the old program failed because the command node was weak," Taren said.

"The command node was a person," said Eli.

"It was a person they treated as equipment."

"And her fix is a stronger person?"

"Stronger, and more controllable."

One operator and eleven instruments, with a tolerance band for how much of each could go missing. It was the exact reverse of everything Meridian had taught Kade by accident.

"What does she think I am?" he said.

Taren swallowed. "A possible command node."

Kade's stomach turned over.

"He can't even do it on purpose," Mara said sharply. "He's produced a Boundary event twice, both times unplanned, and the second one put him under restriction."

"They don't care what he can do this year. They plan in decades." Taren looked at Kade. "They care about what Zero meant last time."

It had meant Aaron, in a ring of twelve for eleven point four seconds, with the machine printing CATEGORY INVALID beside his name.

"We need Sen," Kade said.

"We do," said Taren.

Sera turned on him. "You don't get to say *we*. Not automatically."

He flinched. "No. I haven't earned it."

Kade hated the sentence. It was true, and Taren deserved it. He also hated the part of himself that wanted to call Taren a friend or an enemy and put him in a box with a lid. He had spent a year learning that the hard part of any system was where you drew the line around it. People kept turning out to be the same kind of problem.

---

The tunnel let them out through a plant-room door at the back of the eastern engineering wing. The lights were white again. At the far end of the corridor students went by with bags and cups, and nobody looked twice at anything.

Taren stopped before he stepped out into view.

Sera checked both ways and turned back to him. "Until Sen has that sleeve in her hand, these are the rules. You stay in the middle of the group. You don't touch a terminal and you don't send a message. If you recognize a route, a camera or a face, you say so before it matters."

Taren took out his personal tablet and held it toward her by one edge.

She did not take it. "Eli."

Eli took it and powered it down. "I'll need to pull him off the House schedule as well. And the training archive. And the room controls."

Taren's jaw tightened. "Do it."

"You don't get credit for allowing something I can do anyway."

"Then I won't ask for any."

Sera looked at Kade. "And you don't meet with him alone."

Taren kept his eyes on the dark tablet in Eli's hand.

Kade could have refused. The watching had been about him. He could have said that made it his call which dangers counted. He knew how good that would feel, and he knew what it was. It was taking an injury and cashing it in for authority. He had read a memorandum tonight by people who were very comfortable doing that.

"Agreed," he said.

Mara moved the sleeve to an inside pocket. "These are precautions. They're not a verdict. Sen gets the evidence, and Taren gets to answer it. Nobody gets to make the uncertainty convenient."

"That includes whatever the Directorate says happened tonight," Taren said.

"It includes whatever you say," said Sera.

He bowed his head to that.

Down the corridor a Kepler boy Kade half knew saw Taren and lifted a hand, the beginning of an ordinary hello. Taren's hand began to rise to answer it. Then the boy took in the rest. There were four people standing around Taren, and none of them was smiling, and Eli was holding a tablet that was not his. The boy's hand came down and he walked on.

Taren watched him go. It was the first thing the night had cost him that other people could see.

"They know," he said. "My access is gone. As of tonight I'm no use to them."

"Good," said Kade.

"I doubt that's how they'll put it."

"Do you have another way to reach Crane?" said Mara.

"Through my family."

"Will you use it?"

"Not before Sen has seen that wafer."

"Why should we believe you?" said Sera.

The last of the smile went out of his face. "You shouldn't."

He was not asking for trust. Kade felt an odd relief in it. Taren had spent four months collecting trust on false terms. If anything real was left under that, it could not be paid for out of the old account. It would have to start where Kade had started, at the bottom of a ladder, with a zero beside its name.

"Sen first," Kade said.

Eli checked the time. "Six minutes to her nearest secure room, if internal transit's running."

Six minutes was not long. Kade settled the strap of his case on his shoulder. Taren stepped into the middle of the group, where Sera had told him to be, and not beside Kade, where he had walked since October.

Kade had not forgiven him and did not trust him. He had not left him behind either. It was not much of a category, but it was the only one Kade could stand in tonight.

They set off toward engineering transit together.
