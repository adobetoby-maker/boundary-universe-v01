Kade's plan lasted eleven minutes. Then he did the thing he had said he would do.

Faraday's captain came for him herself. He did not reach for the thing under the verbs; the card in his jacket and a doctor in a corner chair said the same word, and he had learned to say it to himself. What he had instead was Rehab Two, and a woman who wanted him more than she wanted the board. He walked her out of the square and down the south corridor with her a step behind and gaining, and behind them Eli had the east sequence three layers from done and nobody near him, and it was working. It worked for one second, and two, and three, the whole floor bent around a person who was worth nothing. His body told him this was the moment to leave it. He did not leave it. He held. Four.

The beat he spent holding was the beat the respawned striker needed. Faraday had put its first token into the woman Sera had eliminated, and she came out of the south pocket where the respawn had placed her, and she did not waste the rematch on a word. Her shoulder hit his chest. A Redirection pulse took the floor out from under his rear foot, and he went into the safety field and through it, and it sealed behind him with a sound like a held breath, and he was standing in the observation bay behind clear glass, watching his own plan go on without him.

The old instinct got there first. Elimination was failure. The board agreed; Meridian slid from third to fifth in the live projection while he watched. One token left. His channel had gone to nothing under the elimination rules.

A prompt opened on the glass in front of him. RESPAWN ELIGIBLE. Ten seconds.

Mara had ten seconds to spend the last token on him. Nine. She sent Priya left. Eight. She moved Taren toward the north gate. Seven. No call came, and he understood why before the count ran out. Bringing him back would spend the fallback they still needed if Nessa or Eli went down, and he was not the most useful person to return. That was the arithmetic. He had read it aloud off the back of a clipboard on Sunday night, and it had sounded fine then. It stung, and Mara was right, and he stood there holding both.

The prompt went out.

From behind the glass every mistake was obvious and none of them were his to fix. Priya had drifted too far down the south corridor. Anton had let a Curie fighter get a barrier's length between him and Nessa. Kade's hands found the rail. Mara never once looked at the bay. Good.

Then Faraday got to Eli.

Not around the console; over it. The captain came back from the south corridor where Kade had walked her and went over the top of the console with a heel, and the protection on a systems operator did not cover an operator who let go, and Eli let go, because the heel was coming down where his fingers were. The sequence collapsed. The east gate went dark. Sera arrived between them and took the next kick on both crossed forearms, and Kade watched her mouth make one word.

*Run.*

Eli ran. Not away from the fight. West, down the arm, toward the port sunk in the wall beside the west gate, with his tablet already coming out of his jacket. Faraday's second striker went after him. Mara read the route and hauled her own opponent sideways across Sera's line, so that two fights became one tangle in the striker's way, and Sera came free of it and the captain did not. Eli reached the port, went down on both knees the way the mat had taught him, and drove the tablet into the dock.

The west gate opened, and the ring inside it lit.

Every House on the floor turned its head. Nobel, who had been waiting all round for a gate worth the run, went for it with three. Taren was already there. He stepped onto the fresh ring alone, stood on it long enough for it to turn blue, and stepped off, and Nobel's three arrived at a run onto the point he had left and the ring went white under them.

Then the gate closed.

Eli pulled the tablet and the west gate came down behind Nobel with the track barrier already sliding in to meet it, and the ring under three Nobel fighters went dark. The west corridor sealed with three people inside it and nothing to stand on, and Eli looked up from the dock at the observation glass, and Kade could not hear him through the barrier and did not need to. His face said they had won wrong again.

Faraday changed its shape in the square, and Mara read it and did not do what he would have done. He would have pulled Priya back to center. Mara sent her wide, north, behind a barrier that was still moving, and Priya came out at Nessa's shoulder at the exact moment the Curie fighter committed. Nessa said three words, and the fighter's left knee came and Priya took it on her shin guard, and the right shoulder came and Priya turned it past her, and Nessa was through the gap and on the zone. It was not the call he would have made. It was a better one.

Anton stood in the north gate for Nessa with the brace lighting amber every time Curie's defender hit him. He gave up ground a finger's width at a time and never gave up the opening. Nessa reached the zone behind him and said, "Track, two," and Anton let go on one, and the wall came across between them and carried the Curie defender out of the lane like a broom.

The live board changed faster than Kade could follow it. Fifth. Fourth. Third. The last token sat in Mara's hand, unspent, and stayed there.

The pride came up before he could get anything in front of it. Not that the thing he had built had held. That it had stopped being his and held anyway.

The horn. Meridian had qualified for the final. Third overall.

The stands over the observation bay shook with it. He looked up at thousands of blue-painted faces making one noise, and for the first time the bell made sense to him. A feeling this size needed something that size to be struck, and a voice was not enough.

---

The Commons came apart that night.

Kade stood at the edge of it with one hand on the stair rail, which was where the old habit had put him. Near the door. Ready to be the first one gone.

Sera arrived beside him with two cups and gave him one, which occupied the hand. "You walked Faraday's captain forty meters today with nothing in your hands. A banister can manage without you."

Eli had a chair on its side and was carrying it across the Commons with two second-years in front of him calling barrier sweeps that did not exist. Taren had a circle around him and was explaining, with diagrams, that his fourteen seconds had been the tactical center of the entire day. Anton and Nessa were shouting about whether the west gate should have stayed open two seconds longer, and Nessa had her eyes closed and was telling him his diagram was wrong.

He took his hand off the rail. Eli saw it and pointed at him with the chair leg. "Witness. Tell them who carried this House out of Round Two."

"Sera. Mara. Anton, until he said so. And a technician, for eight seconds, complaining."

"*Narrating*," Eli said. "It's a skill."

The room laughed, and he laughed with it, and did not once look at the stairs.

Later, when the noise had thinned to people asleep on furniture, he found Mara in the plan room with the field turning above the table and three of tomorrow's contingencies already changed in her hand. Taren's column of numbers was still pinned to the wall. Somebody had written a *3* at the bottom of it in a different pen.

"You've come down to say something," she said, without looking up. "Say it before you improve it."

He leaned on the table. "The token. You had ten seconds to spend it on me. You didn't. Then you never spent it at all."

"Nobody needed it. Eli needed a door, and he had one."

"I know." He made himself finish it before he could make it smaller. "I stood behind that glass and watched you not buy me back, and it was right, and I hated it. Then I watched Eli run forty meters to a port with the token still in your hand, and the board went up without me on it. I'd like that to be the thing I remember, and not the glass."

Mara set down the stylus. Something in her face eased by a degree, which on Mara was a great deal.

"You wrote yourself back in at the second rotation," she said. "On Sunday. I crossed it out. Tonight you're telling me the crossed-out version was better. Do you know how many people on this island would say that out loud?"

"You'd say it."

"I'd say it about somebody else." She picked the stylus back up. "That's why I'm telling you it counts."

Through the high window the bell hung in its frame over the dark courtyard, fourteen months silent and whatever days Taren had called that morning.

Mara followed his eyes. "Tomorrow."

"You said that like it's already happened."

"I said it like a row on a clipboard." She turned the field a quarter and pointed the stylus at the seventh line, where his name was, and where she had written beside it, in her own hand, *if present*. "Be present. I've left you things to say."
