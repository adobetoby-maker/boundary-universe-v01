# Chapter 15 — House Trial: Final

*Forty-one. Mom says the final's on the big screen in the hospital break room. I said you'd hate that. She said you'd hate it and check the score anyway, which is a different thing. Which is it. Send the number.*

Taren called Bell Watch at seven, and for the first time since autumn he stopped at the months.

"Fourteen," he told the Commons glass, and the House, in every state of dressed, waited for the days. "You're not getting the days. Days are a number, and I've decided I don't hold with numbers before a final. Tonight it's fourteen months and something, or it's nothing at all, and I'd like everyone to eat breakfast inside that uncertainty."

"That's the worst one you've done," Sera said from the stairs.

"It was short."

"That's why it's the worst. I could hear you trying."

Kade ate. Eating was a thing he could do with his hands that did not involve checking anything, and he had learned on Saturday that the hour before a floor was best spent being a body and not a plan. Across the table Anton was fitting two new seals to the brace along his spine, one at the hip and one under the shoulder blade, with the concentration of a man threading a needle. The old seals lay beside his plate in their paper, amber-stained. He had not thrown them away. Kade understood that. You kept the receipt.

Mara came down last with a crease down one cheek from the rule appendix and her tablet already open, and sat, and did not explain the crease, and nobody asked her to.

"Ten o'clock," she said. "Floor walk. Twenty minutes, four Houses, inspection mode. We walk it and touch nothing."

"Then what."

"Then you read me the back of the clipboard on the actual floor, and we find out whether it's still true when the floor's real."

---

The final field was bigger than anything they had stood on Saturday, and empty, it looked like a machine somebody had taken apart to clean.

Kade walked it with the clipboard under his arm and let his eyes do the thing they did. The lower level was a broad ring road around the outside of the arena, and at its four compass points stood the posts, waist-high pillars in rings of white light that would pay one point every twenty seconds to whichever House had a body inside the ring. Four ramps climbed inward from the ring road to the crown, a raised inner platform with a scoring circle at its middle worth two. Above the crown, three transparent platforms stepped unevenly up around a column of pale light that the rules called the relay core and Eli called the entire reason he had put his name in a row. Barrier slabs sat in tracks on every level, waiting to turn a quarter every thirty seconds. Under the ring road, the service tunnels ran, with their mouths open, so that any eliminated fighter could leave without crossing live floor, and anyone still in it had blind corners at their heels.

Atlas walked it in green on the far side, seven of them, in a line behind their captain. Kepler in white had split up to measure things. Horizon in red stood on the crown arguing.

"Stop for a body," Eli said. He had a paragraph from the appendix taped to the inside of his forearm sleeve and was reading off it as he walked. "The slabs. They're not allowed to close on a person. Anything in the track stops the slab. It says here the stop is 'transmitted to the obstruction.'"

"Meaning it hurts," Sera said.

"Meaning it hurts. But it stops."

Nessa had gone up the crown ramp ahead of them and was standing at the foot of the lowest of the three platforms, the one that was barely more than a step above the crown, looking up at the two higher ones with dislike.

"Every reader on this floor is going to be up there," she said, when Kade reached her. "Highest platform, best view. Best view of ten thousand people." She put her palm flat on the low platform's edge. "This one. I'm two meters off the crown and I've got the ring road through the gaps. I read through two live fields and sign my name. Through the whole arena I'm reading weather again."

"You'll be in reach of anyone on the crown."

"So put something in front of me." She said it without heat. "You're always trying to keep me safe by putting me somewhere I can't see. Put me where I can see and let somebody stand in the way."

Anton, who had climbed the ramp behind them without being asked, looked at the space in front of the low platform, and at the ramp head beside it, and nodded once, and that was that assignment made.

Eli was at the core. He could not touch it, so he stood with his hands behind his back like a boy at a museum, and told them what it did, and Kade listened to the version that had grown since Saturday night.

"The core pays nothing. Nobody argues about it because it pays nothing. A systems operator docks and runs a command sequence, and a command turns a slab or opens a gate, and that's ordinary, every House has one. But if the sequence finishes without the operator being touched, the floor changes what it counts. Coupled scoring." He was glowing, faintly, under the paint of not having slept. "Opposite posts double each other while you hold both. Hold the crown as well and the next command's free. Drop either post and it all resets. Every House knows that much. The paragraph nobody reads is on my arm." He held out the sleeve. "During coupled scoring, two active fighters from the same House who touch the core at the same moment score five. Once. The core stops working for anyone afterward. Which means nobody's ever seen it done, because the first House to do it teaches the whole floor the rule."

"So we hold it," Mara said.

"We hold it for the last second or we don't spend it."

Kade looked up the column of light and then down at the crown circle and the four ramps, and knew, in the practical part of him that had stood on the tape, that the whole final was two problems. Keep Eli untouched long enough. Then stand where the doubling was.

Taren's band lit while they were coming down the ramp. He turned it in against his forearm and read it and did not put it away.

"Atlas opens with three on the crown and two on the near posts, and their captain roams," he said. "That's their first shape. They ran it in the seed rounds and they'll run it here because it's won them everything."

"That's on the recordings?"

"That's from a friend of a friend."

He smiled the smile that made people forgive him for being right, and Kade put the message in the file, behind the eleven seconds and the three lights, and disliked the way the file had started to look like a shape. Then Taren fell into step with him along the ring road and asked the other thing, in the tone of a man working a problem for the team.

"When you go out. Not if, we're all agreed it's when. Who do they look at?"

"Mara."

"That's who they should look at. Is it who they will?"

Kade thought about the glass on Saturday, and Mara not once turning her head to it. "Yes."

"Good. That's what I'd have said too." Taren clapped him on the shoulder and went to look at the west post. It was a reasonable question. It was the question. Kade did not put it in the file.

At the foot of the north ramp he stopped the seven of them and read the back of the clipboard aloud, on the real floor, with Atlas watching from across the ring road and not able to hear.

It had no moves on it. It had names. Barrier calls, Nessa, and three words after every one. Switch, anyone carrying anything, still. Whether to leave a post, whoever was standing on it, and nobody argued. The core, Eli, and if Eli said leave him, they left him. Anything after Mercer was out, Vey, and anything Mercer said after he was out, nobody could hear, so that was settled by rule. The opening was the only part with a shape in it, and the shape was this. Meridian would not go near Atlas. Meridian would fight Kepler for the west, and Horizon for the east, for zones Atlas did not want, and stay close on the board, and give the first House on the floor nothing strange to look at for as long as it took Atlas to get bored and come and find them.

"And when they come," Priya said.

"Then they've walked away from what they were holding, and Eli's been docked for a minute nobody was watching him."

Mara took the clipboard from him and read it once, the way she had on Sunday night, and turned it over, and this time did not write anything.

"It's still true," she said, and gave it back.

---

The House was in the courtyard when they came down at noon, and the bell rope was on the ground.

Somebody had unhooked it from the cleat on the frame and laid it out across the flagstones in a straight line, all forty feet of old braided cord, from the foot of the black steel frame to the courtyard arch. Two hundred Meridian students stood along both sides of it and left it alone. Nobody was chanting. A first-year near the arch was crying already and being pretended not to be noticed.

"What's the rope doing," Eli said.

A fourth-year at the frame end answered him, a broad woman with the House crest inked on the back of her hand. "It stays down until the eight of you come back through that arch. Nobody touches it, nobody steps on it, nobody hooks it up. It's not about winning. It went down for the qualifiers three years ago too, and they lost, and it stayed down till they were home, and then it went back on the hook and nobody rang it." She looked at the eight of them the way Sera looked at a capsule. "If you win, the last pull's yours. That's the only difference."

Kade looked down the length of the rope to the arch. It was frayed at the frame end where hands had held it for however many years and gone smooth at the middle where nobody's had. Fourteen months on a hook. He wanted to hear it with a want that had stopped being embarrassing somewhere on Saturday, and standing there he let himself know that.

They walked the line of it, one side of it, eight people in blue, and the House did not touch them either. Taren, who could not go ten steps in silence, went ten steps in silence and then thirty. Priya's calf was taped under the uniform and she was walking as though it were not.

Anton's mother was at the arch. Family access ended there, and she stood exactly at it, a short woman with Anton's shoulders and a coat too warm for the day, and Anton stepped out of the line to her and bent, and she put both hands on the sides of his face and said something into it Kade could not hear. When he came back into the line his seals were perfect and his face was not, and he said, to nobody, "She said do the standing-still thing. She's watched every feed. She says the standing still is the part that's me."

"It is," Sera said.

At the arch Mara turned around. Two hundred faces, and the rope on the stones between them.

"We'll come back through," she said. She did not say win. Kade heard the difference and respected her for it, and then heard the second thing under it, which was that she had said we and meant eight, and had not looked at him when she said it, because she had not needed to check.

---

Ten thousand people had a color, and when Meridian came in under the east gate the blue third of them stood up, and the sound had mass.

His heart did not know sound from force. He had told it on the walk in that it would not, and it did not care what he had told it. Mara was on his left, where she had been on the ring road, and she did not tell him to breathe.

"First name you're going to say," she said.

"Priya."

"Then you're fine. Say it and get out of the way."

The floor settled into its opening shape around them, and he traced it once more, faster, the way he had traced the tape. West post, Priya, with Kepler's white nearest her. East post, Taren, with Horizon's red. South post, nobody, and that was deliberate. The crown, Mara, at the near edge of the circle, and across from her three Atlas green already arranged exactly as a friend of a friend had said they would be. The low platform, Nessa, with Anton planted at the ramp head in front of her like a post the arena had forgotten to light. The core, Eli, with Sera a step out from him and her hands loose. And Mercer, tactical flex, standing at the foot of the north ramp where he could reach the crown or the west post in five seconds and neither of them in four.

A steward read the safety rules. Somewhere on the rail a faculty hand rested on the panel wired to one word on Kade's band, and there was nothing under the verbs on this floor, and he had stopped needing to tell himself so. The count came down on the big display. Five. Four. On three he looked at the seven of them, and not one of them was looking back at him to see whether it was all right to begin. Two. One.

"Priya."

She was gone on the horn. Kepler's pair on the west post had set up to meet a run, and she did not run at them. She ran away from them, the long way, along the outside of the ring road with the crowd on her right, and the two of them looked at each other and went after her, and Nessa said, "West slab, four, high," and at four the slab on the west track turned its quarter and put a wall across the ring road between Priya and the two people chasing her. Priya came around the far side of the wall at a jog and stepped into the west ring alone. It went blue. Kepler's pair stood on the wrong side of a slab, and the blue third of the arena told them so.

"That's one," Sera said.

"That's one," Kade said, and stepped back out of the way of his own plan.

Atlas did not come. Atlas took the crown circle at the horn, three of them, and the two nearer posts, and their captain walked the ring road between them like a woman checking fences, and let Kepler and Horizon come to her. She was tall, a third-year, and she fought the way Julian did, in that she did not fight until it paid. Kade watched her put a Horizon striker over the line with one movement he did not see the start of, and revised his idea of how long Meridian could hide.

Taren held east by talking. He always held things by talking. Horizon's speed pair came at the east post from both sides, and Taren stood in the ring with his hands up and said, out loud, "The one on the left is the good one, everybody knows it," and the one on the right hesitated to be offended, and Taren stepped into the second of hesitation and put a shoulder into the left one's field just hard enough to carry the man's own momentum outside the ring. The tick chimed. Blue.

"Predictable," Taren said on the channel.

"Painfully," Kade said, and meant it as praise. A House watching that would decide Meridian's screen was a mouth, and file it, and be right, and it would not help them.

At two minutes the board said Atlas fourteen, Kepler nine, Meridian nine, Horizon six, and Kade had said one name.

Then the captain did the thing he had not planned for. She did not come for Meridian. She sent one green uniform to stand near him. Not at him. Near. Wherever Kade walked on the ring road, an Atlas fighter walked four meters off his shoulder and did nothing, and Kade understood in about ten seconds what he was for. He was a beacon. Every House on the island had watched the qualifiers and drawn the conclusion that the Meridian plan lived in the boy with the red bar, and so wherever the boy went, Atlas had one more body already there, waiting for the plan to arrive.

"I've got a shadow," he said.

"Seen," Mara said. "Is it good?"

"It's patient." He kept walking. "It's going to be where I am. So let's decide where I am."

He walked north, up the ring road away from everything, into a stretch that had no post and would have no slab turn for another twenty seconds, and the shadow walked with him, and for those twenty seconds Atlas was fighting with six. Then he walked south, to the empty post, and stood in its ring, and the shadow came to stand four meters from him, and Kepler's runner, who had been about to take the empty south post for free, found a green uniform standing beside it and thought better. Every place Kade stood became a place Atlas could not use. He was not scoring. He was spending Atlas's seventh fighter on a boy who was worth nothing on the board, and it was the first time in his life being worth nothing had felt like a tool in his hand.

"They'll pull it," Sera said.

"When they pull it, they've noticed. That's what we want."

Horizon spent a token at four minutes and the whole upper level turned at once. The north ramp swung a quarter and stopped, and it was no longer the ramp beside Anton; it was a lane, running straight from the ring road up to the foot of the low platform where Nessa stood with her eyes moving and her feet still. Three Kepler fighters were on it before it had finished turning.

"Lane's open on me," Nessa said. Flat. "Three. High."

Anton was already moving. He did not go up the lane to meet them. He went down it, to the ring road end, and planted in its mouth with his field up, and the first Kepler fighter hit the field the way a man hits a door he thought was open. Anton's boots went back half a meter across the composite and the brace warning at his hip went yellow. He held. The second fighter piled into the first. Nessa stepped down off the platform behind him, put her hand on his shoulder blade going past, and was on the crown and into the blue of Mara's guard before the third one had worked out there was a second way around.

"Brace," Sera said.

"Yellow."

"That's a color. Give me a sentence."

"It'll take one more like that. Not two."

And that was where Kade watched Sera make her choice. She was a step off Eli. Eli was two commands from the end of a sequence and there was a Horizon striker coming across the crown toward him, and Anton was in a lane mouth with three Kepler fighters on the far side of his field and one block left, and Sera's whole body was already turned toward Anton. He saw her see it. He saw her turn back to Eli, and set her feet, and take the Horizon striker on her forearms, because that was the row she had written her own name in, and the person who most wanted to go and lift people out of trouble had put herself over the one person the plan could not do without.

He opened the channel to change it. To send her, and take Eli himself, and give her the rescue.

"I've got the one I've got," Sera said, before he had made a sound, without looking at him. "Nessa. He needs out."

"Slab on the lane in two," Nessa said. "Anton, back on one. High."

Anton stepped back on one. The slab that had opened the lane turned again and closed it, with three Kepler fighters inside it and Anton outside, and Anton walked back to the ramp head and stood where he had been standing, and the brace stayed yellow. Kade closed the channel with nothing said into it. On Saturday, from the tape, that had been a theory about fallback. Now it was two people solving a thing between them while he kept his mouth shut, and it had been faster than anything he could have said.

Atlas's captain pulled the shadow at five minutes.

He felt it go, the way you feel a draft stop. The green uniform peeled off his shoulder and went for the crown, and a second Atlas fighter came off the near post with it, and the third came around the far side of the circle, and the shape of it was three on Mara, who was holding the crown alone.

He was on the north ramp, six meters below her. He had one word, and it was her name, and he did not have to say what came after it. She turned her head a fraction toward him as the three came in, and he pointed, not at her and not at them, at the west ramp behind her where the Kepler fighter she had not seen was coming up toward Eli, and she was already moving. She left the crown. She went down the west ramp backward, three steps, and met the Kepler fighter on the fourth with her shoulder low and turned him into the rail, and he went over it into the safety field and was gone.

Which left the crown circle empty, with three Atlas fighters arriving in it, and the tick due in three seconds, and Eli one command from done.

"Kade," Mara said.

He went. Up the last of the north ramp and into the circle, into a ring with three green uniforms in it and himself, one boy worth nothing, and stood, inside the line, with his hands open. He did not fight. He was not there to fight. He was there to be a Meridian body inside the crown when the tick chimed, so that the circle did not go green, so that when Eli's last command landed there would be something for it to couple to. Three seconds. The captain looked at him and understood what he was doing and understood in the same moment that putting him over the line would take longer than the tick, and she went past him for the west ramp instead, for Mara, for Eli.

Two. One. The tick chimed and the crown was still blue.

Eli's last command landed.

The core changed color from pale to gold, and the board said it in letters he did not have to read.

COUPLED SCORING ACTIVE.

"Now," he said, and did not say to whom, because he did not need to, and Meridian moved like eight people who had seen the same floor at the same time. Mara came back up the ramp and took the crown from the captain's flank with the bruise on her forearm gone dark under the light. Priya came off the west post through the service tunnel and up out of the east mouth, and Taren, who had been holding east, went down the same tunnel the other way, so that Kepler's slow pair at west found the fastest person on the floor standing in the ring they had come to take back, and Horizon's speed pair at east found a man who would not stop talking. Anton filled the first hole he saw. Nessa called slabs before the lights changed. Sera stood over Eli. Kade went where Meridian was thinnest and stood there.

West and east both blue through one interval. Doubled. The crown blue. The next command free, and Eli spent it on a slab that shut Atlas's captain on the wrong side of the crown for eight seconds. For ninety seconds Meridian scored faster than the other three Houses combined, and the blue third of the arena stopped being seats and became a wall of sound.

Anton's last block was not a block.

Horizon's pair had given up on Taren and gone for Priya at the west post, and the west slab was turning to shut her in with them, and Nessa said, "West slab, closing, two, high," and Anton was there, at the track. He did not put his field up against a fighter. He stepped into the slab's track and put his field up against the slab, and the slab did what the appendix on Eli's sleeve said it would do. It stopped. The stop went through whatever was in the way, and what was in the way was a brace with one block left. The seal at his hip went from yellow to amber to red in the time it took Priya to come through the gap he had held open. Then he stepped out of the track before the seal failed, and the slab finished its turn behind him, and he walked back to his ramp head.

"That was one," Sera said on the channel.

"That was it," Anton said. "I've got none."

"Then stand somewhere and be a wall. Walls don't block."

Meridian thirty-one. Atlas twenty-six. Kepler twenty-two. Horizon sixteen.

Kade had said four words in three minutes. Two of them were names. It was the best floor he had ever stood on.

Then Atlas stopped chasing points, and came for him properly.

He saw it a beat late, which was the whole story of him on a mat. The shadow came back, and it did not stand four meters off; it came in, and a second green came with it, up the north ramp behind him while he was watching the crown. He slipped the first. The second put a Redirection pulse into his lead hip and turned him toward the ramp rail, and he got his weight back under him, and had one second of being proud of that, and the third came from the side he had turned his back on. Not a strike. A hand flat on his chest, and the rail behind his knees, and the ramp's whole height under him.

He went over backward. He had time to see the crown, and Mara in it, not looking at him, and then the safety field over the service tunnel mouth took him a hand's width from the composite and held him and set him down in the tunnel, and the tone sounded, and his band went to one-way, every call reaching him and his own voice dead by rule.

MERCER, ELIMINATED. Four minutes on the clock.

The blue third of the arena made a sound he would hear again, later, set to music.

---

The observation bay was glass, and his hands found the rail, and for three seconds Meridian did not know what shape it was.

He watched the three seconds. The old part of him stood inside them and took them as proof. The newer part had been expecting the old part and was not impressed, and hated, a little, how good the proof felt.

Then Mara said, "Priya. East platform. Go," and the shape came back.

Not his shape. Atlas had watched five minutes of Meridian's opening and had it by heart, and expected the House to fall back into it without its head, and Mara did not give them the opening. She moved Priya off the west post and up to the east side of the crown, where the plan had never put her, because Atlas's captain had left east thin to chase the boy who was now behind glass. Taren saw why before she had finished saying it and went low to cover the post she had emptied. Sera let the crown go on purpose and let Kepler have it, because Kepler on the crown was Kepler not on Eli. Nessa said, "Crown, three, high," and then, when Mara said, "No, they want us on the ramp, hold," said, "Confirmed. Slab four's late. Hold. High," and did not argue, and the call had changed because the information had.

Anton was a wall. Not an anchor, a wall with legs, standing where Atlas had to spend four seconds walking around him, never once putting his field up. Kade had never put that version of Anton in a plan. He had not known it was there to be put.

The captain looked at the glass once. He understood, watching her look, the mistake she had made. Atlas had read Meridian's coordination as a structure with a head on it, and taken the head, and thereby handed Mara something she had not had while he was on the floor. Not command. Permission to stop protecting a plan and start using seven people she knew better than any plan did.

Pride arrived before the disappointment could, and it hit harder.

Three minutes. Meridian lost west. Anton held the retreat off it for Taren with the seal red and his field down, took one legal strike on the brace and another, and on the third stepped across the elimination line himself, before the seal could fail, and came through the field into the bay beside Kade and turned straight back to the glass.

"Taren got out?"

"He got out."

Anton let a breath go that he had been holding since the slab. His mother, beyond the bay's far glass, had both hands over her mouth.

Two forty. Nessa stayed on the low platform one call past safe, guiding Sera around a turning slab she could not see, and Kepler closed the ramp on both sides of her. She came into the bay with her eyes narrowed to slits and stood beside Anton and went on counting slab intervals under her breath, because the intervals had not stopped and neither had she.

Two minutes. Priya, on the east platform, on a double team, gone. One forty, Eli, who had left the core to reach a port at the ring road and been caught in the open, and turned back long enough to lock one last slab before he went over. He arrived shouting "Worth it," and then stood at the glass and stopped shouting. One minute, Taren, who did not go quietly.

Thirty seconds. The last interval had just chimed, and there would not be another before the horn. Meridian eighty-two, Atlas eighty-four. Two Meridian uniforms on the floor, both of them on the crown, and the captain and two more green holding the ramps between them and anything that could still change the board.

Kade's knuckles found out about the rail.

Mara looked at the board. Then up. Not at the posts, at the column of gold light over the crown with its three stepped platforms around it, and her face did the thing it did on a mat when the second lesson arrived, and Eli, beside him, said, "Oh," very quietly, and Kade did not need to be told.

The core paid nothing. Every House on the floor knew it. Atlas's captain held the crown ramps and let two Meridian fighters climb toward a light that was worth nothing, and Kade watched her let them, and watched the moment, at five seconds, when the paragraph on Eli's sleeve reached her too. Two green uniforms turned and went up the platforms after them. Three seconds. Mara reached the near side of the column. Sera reached the far side and had to go around a rail to do it. Two. The Atlas fighter behind Sera got a hand on her shoulder. One. Mara's palm flat on the plate on her side. Sera's on hers, with a green hand still on her shoulder, and the horn went while their hands were down.

The board did nothing for half a second.

MERIDIAN +5.

FINAL. MERIDIAN 87. ATLAS 84. KEPLER 73. HORIZON 69.

He did not remember the bay door. There was glass, and then there was the floor and Eli's weight on one shoulder and Priya hitting him between the shoulder blades hard enough to restart the match and Taren shouting one word into his ear over and over, which was the number three, and Anton crying and making no effort not to, and Nessa with both hands over her face. Sera hugged him, a whole hug, for exactly one second, and stepped back and said, "No," before he could say anything, and he said, "No," in agreement.

Mara came down off the crown ramp with the bruise gone the color of a storm and her breathing not yet under her.

"You put me in a ring with three of them," she said. "With nothing."

"You put me in one first."

"I put you where you'd be worth nothing to them. That was the point of you."

"So you trusted the point."

She looked at him, and the joke he had ready did not come, and hers did not either. "Yes," she said. One word, and it stayed said.

Then the sound came up through his boots. The arena feed had cut to the Meridian courtyard, and on the screen the rope was off the stones. The fourth-year with the crest on her hand had it, and thirty people behind her, leaning back on it together, and the bell in its black frame swung, and struck. Once. The arena went quiet for it. Twice. Taren stopped saying three. Fourteen months of nothing ended in a note big enough to hold what eight people had done, and Kade stood in it and let it be that big.

---

Medical would not let a winning House off the field for eleven minutes, and Park had the corner chair.

She looked at his eyes when he came in and said, "Number," and heard one, and said, "Hand," and watched him hold it out. It was steady, and then the last two fingers did their small separate thing, and she wrote that down as she had written it down before. "Nothing on the card today."

"Nothing on the card."

"You fell off a ramp."

"Legally."

"Legally," she agreed, and went back to her tablet, and that was as close as Park came to a medal.

The alcove was under the west stands, and the crowd came through the wall as a softened roar, and each time the rope team pulled, the bronze came through the structure and a little dust let go of the vent. Anton sat with his forearms on his knees while a medic cut the outer layer off the brace at his hip. Under the seal the skin had a red line across it the width of a finger where the slab's stop had gone through. His mother was beyond the glass partition, not allowed in, and not moving.

"It held," Anton said.

Sera crouched in front of him. "It held because you stepped out."

"It held for Priya."

"It held for Priya because you stepped out. Say the second half. I want to hear you know it."

He looked at the medic, who was not going to help him. "It held because I stepped out."

"Good. Your back's not more attached to you when the score's good." She stood and went to Priya, who tried to wave her off the calf, and said, "Strain," and Sera said, "That's a word for a category," and Priya said, "You're going to be unbearable when they give you a badge," and Sera said, "Yes," with no pleasure at all and unwrapped the tape.

Nessa was on the floor with her back to the wall and her eyes shut, and she was still counting. Kade sat down beside her, not close.

"They've stopped," he said. "The slabs."

"I know." Her lips kept moving. "I'm counting down. It's the only way I've ever found to stop. If I stop at the top I'm still on the platform." A pause. "Slab four was late. Point three."

"Mara held on it."

The steward came before the eleven minutes were up, a woman with a tablet under one arm and the expression of somebody who has been given a form and a room full of people who would not fit on it.

"I can't do it alone," he said. "Not because I'm being noble. I was behind glass for four minutes of it. Bring the tablet in here or take all of us with you."

The crown swap. Mara leaving the circle backward down the west ramp; a boy worth nothing stepping into it; the tick. The model had put a gold command marker over his trace at the moment he had said her name.

The recalculation ran during the medal ceremony, and his tablet shook in his hand while an official was saying the word tradition.

House Trial did not pay a rank for winning. It paid, the display explained, for survival time, objective contribution, opponent quality, tactical-role weighting, and a team-performance coefficient, and it noted that the correction for MERCER, K. was unusually large because his existing rank had contained almost no high-quality team-event data of any kind. Which was the sentence for it. The number under the sentence was another matter.

PREVIOUS RANK: 8,159. TEAM EVENT RECALCULATION: PLUS 6,255. NEW RANK: 1,904 OF 10,482.

Eli read it over his shoulder and said, "One thousand nine hundred and four," and then, "No. Wait. One, nine, zero, four," as if the digits might behave differently in a different order, and then made a sound Kade had not heard from him before and hoped to hear again.

"It's too big," Kade said.

"It's what's there," Mara said. She had come to stand beside him rather than with the House captains, where she had been called. "The model had nowhere to put you. Now it has somewhere."
