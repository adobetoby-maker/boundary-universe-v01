# Chapter Three: SUCCESSION

## Day 4,372

The continuity review reserved six chairs for people and one for an absence.

Aaron identified the distinction at 06:41, when the conference-room display rendered the authorized attendance around the table. Rhys occupied the head position. Kessler, Amara, Directorate counsel, medical authority, and Aaron filled the remaining assigned seats. The chair to Rhys's right carried no name. A gray placard hovered above it.

**DECLARED PRINCIPAL UNAVAILABLE**

The room belonged to the Geneva facility's oldest secure wing, where limestone outer walls enclosed a later copper isolation cage. The season's heating entered through cast-iron radiators beneath windows permanently opacified by security film. One radiator clicked every forty-three seconds as its valve corrected for a temperature difference the room sensor could not locate. The long walnut table retained two pale circles from equipment bases removed before Aaron joined the program. A channel through its center carried current optical links, isolated power, and enough obsolete connectors to document four generations of institutional confidence.

The empty chair faced the door.

Aaron authenticated the meeting packet. Its title supplied less information than the seating plan.

**CONTROLLED CONTINUITY DOCTRINE**

**FIVE-YEAR RESILIENCE REVIEW: PRINCIPAL UNAVAILABILITY**

The first forty-two pages examined site distribution, supply continuity, independent stop authority, and recovery after loss of central coordination. The forty-third page began the exercise.

*Assume the initiating official cannot authenticate, communicate, delegate, or recover authority for thirty calendar days. Do not assume death, resignation, compromise, or hostile action. Evaluate functions, not cause.*

Rhys's office held seventeen functions inside the relevant compartments. Eleven already possessed named alternates. Four could transfer by concurrence of existing authorities. Two had no valid successor.

Both touched the Refuge Node.

Aaron opened the dependency annex. The document replaced the source identity with **CONTROLLED ORIGIN A** and the direct relationship with **RESTRICTED INTERFACE CONDITION**. Even inside the room, not every attendee held reconstructive access. The abstractions prevented the review from distributing the secret while asking whether the institution could survive the loss of the man who governed it.

One unresolved function authorized changes to the source-operation hold. The other adjudicated conflicts between containment, controlled capacity, and disclosure risk when existing doctrine produced no common answer.

The first required someone who understood the source.

The second required someone empowered to decide what that understanding permitted everyone else to do.

Aaron read the attendance list again.

Rhys entered at 06:58.

He carried no tablet. An assistant Aaron had not seen before brought the document case, placed it beside the named chair, and left without entering the isolation boundary. Rhys removed his coat with his left hand. His right arm followed the movement but did not assist it. He sat before releasing the coat to the adjacent stand.

Two weeks earlier, the same sequence would have registered only because Aaron had spent eight years measuring Rhys for changes the man did not report. This morning nobody at the table could plausibly miss it.

Rhys noticed Aaron's attention and directed his own toward the packet.

“The chair is not mine,” he said.

Aaron had not spoken.

“It represents any principal whose temporary loss changes institutional function. Today the exercise uses my credentials because the five-year review identified them as the largest unresolved dependency.”

“That makes the chair yours for modeling purposes.”

“A distinction counsel will preserve and you will resist.”

Directorate counsel, Sabine Morel, entered behind him. “I can preserve it while Dr. Mercer resists. The categories are compatible.”

Rhys sat with both hands on the closed packet. His right fingers reached full extension one after another.

“Then we may begin on schedule,” he said.

The room clock changed to 07:00.

---

Five years had converted The First Coupling from a negotiated doctrine into infrastructure.

Nine certified sites now operated under its derived standards. Six additional facilities used local-closure specifications without access to the classified doctrine that produced them. Every active Conduit lattice declared its electrical, mechanical, and thermal reservoirs before operation. Every paired protocol required independent reconstruction at the receiving system, rather than treating another practitioner's authorization as a command. Limiter activation closed the permitted exchange or destroyed the active geometry into randomized passive states. No central controller could sustain a site after local stop authority withdrew consent.

The network remained a collection of systems designed not to become one.

The first half of the review tested whether it could remain that way without Geneva.

Kessler presented the site exercise. At 04:00 the model removed central scheduling, the aggregate-risk service, both protected ceramic allocations, and every standing travel authorization. Local operations continued from closed baselines. Three sites suspended practitioner work because their current medical certificates depended on Geneva verification. Two entered procurement conservation. None lost stop authority. None opened an undeclared exchange path.

At hour eleven, the Rotterdam monitor detected phase correlation between two unrelated certification sweeps. The local systems halted before the correlation crossed the doctrine's aggregate threshold. At hour fourteen, Brno reconstructed the event as an optical-clock error and circulated the correction through an emergency channel authenticated by three site custodians rather than Geneva.

“The finding is not that the center is unnecessary,” Kessler said. “The finding is that central absence degrades capacity before it degrades control.”

Rhys turned a page with his left hand. “For thirty days.”

“For thirty days under the exercise horizon. Ceramic allocation becomes the limiting dependency at day forty-seven. Medical recertification removes another four sites by day sixty. Neither failure creates unsafe authority. They reduce operations.”

“Unless an external system uses the interval.”

“Strategic monitoring continues under separate command. Response packages retain local activation gates. The capacity obligation survives your office.”

The last phrase entered the room without emphasis.

Rhys marked the finding. “Accepted.”

Amara presented scientific custody next. The program's characterized Conduit library contained one hundred eighty-six stable practitioner architectures, twenty-seven approved paired protocols, and eleven material-interface families. None reproduced Standard Eight. Soren remained the most capable secondary practitioner, a stable Seven whose field-interaction architecture could sustain complex multi-system states inside established boundaries. He could not join incompatible systems whose return conditions lacked a shared model. Calling the difference a matter of training would make the library inaccurate and Soren unsafe.

The review did not call it that.

Amara's custody plan divided scientific authority among three roles. A materials lead could maintain known lattice families. A practitioner lead could authorize characterized sessions with independent medical concurrence. Amara retained consent authority over her own Standard Five architecture and scientific custody of source-derived observations already released to the program compartment.

The four acquired relations she had carried since the deep source work did not appear in the packet.

They remained inside the Mercer-Sen-Fadel partition: stable, unresolved, and excluded from any continuity model that lacked permission to admit they existed.

“What stops the scientific custodian from interpreting an unavailable director's restrictions as obsolete?” Morel asked.

“The restrictions do,” Amara said. “Custody permits preservation, replication inside characterized limits, and review. It does not convert an absence into evidence.”

“What if the evidence changes during the absence?”

“Then the existing gates become more restrictive until an authorized review occurs.”

Kessler glanced at the operational-loss curve. “That rule can suspend every paired protocol on one anomalous reading.”

“Yes.”

“Including an anomalous reading later resolved as instrumentation error.”

“Instrumentation errors do not become less anomalous because a schedule dislikes them.”

Kessler gave the answer its required consideration. “The strategic office needs immediate access to the declared reason for suspension.”

“The reason, the measured consequence, and the requirements for review,” Amara said. “Not source reconstruction.”

“Accepted.”

The exchange took ninety seconds. Five years earlier, the same boundary would have occupied three meetings and generated competing drafts. Doctrine had not removed disagreement. It had given disagreement load paths.

Aaron recorded the phrase in no official document.

Medical authority reviewed practitioner continuity. Dr. Ivo Varga had left active Directorate work two years earlier; his successor, Dr. Leila Haddad, spoke with the deliberate compression of someone who regarded extra words as another route for clinical error. Current systems preserved independent medical stop authority. No technical, scientific, or strategic official could override a practitioner restriction. Loss of central medical records caused credentials to expire toward nonoperation, never toward presumed fitness.

She reached the source-interface category and stopped.

“Restricted Interface Condition has one medically eligible practitioner,” Haddad said.

The packet returned **ONE** without displaying Aaron's name.

“Eligibility expires in seventy-two hours if the controlled medical service cannot answer,” she continued. “A local certificate permits emergency containment maintenance during that period. It does not permit elective contact, new geometry, or extension beyond established limits.”

“Can the certificate renew locally?” Morel asked.

“No.”

“Then central medical absence disables source-interface work after three days.”

“The source-operation hold already disables elective work. Established passive containment continues without a practitioner. If a condition requires direct intervention after hour seventy-two, the institution chooses between an unassessed medical risk and a containment risk. The current architecture does not erase that choice by assigning it in advance.”

Aaron enlarged the certificate logic. The emergency route originated in his classification protocol review. His future Source Interface Custodian credential needed a medical stop that survived civil-trace retirement without giving local systems access to his public medical identity. Haddad's design solved the identity boundary through a sealed eligibility assertion. It did not solve absence of the service issuing it.

“Two geographically separate medical authorities can hold renewal access,” Aaron said. “Each receives current eligibility state without source identity. Joint authentication extends the certificate for one additional seventy-two-hour interval. No further renewal without examination.”

Haddad read the proposed route. “That gives us six days.”

“It gives containment six days. It gives the practitioner one duplicated refusal point and one mandatory examination.”

“The second authority needs the complete recovery criteria.”

“Without the source record.”

“Obviously.”

Kessler added the implementation to the resilience schedule. Morel added jurisdictional review. Rhys added nothing.

His right hand rested below the edge of the table. The left continued turning pages.

At 08:36 the radiator clicked, failed to close its valve, and began heating the room past its set point. Morel removed her jacket. Kessler opened the water carafe. Amara crossed to the wall control and lowered the requested temperature by two degrees, an instruction the valve acknowledged and did not obey.

Rhys remained seated through the scheduled break.

Aaron took his own water to the head of the table. Up close, the gray beneath Rhys's eyes no longer belonged to the security film's altered light. A narrow medication patch showed above his collar where the shirt shifted against his neck. His breathing maintained regular depth until he attempted to move; then the next inhalation arrived too early.

“The room temperature exceeds occupational guidance,” Aaron said.

“File the observation with Facilities.”

“Amara already did.”

“Then the institution has responded at its customary speed.”

Aaron set the water within reach of Rhys's left hand.

“You could have scheduled the review in Conference Two.”

“Conference Two's isolation certificate is under renewal.”

“You could schedule a recess.”

“One exists.”

“You have not moved.”

Rhys raised his eyes. The old impatience remained intact. Its physical delivery had lost efficiency.

“Do you intend to state the inference?” he asked.

“Not without the evidence you have declined to supply.”

“Then we are both respecting the current compartment.”

Rhys drank the water. His left hand returned the glass to the table without sound.

“The next section concerns your office,” Aaron said.

“The next section concerns two authorities presently assigned to it.”

“Under an exercise that removes your authentication.”

“Temporary unavailability is a standard planning condition.”

“So is permanent vacancy.”

For the first time that morning, Rhys's attention left the packet rather than Aaron. The empty chair stood one meter away. Its gray placard persisted above the polished back.

“Permanent vacancy is not today's condition,” he said.

The answer closed the discussion without denying its subject.

Aaron returned to his seat.

---

The source authority exercise began at 08:45.

The model removed Rhys's credentials and introduced three events.

At hour six, a passive containment monitor returned an unclassified phase deviation from Kiruna. At day four, an external research program requested emergency assistance after detecting uncontrolled cross-system interference in a copied Conduit array. At day eleven, Records Integrity found a public filing that connected a current materials supplier to a predecessor program inside the official Discovery history.

Each event belonged to a different compartment. Together they produced a decision no single existing alternate could make.

Amara could evaluate whether the phase deviation matched any characterized source behavior. Aaron could determine whether the copied array created a coupling risk and whether its systems could be joined safely enough to close. Kessler could authorize strategic assistance and protect the controlled-capacity doctrine. Records Integrity could seal, redirect, or preserve the public filing. Haddad could stop practitioner involvement. Morel could determine what each office could lawfully share.

No one except Rhys could view all five findings and issue a binding disposition.

The model waited.

“Run existing authorities,” Rhys said.

Kessler authorized remote technical assistance to the external program but withheld practitioner deployment. Amara classified the Kiruna deviation as uncharacterized and raised source monitoring. Aaron reconstructed the copied array from released telemetry. Two field processes attempted to redirect return momentum into the same isolation mass on incompatible timing. Joining them directly would convert a correct local stop in either controller into an uncontrolled transfer through the other.

He designed a closure sequence that used the array's existing flywheels as declared reservoirs. The first process discharged mechanical energy into its own brake stack. The second held its boundary open for thirty-eight milliseconds without exchanging momentum, then closed after the first system's residual motion dropped below the measured threshold. A temporary optical link synchronized observation only. It carried no control authority and no energy between sites.

The external team could execute the sequence without Aaron entering either field.

Haddad approved remote participation. Kessler approved release of the closure geometry. Amara kept source monitoring elevated because the Kiruna deviation remained unresolved.

Records Integrity examined the public filing. It contained no false statement and no direct source link. Removing it would create a procurement discontinuity visible to ordinary auditors. Vautrin's office recommended preservation with prospective supplier reassignment.

All three events reached stable local outcomes.

The doctrine still had no answer to the relation among them.

“The strategic office can declare them independent,” Kessler said. “No evidence connects Kiruna, the copied array, and the supplier filing.”

“No releasable evidence,” Amara said.

“Do you possess controlled evidence of a relation?”

“No. I possess incomplete access to a source whose behavior we do not fully understand.”

“Then an authorized official needs to determine whether incomplete access justifies treating the events as one condition,” Morel said.

Rhys watched the unresolved decision indicator.

“The authority cannot default to the strategic office,” Aaron said. “Capacity obligations create a preference for action.”

Kessler did not object. “It also cannot default to scientific custody. Uncertainty creates a preference for suspension.”

“Medical authority can stop human exposure,” Haddad said. “It cannot decide supplier history or international assistance.”

“Legal authority can limit exchange,” Morel said. “It cannot determine the purpose of the limit.”

The empty chair accumulated functions with each statement.

Rhys opened the succession criteria.

The heading did not use the word.

**CROSS-COMPARTMENT DISPOSITION AUTHORITY — ELIGIBILITY CONDITIONS**

The eligible official required reconstructive access to Contact Zero, current source-interface knowledge, authority under Controlled Continuity Doctrine, familiarity with the public Discovery architecture, power to convene independent medical and scientific review, and a record of decisions across both containment and capacity compartments.

Aaron knew one living person who satisfied every condition.

Rhys did not. His source knowledge came through Aaron's translations. He understood the full classified record and governed access to it, but he could not independently interpret a Node field state or determine when two incompatible Resonant systems could exchange without catastrophic interference.

The criteria described more than a replacement for Rhys.

They described an office Rhys himself had never fully occupied.

Morel read the list twice. “We cannot create an authority with broader reconstructive access than the current director merely because he becomes unavailable.”

“The access already exists,” Kessler said.

Nobody turned toward Aaron. The courtesy made the direction more exact.

Amara rested one hand on her closed packet. “Access is not authority.”

“No,” Rhys said. “That is the defect under review.”

The room held the sentence.

Aaron examined the eligibility conditions. “You cannot assign this as a standing unilateral authority.”

“Explain,” Morel said.

“The office would combine source interpretation, technical classification, and final disposition. A misinterpretation could authenticate its own operational consequence.”

“Remedy?”

“Separate determination from action. The disposition authority decides whether events require cross-compartment treatment and specifies the protected premise. Scientific, strategic, medical, legal, and records offices retain execution and independent stops. No source interpretation can compel practitioner action. No capacity claim can compel source access. Every decision expires unless reviewed against new evidence.”

Kessler studied the structure. “The disposition still controls which premise every office must use.”

“Yes.”

“Then it remains final authority expressed as constraints.”

“Most final authority is.”

Rhys's mouth changed by less than a smile. “Write the charter.”

Aaron did not touch the document controls.

“For whom?” he asked.

Rhys met his gaze across the table.

“Write the function,” he said.

Morel entered the assignment under Aaron's current credential. Kessler authenticated strategic concurrence. Amara did not authenticate.

“Scientific custody?” Rhys asked.

“I concur that the function must exist,” Amara said. “I have not reviewed the charter he has not written.”

“Recorded.”

The meeting advanced to implementation dates.

No one named a successor.

They gave Aaron thirty days to design the authority that would require one.

---

At 11:52, the isolation cage released the room.

Morel left with three jurisdictional questions. Haddad left with the duplicate medical-certificate design. Kessler remained long enough to tell Aaron that Strategic Continuity would provide its conflict cases by Monday and that she expected the charter to survive a scenario in which her office disagreed with every premise it contained.

“That is one of the design conditions,” Aaron said.

“Good.” She closed her case. “Make the same condition apply to you.”

“It already does.”

“Write it anyway.”

She left.

Amara waited beside the empty chair while Rhys signed the physical meeting attestation. He used his left hand. The signature preserved its familiar shape but not its ordinary speed.

“Callum,” she said, “Haddad scheduled you for twelve-fifteen.”

“She schedules many things.”

“This one has transport attached.”

Rhys returned the pen to its recess. “Then the transport may continue believing in us for another three minutes.”

Amara took his document case before he could reach for it. The gesture contained enough routine to establish that it had happened before.

Aaron watched her place the case over her shoulder.

“You knew about the review,” he said.

“I received the scientific-custody section four days ago.”

“Did you know the final eligibility conditions?”

“No.”

“Did you infer them?”

Amara considered the question with the seriousness she ordinarily reserved for data and whether another person had eaten.

“I inferred that the institution would eventually discover it had assigned you every fact required for a decision and none of the title,” she said.

“That is not an answer about succession.”

“Neither was the meeting.”

Rhys rose. His left palm pressed against the table until his legs accepted the transfer. Amara did not assist him. Aaron understood the precision in that refusal: help offered before the point of failure would convert a difficult movement into a public incapacity. Rhys straightened, waited one breath, and put on his coat one-handed.

The gray placard above the empty chair disappeared when the room cleared its exercise state.

The chair remained.

“The charter is not a designation,” Rhys said.

“It defines who can be designated.”

“It defines what must continue.”

“Through one eligible person.”

“At present.”

Aaron heard the same qualification Rhys had applied to his future Source Interface Custodian role thirteen days earlier. Initially. At present. Institutional language for a dependency no schedule knew how to end.

“Soren will not become an Eight because the charter needs redundancy,” Aaron said.

“No.”

“Amara cannot inherit Coupling.”

“No.”

“Kessler cannot receive source interpretation by broadening strategic authority.”

“She would reject that solution as readily as you.”

“Then the eligibility set remains one.”

Rhys secured the top button of his coat with his left hand. “Your first task is to make the function safe. Arithmetic can follow.”

He moved toward the door. His right foot cleared the threshold by less than the left. The corridor outside extended twenty-two meters to the lift and nine to the nearest stair. Rhys turned toward the lift.

Aaron had never seen him use it for a descent of one floor.

Amara walked beside him at a pace that did not advertise accommodation. Neither asked Aaron to follow.

At the lift, Rhys pressed the call plate. His right hand rose, stopped two centimeters short, and lowered. Amara pressed it without interrupting the sentence she had begun about the Kiruna phase model.

The doors opened.

Rhys entered, then turned back toward Aaron.

“Thirty days,” he said.

The doors closed between them.

Aaron returned to the conference room.

Facilities had finally closed the radiator valve. Heat remained stored in iron, stone, six occupied chairs, and one chair no longer assigned to an absence. The table display showed Aaron's new work item beneath the credentials the erasure protocol intended to migrate away from his public name.

**DRAFT CROSS-COMPARTMENT DISPOSITION AUTHORITY CHARTER**

**RESPONSIBLE OFFICIAL: AARON MERCER**

His private phone vibrated once inside his coat. He had moved Elin's number into it from the card without creating a work-system record. Her message contained an address, a time, and one additional instruction.

*The building may be evaluated only if it starts the conversation.*

Aaron read the line twice.

He entered the restaurant address into no institutional calendar. Then he set the phone beside the meeting packet and opened the blank charter.

At 12:07, Aaron gave the future authority its first prohibition.

*The disposition authority shall operate no field system, command no practitioner, release no classified geometry, and amend no source record by virtue of disposition authority alone.*

An office built to cross compartments needed less direct power than any office it could bind. Otherwise the person interpreting a threat could manufacture the evidence, select the response, compel the practitioner, and certify the outcome inside one continuous chain. Rhys had prevented that failure through habit, competing authorities, and thirty years of institutional opposition. A successor charter could not rely on another person developing the same collection of restraints under pressure.

Aaron divided the function into four operations.

**DECLARE RELATION.** Determine whether events held by separate compartments required treatment as one condition.

**STATE PROTECTED PREMISE.** Identify the minimum classified fact every responding office must preserve without releasing its source.

**ASSIGN INDEPENDENT ACTIONS.** Require each competent office to act within its own authority and return a result rather than a promise.

**CLOSE OR RENEW.** End the cross-compartment condition when evidence no longer supported it, or renew it against a stated expiration and external review.

He added gates around each operation. A relation declaration required two independent observations or one observation with a measurable containment consequence. Source-derived interpretation needed scientific concurrence that the underlying trace supported the stated uncertainty. Kessler's office could challenge the scope of a protected premise without receiving the fact behind it. Medical authority retained an absolute human stop. Records Integrity could refuse any correction that made the public account false. Legal could prohibit an exchange but could not convert prohibition into a technical conclusion.

No hold created permission. No expired permission converted into precedent. No failure to answer counted as consent.

The constraints resembled Coupling because the institutional problem shared its shape. Independent systems needed to exchange enough state to resolve one condition without allowing any system to redefine the others. The charter supplied no energy, expertise, or moral authority that its component offices did not already possess. It declared which decisions could cross, through which interface, and where every consequence returned for accounting.

At 12:28, Aaron built the recusal condition.

The disposition holder could not determine a matter involving their own medical eligibility, classification status, disciplinary exposure, or civil identity. The restriction immediately captured four foreseeable decisions in Aaron's next eighteen months. His trace-retirement process could affect source continuity. Source continuity could affect the timetable for trace retirement. A careless charter would allow him to classify the risks of his own classification and then compel Records Integrity to accept the result.

He assigned those conflicts to a four-office panel: scientific custody, strategic continuity, medical authority, and Directorate counsel. Three could act. Medical refusal remained absolute for practitioner exposure. Records Integrity retained an independent factual-integrity stop over the public account.

The panel could preserve a stable condition. It could not initiate new source work.

Aaron tested the structure against the morning scenario. The Kiruna deviation and copied array produced two observations but no measured relation. The disposition holder could raise a time-limited common review because both affected aggregate Boundary visibility under the same doctrine. The protected premise would state only that source risk could not yet be excluded. Kessler could continue remote assistance. Amara could increase passive observation. Aaron could release the safe closure sequence through technical authority. Records Integrity could preserve the supplier filing. No office could infer that the filing caused the phase deviation, and no official could use the uncertainty to initiate contact with the Node.

The condition expired after seventy-two hours unless new evidence established a shared consequence.

The model closed.

He changed the period to forty-eight. Three days gave administrative convenience more time than the physics required.

The eligibility section remained.

Aaron created a matrix with requirements across the top and current offices down the side. Rhys held classified-history access, doctrine authority, disclosure governance, and cross-compartment standing. He lacked direct source interpretation and Coupling capability. Amara held scientific custody, most of the source-derived record, and the authority to refuse use of her architecture. She lacked strategic disposition and Standard Eight. Kessler held capacity, response, and international coordination. Morel held lawful exchange. Haddad held the human stop. Fadel could independently authenticate transformations between native traces and reconstructed models.

Aaron's row returned complete.

He removed the names and ran the matrix by credentials.

One row returned complete.

The result did not change when he separated source interpretation from technical classification. Both credentials still resolved to the same biological and field-signature assertion inside the controlled register Vautrin had bound thirteen days earlier. After formalization, his public name would stop answering employment questions. The institutional relation would persist. A future meeting packet could display **SOURCE INTERFACE CUSTODIAN** or **CROSS-COMPARTMENT DISPOSITION AUTHORITY** without exposing that one continuing person occupied both chairs.

The arrangement protected the secret. It did not create a second person.

Aaron added a succession-development obligation. The office had to identify which eligibility requirements depended on unique capability, which could be trained, which could be distributed, and which should never converge again. It prohibited altering a practitioner's Standard classification to satisfy administrative redundancy. It prohibited treating Soren's stable Seven architecture as an incomplete Eight. It prohibited compelled source exposure as a qualification path.

Those sentences required no supporting discussion. Institutions optimized measured deficits. The charter needed to prevent a future staffing table from converting a human boundary into a vacancy target.

At 12:49, the heating pipes contracted with a succession of small metallic knocks inside the wall. Facilities had corrected the valve after the room no longer needed correction. The stored warmth left the tabletop more slowly than the air.

Aaron placed the draft into restricted review. Morel, Amara, Kessler, Haddad, and Fadel received access to the sections their authority could evaluate. Rhys received the complete document. Aaron withheld subject authentication from the eligibility section. He could design the conditions. He would not certify himself as their answer.

The system accepted the draft and displayed the review deadline.

Thirty days.

The review had examined what happened when Rhys could no longer carry the Directorate's full classified weight. Every technical safeguard distributed a part of it. Every institutional safeguard kept one office from converting its own fear into automatic permission. Every remaining line converged on the same unnamed result.

The question of succession had not been asked.

Aaron had received the assignment anyway.
