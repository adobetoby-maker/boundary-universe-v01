# Chapter Twelve: HANDOVER

## Day 4,392

Authority crossed the Geneva facility on paper.

At 07:12, a Records Integrity carrier collected the first transfer book from Rhys’s office, enclosed it in a gray case, and walked it twenty-six meters to Conference Four. The route passed through two authenticated doors and one open corridor where facilities staff replaced a failed radiator valve. No network recorded the contents. No wireless credential opened the case. The carrier placed it on a steel table inside the conference room, rotated both mechanical keys, and stepped back while Joël Maret compared the broken wax indicators against the morning custody sheet.

The book had governed source access for eight years. Its journey required four minutes.

Aaron stood at the end of the table designated for the receiving principal and did not touch it.

Conference Four occupied the seam between the facility’s nineteenth-century accounting wing and its later secure extension. One wall exposed pale limestone beneath a copper isolation mesh. The opposite wall consisted of ceramic panels whose joints aligned to the millimeter and whose concealed clips allowed inspection without drilling through the field enclosure. Heat came from the radiator under the old wall. Cooling came through the raised floor under the new one. The two systems disagreed about the room temperature by 1.6 degrees.

Sabine Morel had marked the disagreement outside the scope of the transfer.

“Custody seal correct,” Maret said.

He lifted the book onto an isolated reading stand. The gray cover carried no title, only a recessed rectangle where a previous classification label no longer remained. Aaron recognized the volume from the first cabinet gap in Rhys’s office. It contained the Day 1,186 executive bargain: the decision that converted Contact Zero suppression from an emergency act into institutional doctrine.

Twenty-two more items waited upstairs.

Amara entered through the scientific-authentication door and placed her palm on the local discharge plate. The monitor accepted ordinary retained charge. Her four acquired relations remained below the room’s admission threshold, stable and quiescent. She signed as Program Scientific Lead, then took the seat to Aaron’s left.

Kessler arrived with two Strategic Continuity officers who stopped outside the compartment. She surrendered her active tablet, authenticated the printed capacity schedule, and sat opposite Amara. Morel occupied the legal seat. Dr. Leila Haddad placed a sealed medical finding beside the empty chair at the head of the table.

The chair no longer carried a gray placard.

At 07:29, its display rendered two lines.

**OUTGOING PRINCIPAL — CALLUM RHYS**

**AUTHORITY TERMINATES 12:00**

Aaron’s display remained blank.

Rhys entered one minute before the room clock changed.

His medical escort opened the outer door but did not cross the isolation line. Rhys advanced with his cane in his left hand and a thin document case secured against his right side by a shoulder strap. The arrangement left no hand free for the chair. Amara rose and drew it back without comment. Rhys turned, transferred his weight against the table edge, and lowered himself in three controlled stages.

The escort closed the door.

Rhys examined the attendance record. “Full?”

“Full,” Morel said.

“Then begin.”

No one thanked him for attending his own withdrawal.

Haddad opened the medical finding first.

The document used eleven sentences to separate declining capacity from lost capacity. Rhys remained able to understand the nature and consequences of the transfer, compare alternatives, communicate a stable choice, and identify the authorities moving with and outside his office. Progressive right-side motor impairment, exertional intolerance, and an unreliable mobility forecast made continued full-duty performance medically unsustainable. The finding recommended withdrawal from active authority before an acute incapacity forced emergency succession.

It did not diagnose him for the room. It did not predict survival. It did not convert a recommendation into an order.

Haddad turned toward Rhys. “Do you understand the finding?”

“Yes.”

“Do you dispute any functional statement in it?”

“No.”

“Has anyone conditioned treatment, access, or personal support on your agreement to transfer authority?”

“No.”

“Do you choose to withdraw under the terms before you?”

Rhys rested his left hand beside the sealed case. “I do.”

Haddad signed the capacity line and moved the finding to Morel.

Aaron had watched Rhys authenticate source operations under isolation alarms, ministerial demands, a non-restorable injury, and evidence that reduced every available decision to a different form of damage. The sentence that ended his authority required no biometric stress measure and activated nothing by itself. It entered a paper record in blue-black ink.

Morel reviewed the legal instrument.

Rhys resigned three linked appointments: Director of Advanced Research Classification, initiating authority for the Contact Zero protected record, and principal disposition holder under Controlled Continuity Doctrine. The first returned to the Directorate appointment system. The second and third transferred through the succession charter under review. His existing decisions remained valid until their own expiry conditions. His withdrawal supplied no presumption for renewal, reversal, or reinterpretation.

The instrument created no emeritus access.

Aaron read that clause twice.

Rhys would retain personal medical records, ordinary civil rights, and the controlled knowledge already inside him. He would retain no authentication, supervisory standing, source-review entitlement, or informal concurrence function after twelve. Future officials could interview him as a classified witness under a specifically authorized question. They could not keep him attached to the office as an unwritten explanation.

“Your requested amendment?” Morel asked him.

“Included.”

“You understand that a future disposition holder may reach a conclusion you oppose and need not consult you.”

“That is the purpose.”

“Including on suppression renewal.”

“Especially on suppression renewal.”

Morel signed.

At 07:51, she placed the resignation beside the succession charter Aaron had drafted.

The charter occupied thirty-six pages. Its first prohibition remained unchanged: the disposition authority could operate no field system, command no practitioner, release no classified geometry, or amend any source record by virtue of disposition authority alone. Medical Authority retained an absolute stop over human exposure. Scientific custody could refuse an interpretation unsupported by the native trace. Records Integrity could reject any public correction that made the official account false. Strategic Continuity retained capacity and response execution but could not compel source access. Counsel could prohibit an exchange but could not turn the prohibition into a technical finding.

The disposition holder could declare that facts separated by compartment formed one decision condition. He could specify the minimum protected premise each office needed. He could require answers. He could set an expiring constraint.

He could not require the answers to agree.

The final review had added three controls. Every active disposition expired within forty-eight hours unless a named external authority renewed it against new evidence. Any decision touching the holder’s medical eligibility, civil identity, classification, or disciplinary exposure transferred to the four-office conflict panel. The suppression rationale required annual reconstruction from current evidence, with prior disposition carrying a duty to review and no presumption of renewal.

Aaron had written each clause to constrain a person whose eligibility matrix contained his name alone.

He had not signed the matrix.

Maret distributed four paper attestations. Amara received scientific access and reconstruction competence. Kessler received Controlled Continuity standing and cross-compartment decision history. Morel received lawful appointment and conflict-control compliance. Haddad received present medical eligibility for source-interface duty, separate from fitness for administrative office.

No attestation asked whether Aaron wanted the authority.

He had consented to candidacy on Day 4,372 when he accepted responsibility for the charter. The question now concerned whether the institution could prove that its answer did not depend on Aaron proving himself.

Amara read first.

“Aaron holds reconstructive access to the complete Contact Zero scientific record,” she said. “He can distinguish native source observations from human translations and identify uncertainty introduced at each reconstruction. He remains the only medically characterized practitioner able to establish a direct source interface without acute dissociation. That capability does not authorize its use. Scientific concurrence remains independent.”

She signed.

Kessler read her finding.

“Aaron has issued or contributed to binding dispositions across containment, characterized capacity, copied-system response, international assistance, and Controlled Continuity implementation. He has opposed my office when evidence required restraint and supported it when restraint would have displaced risk into ungoverned development. Strategic disagreement supplies no finding against eligibility.”

Her pen paused above the signature line.

“It may supply one against convenience,” she added.

“Convenience is not listed,” Morel said.

“A drafting oversight.” Kessler signed.

Morel confirmed the appointment route and the four-office recusal mechanism. Haddad confirmed that Aaron’s recent exposure restriction had ended, that the elective source-operation hold remained institutional rather than medical, and that no administrative title could override a future practitioner stop.

Maret collected the attestations.

The eligibility display changed from blank to amber.

**CONDITIONS SATISFIED — SUBJECT AUTHENTICATION WITHHELD BY DESIGN**

Rhys turned to Aaron. “Do you accept the appointment and its constraints?”

The room provided no abstraction behind which to stand. Aaron could name every obligation and most foreseeable failures. He also knew the limit of that knowledge. The fullest source disclosure had expanded the consequence space beyond any one office’s capacity to model it. The Quieters detected relations, not declared intentions. The Node preserved concealment by spending coherence that humanity could not restore from an external reservoir. A future Consensus architecture remained possible, not promised. In the interval, every decision would distribute cost among systems unable to share a complete description.

Authority did not solve the incompatibility.

It assigned him to keep the exchanges bounded.

“I accept,” Aaron said.

The display changed to white.

**DESIGNATE — AARON MERCER**

**EFFECTIVE 12:00**

The public name remained active. The classification protocol did not advance. For the next several hundred days, Aaron Mercer would hold a named senior office while Records Integrity continued designing the conditions under which that name could vanish without interrupting the work.

The membrane grew thinner by one credential.

At 08:16, the transfer of records began.

Three custody carriers moved the books one at a time. Maret admitted each case, verified its seals, and assigned the item to a receiving cabinet along the room’s ceramic wall. The cabinet contained no electronic lock. Its keyway accepted two dissimilar mechanical keys, one held by the disposition office and one by Records Integrity. Inside, passive optical fibers registered whether a shelf carried weight without identifying the object.

The first books documented decisions Aaron knew from their consequences. The early Helix classifications. Yuki Osei’s protected clinical disposition. The construction of the official Discovery chronology. The original Conduit development mandate. Kessler’s capacity challenge. The negotiated Controlled Continuity Doctrine.

Others contained decisions he had never seen assembled in one chain: ministerial briefings limited to consequence class, lunar procurement exceptions, twelve living-subject classifications, source-location decoys, unexecuted disclosure contingencies, and annual decisions not to broaden the Contact Zero compartment.

The absence of novelty did not reduce their mass.

At 09:03, the nineteenth book failed custody verification.

One wax indicator showed a shallow fracture beside the receiving seal. The case remained locked. Its pressure strip reported no opening, and the departure photograph showed the fracture already present but partly hidden beneath Rhys’s thumbprint.

Maret halted the transfer.

The room stopped with him. No one argued that a damaged seal probably meant nothing. The books already inside the cabinet remained received. The four still upstairs remained under outgoing custody. The damaged case occupied neither state.

“Return it,” Rhys said.

Maret did not move. “The outgoing principal cannot accept returned custody after declaring transfer intent without a new ledger entry.”

“Then create one.”

“I cannot create it retroactively.”

Rhys turned toward Morel.

“Records Integrity is correct,” she said. “The case can enter joint exception custody pending content verification. Neither principal exercises it.”

“That delays completion,” Rhys said.

“It delays one object,” Aaron said. “Authority transfers independently at twelve.”

Rhys shifted his attention to him.

The damaged case held the Primary Stewardship designation and its amendments. Without it, Aaron could authenticate current source-interface obligations through existing credentials but could not prove the complete historical chain by which those obligations reached him. The distinction created no immediate safety failure. It created a future provenance defect precisely like the relations his erasure protocol now treated as dangerous.

Aaron said, “Open under dual observation. Compare every leaf against the cold optical register. If content and order match, reseal under exception custody and document the damaged indicator as inherited state. Do not replace the original wax or represent the chain as uninterrupted.”

Maret considered the instruction. “Disposition authority is not yet effective.”

“Technical custody recommendation.”

“Under which current office?” Rhys asked.

Aaron could have cited Senior Coupling Specialist and reached too far. The record concerned source stewardship but no operating system. He could have relied on his future appointment and allowed noon to contaminate nine o’clock.

“None,” he said. “Withdrawn.”

The recommendation left the room.

Maret turned to Rhys. “Outgoing disposition?”

Rhys examined the fractured seal. “Joint exception custody. Verify against the cold register. Preserve the damaged indicator as inherited state.”

The same answer entered under valid authority.

He did not look at Aaron when he gave it.

Two Records Integrity examiners arrived with a portable optical reader and an unmarked steel tray. They established a temporary clean surface at the far end of the table, photographed the case from eight angles, and opened it with both keys. The wax indicator separated along its existing fracture. No secondary line crossed the embedded thread.

The book inside contained one hundred twelve leaves. The examiners compared edge shape, fiber pattern, binding tension, and reflected cold-register marks without projecting the text. Every leaf matched. Page order remained intact. The case received a new outer seal enclosing the original fragments in a transparent pocket.

Custody resumed at 10:11.

The delay consumed most of Rhys’s permitted work interval. Haddad checked him twice without interrupting the record. His breathing shortened after he stood to authenticate the final cabinet. His right foot dragged across the last half meter. He rejected the offered chair until the key turned, then sat before Haddad needed to instruct him.

At 10:34, Maret closed the custody ledger.

“All records received or placed under documented exception custody,” he said. “No unassigned item remains.”

The room moved to operational authority.

Rhys opened the thin case he had carried himself. It contained no record book. Three objects rested in fitted recesses: a mechanical key, a transparent ceramic tile crossed by nine dark conductive traces, and a narrow paper envelope.

The key opened the disposition cabinet with Records Integrity concurrence.

The tile supplied one half of the lunar source-authentication root. Its traces held no stored energy. When placed inside a powered reader, their fixed geometry constrained which optical, thermal, and field paths could participate in a challenge response. The paired half remained at the Lunar Far-Side Array. Neither tile generated authority or signal. Together, under declared local power, they permitted two distant systems to verify that each still occupied the approved physical boundary.

The envelope contained single-use recovery phrases printed by an isolated mechanical process. Aaron would never see them together. Maret would divide them among three custodians before noon.

Rhys touched the tile with two fingers of his left hand. “This root authenticated the first post-Helix lunar closure.”

“The geometry has changed since,” Aaron said.

“Four times.”

“Then the object authenticates continuity, not present configuration.”

“Correct.”

Rhys lifted his hand. Maret rotated the case toward Aaron.

Aaron did not take the tile. “Receiving it personally would join physical custody to subject identity.”

“Proposed alternative?” Maret asked.

“Assign it to the office cabinet. Record me as current authorized user, not owner. Future credential rotation must not require this civil name.”

Morel examined the amendment sheet. “That anticipates classification.”

“It prevents the handover from defeating a process already under review.”

Rhys said nothing.

Maret wrote the custody amendment, passed it to Morel, then to Rhys. Aaron’s line recorded acknowledgment only. After the signatures, Maret placed the tile in a shielded drawer and locked both cabinet keys.

The authority root entered Aaron’s office without entering his hands.

At 11:07, the passive-continuity alarm illuminated.

No audible tone crossed the room. A narrow violet bar lit Amara’s desk display, followed by a source-neutral condition code.

**INTERNAL COHERENCE PROXY — NINETY-DAY DEVIATION LIMIT**

The measurement came from Passive Continuity Room Two. It did not open the source path, transmit toward the Node, or alter any field boundary. Three independent detector families had completed the clock-substitution review Aaron ordered the previous afternoon. Channel nineteen’s 1.8-second change resolved as instrument-local variance. The broader decline did not.

Amara authenticated the scientific view. “The rolling trend crossed the review limit at ten fifty-nine. Magnitude point three one percent. Confidence interval excludes flat baseline. No immediate continuity threshold.”

Kessler opened the capacity consequence layer. “No aggregate network change. No strategic detector response.”

Haddad checked Aaron’s standing source certificate. “Eligible for emergency maintenance. Elective hold remains.”

The condition required cross-compartment disposition because it altered the evidence beneath the suppression rationale and might eventually require the only medically eligible interface practitioner. It did not require intervention now.

The room clock read 11:09.

Rhys retained authority for fifty-one minutes.

“Outgoing disposition holder?” Morel asked.

Rhys kept his eyes on the violet bar. “The condition remains open at twelve.”

“You can issue an interim disposition.”

“I can.”

He did not.

Aaron understood the refusal. A decision lasting beyond noon could satisfy the letter of transfer while placing Rhys’s conclusion inside the first interval of Aaron’s authority. Rhys would leave neither an order nor a silence that defaulted toward operation.

Morel turned to Aaron. “Designate may prepare a disposition. It cannot take effect before twelve.”

Aaron opened the native measurement summary.

The coherence proxy derived from tiny reciprocal corrections across isolated instrument mounts, thermal sinks, and local field-null elements. Every joule, charge movement, and momentum transfer returned through a declared path. The measurement indicated that the concealed system beyond those instruments spent more internal order to preserve the same external quiet. It did not show why. It did not establish that human intervention would restore what the Node spent. Day 4,377 demonstrated only that Aaron’s Coupling could let coherence and concealment coexist briefly. Scaling that exchange might make the relation persistent enough for Quieters to detect.

He separated the condition into actions.

Scientific custody would increase passive sampling from six-hour to two-hour windows for forty-eight hours, using existing local power and no source transmission. Lunar authentication would compare the decline against external Boundary weather without querying the concealed system. Strategic Continuity would hold network geometry constant and identify any independent change in propagating control relations. Medical Authority would preserve Aaron’s emergency certificate and add no exposure authorization. The source-operation hold would continue.

He set two intervention gates. A measured approach to the immediate continuity threshold could authorize preparation of the minimum corrective geometry, not activation. Actual threshold crossing, confirmed by two detector families after environmental subtraction, would convene an emergency decision with scientific and medical stops intact. No trend forecast alone would open contact.

The disposition expired in forty-eight hours.

Amara read the scientific premise. “Concur.”

Kessler read the network constraint. “Concur.”

Haddad read the practitioner condition. “Medical stop retained.”

Morel reviewed the compartment exchanges. “Lawful.”

Maret assigned the prepared record an activation time of twelve.

Rhys watched each office sign. His face supplied no approval Aaron could use.

At 11:46, the meeting entered its final item.

Morel read the appointments that would persist after noon. Amara retained Program Scientific Lead and independent source-record custody. Kessler retained Strategic Continuity authority. Haddad retained medical stop authority. Maret’s office retained factual-integrity and custody stops. None reported to Aaron for the exercise of those independent functions.

Aaron received two appointments. Director of Advanced Research Classification carried Rhys’s senior institutional rank, convening authority, compartment-admission governance, and responsibility for the official Discovery architecture. Cross-Compartment Disposition Authority carried the narrower power defined in Aaron’s charter. The Contact Zero initiating authority did not transfer as a personal privilege; it dissolved into the constrained offices the charter named.

Full authority did not mean total authority.

It meant no senior official remained above Aaron who could settle the complete question for him.

Rhys signed the outgoing certification at 11:53.

Aaron signed acceptance at 11:55.

No one spoke during the remaining five minutes.

The radiator beneath the limestone wall clicked at 11:57 and admitted more heat despite the room already exceeding its set point. Air rose through the old iron fins. The floor system increased cooling to compensate. Two machines spent energy opposing each other because their controllers did not share a boundary model. Facilities would repair the valve after the classified meeting released the room.

At 12:00, Rhys’s display went dark.

Aaron’s rendered three lines.

**DIRECTOR — AARON MERCER**

**DISPOSITION AUTHORITY — ACTIVE**

**NEXT PREMISE REVIEW — DAY 4,422**

The prepared coherence disposition authenticated under his credential. Passive sampling increased. No source path opened.

Rhys removed the shoulder strap from across his chest with his left hand. The empty case remained on the table.

Morel closed the session. Kessler collected no papers and left through the strategic door. Haddad spoke quietly to the escort outside. Amara waited beside the discharge plate until Aaron completed the receiving log.

Rhys attempted to stand.

His left hand found the table. The cane held. His legs did not complete the first transfer. He sat again, reset his feet, and tried a second time. Amara moved the chair backward by six centimeters without taking his weight. The change supplied enough clearance. Rhys stood.

He remained the same height.

The room no longer organized itself around him.

“My transport leaves at twelve thirty,” he said to Haddad when the door opened.

“It leaves when you enter it,” she said.

“An operationally imprecise schedule.”

“You no longer hold operational authority.”

Rhys considered the correction. “So noted.”

The escort took the document case. Rhys kept the cane.

Aaron walked with him through the old accounting corridor. The failed radiator valve lay on a canvas sheet beside two blackened spanners and a shallow metal pan. A facilities technician cleaned mineral scale from the brass seat with a narrow wire brush. Without meeting traffic, the corridor smelled of wet wool, machine oil, and hot dust.

At the junction to Rhys’s former office, he stopped.

“You have eleven minutes before Medical claims breach,” Aaron said.

“Nine. The lift is slow.”

“Your personal estimate conflicts with the published schedule.”

“I have acquired relevant experience.”

The old exchange arrived intact. It carried no warmth either man needed to name.

Aaron asked, “Where does the transport take you?”

“Home. Then outpatient rehabilitation on Mondays and Thursdays. Haddad has arranged a physician nearer Nyon because apparently distance remains physical after retirement.”

The answer described a next week. Aaron accepted it as the correction it supplied.

“Will you return to the facility?” he asked.

“Only under a specific witness authorization or as a patient. Do not create the first merely because the second is inconvenient.”

“I won’t.”

“Good.”

Rhys turned toward the lift, then stopped before completing the first step.

“The office contains no private guidance,” he said. “Anything you find there belongs to the record or should be destroyed under its retention rule.”

“You removed nothing.”

“I removed a coat and two books I brought from home.”

“No letter.”

“You have twenty-three decision books.”

“One might have expected compression.”

“You distrust summaries.”

“Correctly.”

Rhys gave the smallest inclination of his head. “Then the handover is complete.”

He continued toward the lift. The escort stayed half a pace behind his right side. When the doors opened, Rhys entered without assistance and turned using the cane as a third point of contact. Aaron remained in the corridor until the doors closed.

Rhys had not died. He had not vanished. He had withdrawn while he could still choose the boundary of his own authority, and the institution had recorded the difference.

At 12:19, Aaron entered the office that now belonged to his appointment.

Facilities had adjusted nothing. Three secure cabinets lined the wall beside the sealed fireplace. Their shelves stood empty because the transferred books now occupied the disposition cabinet below. Rhys’s desk retained a pale rectangle where the document case had rested and a ring of mineral residue beneath the water carafe. The chair faced the desk at the same exact angle as the previous afternoon.

The windows faced the interior court. Security film reduced noon to a flat gray field, but it could not remove the movement of rainwater through the stone channel below. The channel accepted runoff from three roof pitches built in different centuries. Lead-lined outlets delivered it into one drain whose capacity depended on the oldest section releasing first. Facilities had installed overflow slots after a summer storm forced water beneath the archive door. The slots left deliberate gaps in a wall otherwise designed to close.

Rhys had kept the technical report in the lower desk drawer. Aaron found it beneath the office inventory, read the corrective-action date, and returned it to the same position. The report belonged to the building record. Its usefulness did not increase because a new person occupied the room.

Aaron placed no object on the cleared surface.

The terminal requested his first full-authority authentication. Eleven open matters waited beneath it: the coherence review, three classification renewals, a copied-array response, two Conduit ceramic allocations, the Relational Disclosure Control annex, the lunar root rotation, an unresolved supplier relation, and the annual suppression-premise schedule.

The system also displayed the source-operation hold.

**ACTIVE — NO EXPIRY BY SUCCESSION**

Aaron verified the line first.

Then he opened the coherence review he had already constrained for forty-eight hours. Amara’s first two-hour sampling window would close at thirteen. Kessler’s network comparison would arrive at fourteen. Haddad’s medical certificate remained valid through Friday. No result yet required a new decision.

He could wait for evidence without waiting for another person to authorize the waiting.

At 12:26, his private phone vibrated inside the isolated drawer.

He completed the authentication before opening it.

Elin had sent a photograph of the repaired kitchen table. A steel washer sat beneath one leg on the sloping floor. The glass near the edge remained still.

**Structural correction or surrender to local conditions?**

Aaron considered the distinction.

**Controlled accommodation. Document the washer.**

Her response arrived before he returned the phone to the drawer.

**I’ll add a plaque. Wednesday remains load-bearing.**

He read it once, then a second time without an operational reason.

**Wednesday remains,** he wrote.

The phrasing disclosed nothing and concealed nothing relevant. He closed the drawer.

At thirteen hundred, the new passive sample reached the office. The decline continued inside the projected band. Aaron signed no source operation. He requested the next measurement, preserved every independent stop, and left the forty-eight-hour disposition unchanged.

At 13:08, Strategic Continuity requested a priority ruling on the two protected ceramic allocations. Rotterdam needed replacement limiter plates after a manufacturing inclusion appeared during ultrasound inspection. Brno wanted the same production lot for an expansion already approved under the annual capacity schedule. Either allocation fit doctrine. Only one fit the available material.

Aaron required Rotterdam to provide measured remaining service life and Brno to provide the consequence of delay. He did not request their preferred outcomes. The answers returned through separate compartments: Rotterdam retained twenty-six safe operating days at its reduced load; Brno’s installation window closed in nine, delaying the expansion by four months. Kessler recommended Brno. Amara confirmed that Rotterdam’s defect remained stable under the reduced cycle. Local medical authority suspended paired work at Rotterdam until replacement.

Aaron allocated the new lot to Brno and moved Rotterdam’s reserve plates forward in the next manufacturing sequence. The decision preserved no maximum amount of activity. It preserved two local boundaries, one by material and one by restraint. He set the allocation to expire if the defect growth changed.

The ruling required fourteen minutes. Rhys had made hundreds like it. None appeared in the twenty-three decision books unless later events gave it historical weight.

No voice corrected him from the head of the table.

None remained above him that could.
