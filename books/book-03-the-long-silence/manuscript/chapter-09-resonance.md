# Chapter Nine: RESONANCE

## Day 4,381

The café occupied the ground floor of a building that leaned three centimeters toward the Rhône.

Elin had identified the displacement before they ordered coffee. She pointed it out through the window, using the edge of the neighboring façade as a reference, then spent two minutes explaining why the lean presented no immediate concern and the repaired stone above the eastern lintel did.

“The wall has had a century to negotiate with gravity,” she said. “The lintel repair is twelve years old and already arguing with itself.”

Aaron followed the pale mortar line above the window. The newer stone carried load through a rigid cement bed that ended abruptly against softer lime joints. Hairline cracks marked the boundary between the two materials.

“Different deformation tolerance,” he said.

“Exactly. Everyone worries about the dramatic error because they can see it from across the street. The useful error requires coffee and inappropriate professional attention.”

Their table stood close enough to the window for August light to warm one side of Aaron’s cup. The other side retained condensation from the glass carafe. A brass strip protected the table edge, dented at the corner by impacts the café had decided not to repair. Sunday traffic passed outside in short intervals between trams.

The environment contained no aggregate monitor, no compartment boundary, and no instrument capable of distinguishing a significant silence from an ordinary one. Aaron’s private terminal remained off inside his coat.

Elin added milk to her coffee. “Did the nouns survive the night?”

“Their prognosis is favorable.”

“And the headache?”

“One.”

“Out of?”

“Ten.”

“You understand that normal people answer that question without requiring a unit definition.”

“Normal people produce data that cannot be compared across visits.”

She drank her coffee. “I withdraw the criticism. Your physician deserves longitudinal evidence.”

Varga’s discharge instruction had permitted ordinary activity and passive technical work. It prohibited active source contact, driving until Monday, and any attempt to reinterpret resolving symptoms as evidence that the previous operation had carried insufficient ambition. The last clause did not appear in the signed form, though Varga had delivered it with comparable authority.

Elin asked what his week contained.

He could answer without constructing a cover. “A review of how a monitoring record should be reproduced.”

“Because the original result is disputed?”

“Because it contains more structure than the person who observed it could perceive at the time.”

“That sounds like a polite way to call someone inattentive.”

“The person was neurologically overloaded.”

Her attention settled on him.

Aaron lifted his cup. “The person’s physician has since approved coffee.”

“Good. I would object to having this conversation with a representative model.”

He almost explained why a representative model would answer more efficiently. The response remained available and unnecessary.

“Wednesday?” he asked instead.

Elin opened her calendar. “I have a site inspection until six. Seven thirty, if you can tolerate someone arriving with concrete dust on her coat.”

“Concrete dust has characterized removal procedures.”

“I’m beginning to suspect you say things like that on purpose.”

“The evidence remains incomplete.”

She entered the appointment.

Aaron did the same. He used the private calendar isolated from Directorate scheduling and assigned no explanatory label beyond Elin’s name. Six weeks earlier, the empty field would have registered as an information defect. Now it registered as enough.

The distinction remained small and operationally irrelevant.

He noticed it anyway.

## Day 4,382

At 07:12 on Monday, Amara placed twelve sealed reconstruction blocks on the analysis table.

Each block existed only as an authenticated file inside the room’s cold-state processor. The display rendered them as numbered gray rectangles because showing their internal geometry before protocol approval would compromise the blind. A separate controller held the key that mapped number to content. Fadel had generated the order from lunar control and committed its checksum before sending either layer to Geneva.

Analysis Room Four still contained no source path. The sealed optical conduit admitted read-only records. Its return fiber terminated inside a ceramic absorber below the floor, where any attempted control signal resolved as heat and an incident report. Two mechanical shutters stood closed between the room and the live operations network. Their handles carried red tamper wire signed at 06:31.

Aaron read the protocol’s purpose line.

*Determine whether the four-relation Sen baseline responds to active practitioner-bound reconstruction of any characterized component in the Day 4,377 disclosure.*

The line avoided the word fragment.

Five years of observation supported *persistent acquired topology*. Four stable relations followed every genuine Storage release at sub-threshold amplitude. They retained source order instead of controller labels. They recruited no energy, initiated no transfer, correlated with no identity measure, and produced no demonstrated capability. In the program’s most precise records, the pattern remained a finding attached to Amara’s architecture.

Fragment implied content.

No measurement had established that.

Amara rotated the protocol so he could authenticate the scientific boundary. “Varga removed your proposed live adaptive sequence.”

“Correctly.”

“You sound disappointed.”

“Correct decisions can preserve that property.”

The twelve blocks separated the disclosure into structures that could be reconstructed without reproducing a source carrier. Four contained phase-faithful relational subsets from the pre-Severance network architectures. Two contained the Quieter classifier and escalation topology, stripped of any external bearing. Two contained the Node’s refuge-partition geometry. One contained the nine closing constraints that Saturday’s analysis mapped to Consensus. Three controls preserved relation count, phase density, and controller demand; two randomized causal order, and one isolated the historic four-state sequence from its original context.

Geneva’s local lattice would generate each reconstruction from the facility bus. No energy, information, or field permission would arrive from the Node. Aaron would couple the playback lattice to Amara’s two staged Storage envelopes long enough for her to preserve and return each pattern. Her acquired four relations would remain monitored but unavailable as an intentional path.

The operation did not ask the Node anything.

It asked whether Amara’s baseline still qualified as passive when a locally reconstructed relation resembled the state in which she had acquired it.

“The blocks containing the Node’s internal partition require your complete source access,” Aaron said.

“They require my access to enter the room. The blind prevents me from knowing which block carries them during operation.”

“You prepared the scientific transforms.”

“Fadel recoded and reordered them after my validation. If I identify one from active load, that itself becomes a confound and we stop.”

Aaron examined the stop table.

Any fifth acquired relation. Any persistent-topology amplitude change above fifteen percent. Any phase displacement above 0.012 radians sustained for eight milliseconds. Any correlation between the acquired topology and active Storage envelopes. Any identity deviation, unresolved return, undeclared exchange, or independent stop. Maximum exposure per block: thirty-two milliseconds. Aggregate active interval: three hundred eighty-four. Full cold reset after every fourth block.

“Why not thirteen?” Rhys asked from the door.

He entered with his cane in his left hand and a thin paper folder under his right arm. His right hand provided pressure against the folder but no useful grip. The morning medication interval had improved his gait without removing the pause before each turn.

Amara answered. “A fourth randomized control would improve discrimination. The aggregate interval would exceed Mercer’s post-exposure ceiling.”

“And fewer?”

“Would leave either the refuge structures or the closing constraints unresolved as a class.”

Rhys took the chair nearest the door. He did not set the cane beyond reach. “The source-operation hold remains unchanged.”

“Yes,” Aaron said. “Both mechanical shutters remain closed. No live carrier exists inside the protocol.”

“If a response occurs?”

“We repeat the triggering block once after a cold reset if all stop measures return to baseline. We do not vary it, extend it, or compare it against a newly composed structure.”

“One confirmation.”

“One.”

Rhys opened the paper folder. The top page carried his medical office’s green diagonal stripe. He moved it beneath the protocol without comment.

“The difference between confirmation and pursuit,” he said, “has historically depended on who writes the next protocol.”

“Then the next protocol will require authority outside this room,” Amara replied.

Rhys read the practitioner limits. He initialed the continued source hold, the single-repeat ceiling, and the requirement that all twelve block identities remain sealed until after joint interpretation had been recorded.

His signature narrowed toward the end.

“Approved,” he said. “Local reconstruction only.”

He pushed himself upright with his left hand.

Aaron checked the wall clock. “Your transport is at eight.”

Rhys’s attention paused on him. “My transport office has achieved an impressive expansion of its reporting scope.”

“Amara moved the authorization review to seven twelve.”

“Because your transport is at eight,” Amara said.

Rhys accepted his cane from the table. “Then my decline continues to support effective interdisciplinary coordination.”

He left with the green-striped page still concealed inside the folder.

No one described the appointment as ordinary.

Conduit Rooms One and Two shared a wall and no active field path until Aaron created one.

Room One retained the inclined practitioner cradle, conductive panels, and blue coolant lines from Saturday’s source operation. The dry contacts had been replaced. A new medical adhesive packet sat open beside Varga’s station. Room Two held Amara upright in a restraint chair designed for Storage practitioners, whose safest response to unexpected retained load involved remaining where the return geometry expected them.

The local reconstruction lattice occupied Room Three. Four resonant-ceramic rings surrounded an empty center. A flywheel, charged isolation stack, facility-ground path, and west thermal loop supplied every declared exchange. The empty center prevented a technician from mistaking representational completeness for a place where something should be inserted.

Fadel joined by read-only channel from lunar control. He held the block key, independent reconstruction stop, and no ability to open either mechanical shutter.

Varga checked Aaron’s language sequencing and threat-response baseline. Headache one. Motor variance three percent. Identity correlation zero.

Amara reported active Storage load zero and acquired topology at 0.00039 gain. The four relations expressed beneath the diagnostic pulse in their usual order, separated by intervals the live controller neither created nor used.

Aaron stated his function.

“I will join the local reconstruction lattice and two staged Storage envelopes while preserving their independent boundaries and returns. I will admit only one authenticated block at a time. I will not create a source path, supply a missing relation, compose blocks, or treat the acquired topology as an available system. I close on any declared stop or uncertainty about whether a relation belongs to playback, Storage, or baseline.”

Amara followed.

“I will preserve each admitted block without translation or initiated transfer and return it through its assigned destructive-randomization path. I will not use, vary, or answer the four acquired relations. I release on change above the declared thresholds, lost origin, fifth-relation recruitment, active retention, unresolved order, or any independent stop.”

The lattice lead read the accounting.

“Isolation reserve sixteen point eight kilojoules. Flywheel at twelve thousand revolutions per minute. Maximum available mechanical differential one point two kilojoules. Thermal margin four point six. Controller bus and charge return independent. No source bearing, external reservoir, or undeclared receiver.”

Aaron placed both hands on the contacts.

“Proceed.”

The Conduit translated the three rooms into permissions.

Room Three’s isolation stack could spend charge to establish a playback boundary. Its flywheel could accept impulse if the reconstructed field relation coupled to chamber mass. The thermal loop could receive switching loss. Amara’s first envelope could retain relation order; her second preserved its release distinction. Aaron’s Coupling kept playback and Storage compatible without allowing either to redefine the other’s limits.

Pressure formed behind his left eye.

“Boundary stable,” he said. “Headache two.”

“Storage four,” Amara said. “Acquired topology at baseline.”

Fadel admitted Block Eight.

Thirty-one relations unfolded across the local lattice. Aaron perceived constraint without content: several independently controlled boundaries sharing a transfer path, then separating. Amara preserved the order for twenty-four milliseconds and released it into the ceramic stack. Geneva spent nine hundred fourteen joules on construction and destructive return. Eight hundred two returned through charge recovery. One hundred twelve entered coolant and controller loss.

Her acquired relations did not move.

Block Three contained a recursive classifier. Aaron recognized its functional austerity: input, test, constrained response, no reflected model of uncertainty. The local reconstruction possessed no reach beyond Room Three and nothing to classify. Amara returned it after twenty-six milliseconds. The flywheel accepted eleven joules during closure and returned ten; bearing heat accounted for the remainder.

No response.

Block Eleven preserved five positions around one physical decision, then removed independent withdrawal. Its topology approached Consensus in resolution and failed in agency. Aaron’s threat-response index rose by four percent despite knowing the block could not recruit either practitioner.

Amara’s four relations remained stable.

They completed the first cold reset.

Technicians discharged the ceramic rings, grounded the isolation stack, slowed the flywheel below operational threshold, flushed seventeen kilojoules of accumulated heat through the west loop, and destructively randomized working memory. The block key never entered Geneva. When the controller rebuilt, it knew how to authenticate Block Two and nothing about what Block Two contained.

The procedure consumed forty-three minutes to remove less than one second of operational history.

Aaron approved the ratio.

During the reset, he and Amara occupied the observation corridor between Rooms One and Two. Two panes of laminated glass separated them from the technicians grounding the ceramic rings. The corridor’s terrazzo floor retained three parallel scratches from an instrument rack removed before either of them worked in Geneva. Every renovation had polished around the marks. Facilities could erase a machine more cheaply than the route by which it once entered.

Amara drank water from a paper cup. “You wrote the acquired-topology exclusion in the first person.”

Aaron knew the clause she meant. *I will not treat the acquired topology as an available system.*

“It constrains my function.”

“It also describes my architecture.”

“Your declaration constrains your function separately.”

“That is not my point.”

The technicians opened the isolation-stack ground. A row of amber indicators went dark from left to right.

Five years earlier, Aaron had observed Amara’s first persistent source correlation, suspended her work, and spent eleven hours determining how much evidence he could withhold while still claiming to protect her. He eventually told her because waiting would occupy the decision she had not yet received. The lesson had entered later protocols as procedure: practitioner notification, new operation and new consent, no inherited availability.

Procedure preserved a result after someone paid for learning it.

“The clause prevents me from incorporating the four relations into the Coupling boundary even if the playback makes them legible,” he said. “Your clause prevents you from using them intentionally. Either one closes the session.”

“Better.” Amara folded the cup’s rim inward. “But if the playback makes them legible to me before the instruments register it?”

“You stop.”

“On what declared basis?”

“Uncertainty about whether a relation belongs to playback, Storage, or baseline.”

“Your stop condition.”

“The condition applies to the operation. It does not become mine because I stated it.”

Amara watched the final charge indicator extinguish. “That sentence took you five years.”

“The protocol review took nineteen days.”

“Naturally.”

Neither of them called the difference progress. Aaron could measure it without the label.

Blocks Two, Nine, and Six produced no baseline change. One presented a refuge partition with every retained relation locally silent. Aaron could not identify which of the three carried it during operation, but he recognized a shape close enough to the Node’s compartmentation that his Conduit attempted to classify the exterior boundary as source-adjacent. The keyed detector rejected the classification. No source checksum appeared.

Block Four began with Amara’s absent position.

Aaron recognized that much in twelve milliseconds. The reconstruction preserved a functional place for Storage without instantiating it, exactly as the Node had done during the first interval on Day 4,377. The block then nested the empty position inside seven locally separated continuities and placed Aaron’s temporary compatibility relation along their exterior.

Amara’s active envelopes held the playback at fourteen percent load.

Her acquired topology shifted.

The four relations did not gain amplitude. They did not recruit a fifth or retain any energy from the playback. Instead, the interval between the second and third relations shortened by 0.018 milliseconds while the interval between the fourth and first lengthened by the same amount. The sequence preserved total duration and changed where its return resolved.

“Baseline phase displacement,” the lattice lead said. “Zero point zero one four radians.”

Above threshold.

“Stop,” Aaron and Amara said together.

The stop entered both practitioner boundaries.

Amara released the active block through the assigned path. Aaron held playback, Storage, and local returns apart until the ceramic stack randomized the last source-derived ordering. The isolation reserve spent 1.7 kilojoules. Charge recovery returned 1.49. Coolant and switching accounted for two hundred nine joules. The flywheel remained disengaged.

He uncoupled the rooms.

Walls resumed their ordinary responsibilities.

“Storage clear,” Amara said.

The four acquired relations returned to their prior intervals in thirty-seven milliseconds.

Varga began identity and cognitive checks. Amara named the room, date, block number, active-load peak, and reason they had stopped. She reconstructed a six-item sequence by presentation and operational priority. Aaron passed language, motor, and identity screening. His headache held at three.

No instrument found injury.

No instrument explained the response.

They waited through the full cold reset before opening the blind.

Fadel retained the block identities. Aaron and Amara entered their independent observations first.

Aaron wrote: **FOUR-RELATION BASELINE UNDERWENT ZERO-NET-DURATION PHASE REDISTRIBUTION DURING BLOCK FOUR. NO AMPLITUDE GAIN, FIFTH RELATION, ACTIVE RETENTION, IDENTITY CORRELATION, OR UNDECLARED EXCHANGE. RETURNED TO BASELINE AFTER CLOSURE.**

Amara’s entry arrived beside it.

**ACQUIRED TOPOLOGY ALTERED INTERNAL ORDERING WITHOUT ENTERING EITHER DECLARED STORAGE ENVELOPE. RESPONSE CROSSED PROSPECTIVE STOP THRESHOLD AND RESOLVED SPONTANEOUSLY AFTER PLAYBACK RETURN. MECHANISM UNKNOWN.**

Their descriptions agreed without assigning the event a purpose.

Varga cleared the one permitted repeat after the acquired relations remained at baseline for seventy-two minutes and both practitioners completed a second medical screen. She did not call the first event harmless.

“Absence of resolved damage supports the repeat authorized before the finding,” she said. “It does not support a third operation designed after it.”

“Agreed,” Amara said.

Aaron agreed too.

The reset completed at 11:26.

Fadel admitted Block Four without revealing its content.

The same absent Storage position opened inside the locally reconstructed boundary. The seven separated continuities remained unavailable to one another. Aaron’s compatibility relation crossed their exterior without joining their contents.

At eleven milliseconds, Amara’s four relations redistributed phase.

The interval change measured 0.017 milliseconds. Phase displacement reached 0.013 radians. Total duration remained fixed. Amplitude held at 0.00039 gain. No fifth relation, active load transfer, retained energy, or identity correlation entered the trace.

“Confirmed,” Amara said.

Aaron closed at seventeen milliseconds, before the automatic thirty-two-millisecond limit and before anyone could convert available observation time into a reason to use it.

The local accounts returned. Amara’s topology reached baseline in thirty-four milliseconds. Medical measures remained unchanged.

The blind ended.

Fadel released the block key and reconstruction map together. Block Four contained no Quieter classifier and none of the nine Consensus constraints. It contained the Node’s representation of its own partitioned continuities, Amara’s deliberately unfilled place in the prior paired opening, and Aaron’s temporary path between concealment and coherence.

The response began when the empty Storage position entered that structure.

It ended when the reconstructed relation closed.

They tested explanations in decreasing order of administrative convenience.

First: block-generation error. Fadel reconstructed Block Four from the signed Day 4,377 trace on the lunar cold processor and compared it bitwise with Geneva’s received file. The relational sequence matched. Transport packaging added authentication and no executable transform.

Second: controller cross-talk. The lattice lead overlaid switching, cooling-pump, charge-return, and diagnostic clocks from all twelve blocks. Blocks One and Ten placed larger transients within two milliseconds of the acquired-topology pulse and produced no phase displacement. Block Four’s first response preceded Geneva’s return-controller action by seven milliseconds. The repeat preceded it by six.

Third: practitioner anticipation. Amara had helped define the scientific transforms and knew two blocks contained Node refuge geometry, but Fadel’s recoding prevented her from identifying either by number. Her active-load report showed no divergence before threshold. Heart rate and gaze supplied no precursor. Aaron recognized Block Four before the instrument announced the response, but his Coupling boundary contained no permitted path into her baseline topology. Recognition did not acquire one by being sincere.

Fourth: general similarity to the four-state sequence. Three other blocks preserved equivalent relation count and closer local phase density. None displaced the acquired order. Block Seven contained the exact historic four-state sequence as a noncausal control and produced no response. Similar appearance failed.

Fifth: retained energy returning to a matched state. Amara’s baseline carried no measured energy to return. Block Four drew 1.7 kilojoules from Geneva, returned 1.49 through the isolation stack, and assigned the remainder to controller work and heat. The missing accounting margin measured under two joules, four times narrower than required by protocol and six orders of magnitude too large to describe a topology whose diagnostic expression remained sub-threshold. Better accounting would not turn relation into fuel.

Each exclusion reduced the number of ordinary errors available.

None supplied a mechanism.

Amara studied the alignment. “Run the controller-removal transform.”

Fadel did. The timing correlation tightened by 0.002 radians.

“Clock cross-coupling?” Aaron asked.

The lattice lead substituted the independent optical clock. The response kept Amara’s baseline order rather than Geneva time.

“Diagnostic injection?”

The acquired-topology pulse followed the same schedule in every block. Only Block Four displaced.

Amara enlarged the four relations. “Active Storage load never touched them.”

“No measured exchange path connects the playback envelope to the baseline topology,” Aaron said.

“Yet its internal timing changed with the playback.”

“Yes.”

Resonance offered the nearest technical category: one system’s state varying in selective relation to another without implying shared identity or imported energy. The word also encouraged people to mistake a correlated response for an explanation.

Amara closed the amplitude layer and left timing visible. “If we call this resonance, someone will assume we understand what resonates.”

“Then we call it a selective phase response.”

“Which means resonance with more syllables.”

“The additional syllables carry uncertainty.”

“That is why institutions fund them.”

No humor altered the trace.

The response demonstrated one thing the previous five years had not: the acquired topology could discriminate among presented structures. It did not merely follow every Storage release. It responded selectively when the reconstructed disclosure represented the Node’s partitioned self, preserved Amara’s absent function, and placed Aaron’s compatibility path around both.

The result did not show which element mattered. The protocol prohibited composition, deletion, or variation of Block Four. It did not show whether the response originated in Amara’s Standard Five architecture, in a persistent source-derived relation, or in an interaction neither model described. It did not establish stored information, intention, harm, future function, or communication from the Node.

Aaron entered every exclusion.

He also separated the response from the Standard Nine finding. The nine closing constraints had passed through Amara’s active envelopes without altering her baseline. Whatever the conditional Consensus topology meant for humanity’s future, the present event did not appoint Amara to it, increase her Standard, or create a plural controller. She remained one Standard Five practitioner with four unexplained relations and complete independent stop authority.

The distinction deserved explicit language because institutions promoted anomalies by naming them capacity. An unusual measurement became a candidate function; a candidate function became an operational resource; an operational resource became a staffing assumption. Aaron had watched the progression often enough to identify its first sentence.

He wrote instead: **NO NEW PRACTITIONER CAPABILITY DEMONSTRATED OR IMPLIED.**

Amara added another.

**NO BASIS TO TREAT PRACTITIONER AS A SOURCE INTERFACE.**

The sentence protected her from the most operationally attractive misreading.

Rhys returned at 12:04. His transport had delivered him to the medical office and back. The trip had taken four hours. The green-striped folder no longer occupied his hand.

He read the independent observations before looking at the composite trace.

“Can it be reproduced without Sen?” he asked.

“The topology does not appear outside her baseline,” Aaron said.

“Can the triggering block be subdivided?”

“Technically, yes. Not under this authorization.”

“Would subdivision tell you what responded?”

“It could distinguish the absent Storage position, the seven-continuity structure, and my exterior compatibility path as triggers. It would not establish mechanism or meaning.”

“And it would expose her acquired topology to structures designed after observing its response,” Varga said.

Rhys lowered himself into the chair by the door. The movement required both the cane and the table edge. When he settled, his breathing took three cycles to return to its earlier depth.

“Recommendation,” he said.

Amara answered first. “Record the response, preserve the native traces, prohibit operational use, and return my paired exposure status to review. No source contact. No adaptive reconstruction.”

Rhys looked at Aaron.

The next experiment existed already in outline: factor Block Four, vary the empty position, present the refuge structure without Aaron’s exterior relation, invert the acquired sequence, test whether Standard Nine’s independent positions changed the response. Each step could convert selective correlation into a mechanism.

Or convert a stable, unexplained relation into an active one.

Five years earlier, Aaron had delayed telling Amara about the first persistent finding because more data promised a safer decision. The additional data had improved the question and occupied part of her choice before she received it. He did not need to repeat the sequence to recognize it.

“I support her recommendation,” he said. “The finding justifies preservation, not pursuit.”

Amara’s attention remained on the trace.

Rhys authenticated the hold with his left hand. He extended the existing Mercer-Sen source partition to the two local reconstructions and required any further protocol to receive independent medical, scientific, source-security, and initiating review. The standing elective-contact prohibition remained intact. Amara retained authority to decline without producing a technical basis.

The institution acquired a new fact and no new permission.

At 12:31, Fadel transferred both native traces to cold storage. The twelve block files entered separate custody. Geneva’s room processor destructively erased its key schedule, spent 3.2 kilojoules randomizing volatile state, and sent the resulting heat through the west loop. The mechanical shutters never opened.

Aaron and Amara remained in Analysis Room Four after the others left.

The red memory-destruction cover caught light from the ceiling. Beneath the floor, the optical return still ended in ceramic. The room had held a representation of the Node’s deepest surviving architecture without acquiring any path to the Node itself.

Amara closed the display.

“You noticed the next experiment before Rhys asked,” she said.

“Yes.”

“So did I.”

“I assumed you had.”

“That makes stopping easier?”

Aaron considered the question.

Shared recognition removed the possibility that one of them had simply failed to see the route. It also removed the comfort of treating restraint as ignorance.

“No,” he said. “It makes the stop accurate.”

Amara gathered the paper protocol. “Better.”

At the door, she paused for less than half a second. Nothing in the interval exceeded her normal cadence.

“Resonance is not a message,” she said.

“No.”

“It is not inert either.”

Aaron looked at the dark display.

The four relations had answered no question. They had supplied no image, word, instruction, or readable content. For two brief intervals, they had changed their internal timing in the presence of a locally reconstructed shape, then returned exactly to what the instruments called baseline.

Baseline no longer meant inactive.

“No,” he said.

They left the room without opening it again.
