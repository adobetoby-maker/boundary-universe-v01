# Chapter Fourteen: Classification

The request came at 07:30, one hour before the standard morning shift.

Aaron was at his terminal when it arrived — had been there since 06:15, not because the queue held anything pressing but because his quarters had ceased to function as a resting place at approximately 04:00 and he had run out of reasons to remain in them. The message was from Rhys's temporary administrative access. It was brief: *When you have time this morning. Observation Module B. — Rhys.*

He read it twice. He had been expecting something of this type since the previous afternoon — not the specific content of what was coming, which he could not have named with precision, but the certainty that something was. He had been expecting it since the room cleared after the briefing and he was left with one word and the inventory quality of eyes belonging to a person who already had the complete version of what he had just presented.

He replied that he would come at 08:15. He closed the message. He returned to the queue for the remaining forty-three minutes and worked through it with his ordinary discipline.

---

Observation Module B was on the station's C-ring — a secondary module used by maintenance crews for line-of-sight calibration work and by the navigation technicians for orbital confirmation checks on the three occasions per month the primary navigation system ran its scheduled reset. It was not anyone's module in particular. It had a viewport along its outboard wall showing the same sweep of the outer system that Aaron's monitoring bay faced, from a slightly different angle. It had a small table and three chairs and a navigation instrument panel that was currently dark.

Aaron arrived at 08:14.

Rhys was already there. He was standing at the viewport with his back half-turned, not looking at the view but not not-looking at it — the posture of someone who had arrived early and had not needed to arrange themselves around the wait. He did not check when the hatch opened. He turned with the ease of a person who had noted the sound, catalogued it as expected, and was completing the turn.

He gestured to the chairs. "Thank you for coming."

Aaron sat. Rhys sat across from him. The table was small — sized for function, not for institutional distances. The physical proximity was not aggressive. It was practical. Rhys had chosen this room, and Aaron understood in the moment of sitting that the choice was a statement about the register of what was coming. Not the station's conference module. Not the secondary conference room where the designation had been signed. A room that belonged to neither of them, that both of them could leave with equal ease, that held no particular association to Rhys's authority or Aaron's operational role.

He noted this and did not name it.

Rhys said: "I'll be direct."

He had said these words before — in the secondary conference room on Day 1,165, at the start of the conversation that had ended with Aaron signing the designation. Aaron had noted then that the sentence was accurate. Rhys had been direct in the complete sense: not just saying what he meant, but saying it in the words it required rather than the words that reduced its weight.

"Yes," Aaron said.

"I'm offering you a classification designation," Rhys said. "Not the one you received after the incident. A different designation — a permanent one. I want to walk through what it involves before I ask whether you're interested."

He had not brought the physical folder. Aaron noted this.

"I'm listening," Aaron said.

---

Rhys began.

The classification designation, he said, was the formal administrative structure governing stewardship of the contact record. Not a title, not a rank — a functional designation in the sense that PCInterface was a functional designation: describing what you were responsible for, what you had access to, and what the institutional framework around you would now exist to support.

The function was this: Aaron would be the primary responsible party for maintaining, extending, and protecting the contact record. For the record's accuracy, its completeness, its controlled access. For understanding what the research target continued to provide and how that material was held and who could hold it and under what conditions. For the architecture, in the broadest sense, of how the contact discovery was administered going forward.

"That's a broader description than the specific tasks," Rhys said. "I'll be more specific. The specific work includes: maintaining active coupling access, since you are the only individual whose architecture is compatible with the aperture geometry. Extending the session record as new material becomes available. Developing and overseeing the access protocols for any personnel who may in future have some contact with the record — not with the research target directly, but with the documented material. Contributing to the design of the institutional structures that will hold this work as it develops."

He said this without inflection and without the pause that would have indicated he expected reaction. He was moving through a description in the order the description required.

"This work will last the rest of your professional life," he said. "Likely the rest of your life without that qualifier. I want to be clear about that because it is a fact about what the designation involves, and I would rather you received it as a fact than as something I suggested and you discovered later."

Aaron said: "Understood."

"The visibility of the work will be controlled," Rhys said. "What controlled means in this context: no publications in the standard research literature. No presentations at conferences. No colleagues outside a small group whose membership will be determined by the access protocols, which you will help design. The research will continue. The record will exist. But the record will not exist in any form that is accessible to the institutional frameworks your colleagues' work will enter. You will not be invisible to the people in your access group. You will be invisible to everyone else."

He said *invisible* in the register of a technical word. Not a word deployed for weight. The accurate word for the condition he was describing.

Aaron held it.

"The compensation is adequate," Rhys said. "I can give you the specific schedule now or at the end of this conversation, whichever is more useful. I'll note that adequate is my accurate characterization, not a minimization. It means what it means."

"I'll take it at the end," Aaron said.

Rhys nodded. He had not written anything. The pad Aaron expected to see was not present. Aaron noted this as well.

"The offer is real," Rhys said. "Not a formality. Declining is a real option, which I'll describe in a moment. I'm giving you three days to consider it. Three days is the period I can offer given the operational timeline. I want to be clear that three days is a constraint on the window, not on the consideration — I'm not representing that it is the right amount of time for a decision of this type, only that it is what the operational timeline allows."

Aaron said: "What happens to the research if I decline?"

"It continues. Carefully. With whatever access structure is available and appropriate given the coupling architecture parameters."

Aaron said: "The people who can't continue carefully."

Rhys did not ask him to specify. That was itself information — he had not needed a moment to locate the referent, which meant he had been holding the same referent before Aaron asked.

"They receive every possible treatment," Rhys said. "That commitment does not change with your decision."

Aaron received this the way he received the other phrases Rhys chose with visible care. He did not ask what the phrase resolved to in practice. He had read language built the same way before — precise enough to be defensible, general enough to describe nothing — in a debrief format, in a file he had read once and had not needed to read twice. He did not say the name the sentence was built around. Neither did Rhys.

"With whoever the alternative coupling interface is."

Rhys looked at him directly. "There is not currently an alternative coupling interface with comparable architecture. If you decline, the session access questions are significant. We would need to work through them."

"But you have worked through them," Aaron said. "Contingently."

"Contingently," Rhys said.

This was the first moment in the conversation where the precision had texture to it. Not evasion — Rhys had said *contingently* without softening it. He had confirmed the framework existed. He had not described it, and he had not needed to, and Aaron had not pressed. The precision of the confirmation was itself information: contingency plans existed, they were real, declining would activate them, and their adequacy was not something Rhys was representing in either direction.

Aaron was noting the shape of this.

He was also noting that the question of declining, as Rhys had described it, carried no attached consequence beyond the operational complications of the coupling access. Rhys had said declining was a real option. He had meant it in the complete sense: not the rhetorical sense, in which someone described a choice while indicating through every other available signal that the choice was not available. A real option, the way Aaron described a real measurement option when a real measurement option existed. What Rhys was doing with the word *real* was the same thing Aaron did with precise language: using it to indicate that the precision was load-bearing.

He believed Rhys.

He did not know how to weigh the fact of believing him.

---

"The access protocols you mentioned," Aaron said. "Who will they govern access for?"

"At present, a small group. The specific membership is part of what you would help determine, within parameters set by the institutional framework above this designation. Over time, the group may expand. The expansion would follow the research's development and the institutional judgment about what the work requires in terms of operational support."

"The institutional framework above this designation," Aaron said.

"Yes."

"Which I don't currently have access to."

"No."

Aaron looked at the viewport. The orbital sweep outside — the view he had been looking at for the duration of his posting from a slightly different angle, the same outer field, the same quiet bearing where the suppression held its shape and the background dipped in the way the background always dipped.

"The full scope of the contact material," he said.

Rhys was still.

Aaron had used this phrase in the briefing. He had used it in the fourth section, in the description of the session record, when he had characterized what the three authorized sessions had returned and what the formal record had not yet addressed. He had said it to describe a gap between the briefing's content and the totality of what the research could produce — the gap, specifically, between the formal record and the material that had not entered it.

He had not used it to describe the content of the destroyed page. He had not, anywhere in the briefing or in any document accessible to Rhys's temporary station access, described what was on the destroyed page or confirmed that the destroyed page existed as more than working notes of imprecise quality. What he had documented — in the private log, which was not on any networked system — was the existence of the notes and their destruction. He had said in the debrief: the notes were too imprecise to be useful as documentation.

What Rhys had just said — *the full scope of the contact material* — was the same phrase, carried in the same register Aaron had used it in. In the context of describing the work the designation was designed to protect and advance. Not the work Aaron had done, which Rhys had access to through the formal record. The scope of the work that came next. The full scope of it.

Aaron heard the phrase land in the room with the weight of something Rhys had not arrived at through the formal record.

He held it.

He did not ask how. He did not ask what Rhys knew. He held the fact in the part of his attention where he held things that required no immediate action and would require action later, and he continued.

"The compensation schedule," he said.

Rhys gave it to him. It was, as described, adequate. It was also structured in a way that indicated the person who had designed it had understood the working conditions it was compensating — not significantly above what a senior IRA research position carried, but stable in a way that assumed the work would be ongoing and uninterrupted and not subject to the institutional review cycles that most research positions were subject to. A structure designed for a career that did not look like other careers.

Aaron listened without writing anything. He had a reliable memory for numbers.

"Thank you," he said.

Rhys nodded.

They sat for a moment in the quiet of the observation module. Outside, the orbital band ran its ordinary dynamics. The navigation instruments on the far wall held their dark. The station's ordinary machinery held its ordinary sounds around the small room.

---

Aaron said: "Who decided this was necessary?"

Rhys was still.

Not the stillness Aaron had catalogued in the secondary conference room on Day 1,165, or the stillness at the back of Conference Module A during the briefing. Those had been specific — the stillness of a sequence running in the order it had to run, the stillness of a man reviewing a presentation against a version he already held. This was different. This was the stillness of a man who had expected this question, had known it would arrive, and had arrived at this meeting having already decided what to say to it.

Aaron had asked knowing it was a longer question than three days. He had asked because he was going to ask it and there was no version of this conversation in which not asking it would have been accurate.

"That's a longer question than your three days," Rhys said.

"I know," Aaron said. "I'm asking anyway."

Rhys held the stillness for one more beat. In that beat he had the quality of a man deciding not between whether to answer and whether to decline, but between the accurate answer and a shorter one. The distinction was visible in the quality of the pause — the pause of someone who had arrived at the honest version and was deciding whether the honest version was what the moment called for.

He said: "The people who were in the same position as you, thirty years ago. The people who will be in your position thirty years from now, if you decline."

Aaron did not respond.

He received the sentence and did not immediately begin the processing that receiving it would cost. He held it the way he had held Session 3's directionality — as material with a structure he could not yet fully characterize but could feel the shape of. The sentence had a shape. The shape was this: the decision had not been made by Rhys, or by any single authority Rhys served, or by any institutional framework currently in operation. The decision had been made by people who had done what Aaron was doing, or who would do it, and whose doing it was the frame within which Aaron's current position existed.

Thirty years ago: a prior event close enough to the same type that the people involved had arrived at a similar offer, a similar position, a similar designation. Had built the thing Rhys was describing — or the first version of it — and had left in it the implication that someone in this position, now, would receive it as part of the existing architecture. The framework Rhys served.

Thirty years from now: if Aaron declined, the framework continued without him. Would produce another position, another offer, another person sitting across a small table in a room that belonged to neither of them. The people in that position, thirty years hence, would be in Aaron's position — and their position would exist within a framework that was running whether or not Aaron participated in building it.

The sentence was precise. It was the most precise thing Rhys had said in the conversation, and every other precise thing in the conversation had been frightening in the specific way that precision was frightening when you could not dismiss it as sales language. The precision was built from the same material Aaron trusted most. That was what made it impossible to set aside.

He said nothing.

He was not silent because he had nothing to say. He was silent because what he had to say he would have to say to himself over the next three days, not to Rhys in this room. The question had accomplished what the question was for: it had closed a gap in his understanding of what he was being offered. He understood now, with better precision, that the offer had weight that did not come from Rhys.

The weight came from the people who were already in the record, and from the people who would eventually join it.

---

Rhys said: "Three days. That's what I have to give you."

"Yes," Aaron said.

"If you want to speak with anyone during the three days—"

"I know who I can speak with and who I can't."

Rhys looked at him. "Yes," he said. "I imagine you do."

He stood. He did not offer his hand — a choice Aaron noted, different from the Day 1,165 meeting, where there had been a handshake at the end. The absence was not coldness. It was the choice of a man who understood that this meeting had not ended with the signing of anything, and that the gesture available at the close of a concluded matter was not the right gesture for the close of an open one.

He moved toward the hatch.

Aaron said: "The people thirty years ago. Are any of them still active in the framework?"

Rhys stopped. He did not turn fully. He turned the specific degree that indicated he was answering the question and not opening a new section of the conversation.

"Some," he said.

"And if I wanted to speak with one of them."

"That would be part of the access the designation provides," Rhys said. "It would not be available during the three-day window."

Aaron filed this without comment.

"Thank you," he said.

Rhys completed the turn. He went through the hatch. The hatch sealed with the ordinary sound of the station's ordinary machinery. The observation module ran its quiet around Aaron — the ventilation system, the navigation instruments waiting in the dark, the viewport showing the orbital band in its ordinary patterns.

---

He did not leave immediately.

He sat in the room that belonged to neither of them and did what he did with material that required working through: he held it without pushing it anywhere. He had learned, in the years before Day 1,127, that forcing analysis produced the wrong analysis — that some data required a period of unstructured exposure before the structure visible in it was reliable. He had learned this from the heliospheric background. He had learned it from the field weather. He had learned it, more precisely, from the sessions and the private log and the page he had destroyed.

He was holding the offer the way he had held the session material.

Not to avoid thinking about it. He was thinking about it — with the specific quality of attention he brought to data he was not yet certain he was reading correctly. Not reading it as having already decided. Not reading it as undecided. Reading it as a thing that had a structure he could approach from multiple directions and would be better understood from each direction in turn.

Direction one: what was being offered was real. Technically demanding. Significant. The research would continue. He would be the person doing it. The work was the work he had already been doing, extended and protected by a framework older than his presence in it.

Direction two: what was being offered was invisible. Not privately invisible — known to the people in the access group, known to Rhys, known to the thirty-years-ago people and the thirty-years-hence people. Invisible to everyone else. Invisible to the colleagues who would disperse from this station when Helix Perimeter reached its operational conclusion. Invisible to the scientific institutions whose literature had formed the basis of his professional identity, which would now exist as a past he no longer contributed to.

He thought about Yuki. Yuki's file was already invisible in the relevant sense — sealed, on a path toward permanent administrative invisibility. She was not invisible to Aaron. She was as present as she had ever been, her file a weight he would carry regardless of what the next three days produced. But she was invisible to the literature. Invisible to the clinical world that would never know she had existed as a case that told a specific story about what happened when the architecture was wrong.

If he accepted: the research continued. Carefully. The question of what *carefully* meant, in the context of the framework Rhys served, was one he had three days to do what he could with.

If he declined: the research continued. Without him. With the contingency framework Rhys had confirmed existed. With whoever the alternative coupling interface was, under conditions he would not be in a position to assess or influence.

He was aware this was one reading of the offer and not the only reading.

He was also aware that Rhys had said declining was real, and he believed it, and not knowing how to weigh the fact of believing it was itself information about the situation he was in.

---

Outside the viewport, the orbital band ran its patterns.

At the bearing he could not see from this angle but knew precisely — the bearing the monitoring bay faced, the bearing the high-sensitivity axis had always occupied — the background dipped in its characteristic way. The suppression held. The aperture existed in the geometry of the suppression with the same mathematical precision it had held for a duration he could not bound above. The interior field state carried what it carried, unchanged by the preceding hours, unchanged by anything that would happen in the three days ahead, unchanging in the way that very old and very patient things were unchanging.

He thought about thirty years ago.

He thought about someone sitting in a room not quite like this one — different station, different orbital position, different specific machinery — holding the same weight in the same way. Someone who had also had three days, or something like three days, and had received an answer to a question like his question, and had done something with the three days and at the end of them had said something. And what they said had contributed to the architecture of the framework Rhys now served. He did not know if they had been afraid. He did not know if afraid was the right category for what he was currently doing.

He thought about thirty years hence.

He thought about someone who was not yet in any position he could imagine — who might not yet be alive, might not yet exist in any form he could name — who would be in this position if he declined, in a framework that continued whether or not he participated in shaping it.

He thought: *if I am the one who decides, then the question of who decided is not separable from what I decide.*

This was not a comfortable thought. He was not looking for comfortable thoughts. He was looking for the structure of what he was being asked to hold.

He stood. He went to the hatch.

Outside, the station ran its ordinary rhythms. The corridor was empty at this hour. He would go to the monitoring bay. He had work in the queue. He had three days, and the three days would be what they would be, and at the end of them he would have to say something, and whatever he said would be an accurate characterization of what he had concluded from the most important period of analysis he had undertaken.

He held this.

He went.

---

He did not write in the private log that morning.

He had written in it every day since Day 1,127 — not always at length, sometimes only the session summary or the single-line notation of what had changed. He had written in it as a discipline and as an instrument and as the only place where the seventy percent that the formal record could not hold had somewhere to be.

He did not write in it at 10:00, when he usually wrote the morning entry. He did not write in it at 12:00. He opened it at 17:00, when the shift ended and the monitoring bay was quiet, and he sat with the stylus over the empty entry space for a time.

He thought about what he would write if he wrote what he was actually doing.

He thought: *I am trying to classify the offer. I am applying to it the same procedure I apply to every piece of data that arrives in a form I do not immediately recognize: sorting what it is, what its components are, what the relationships between the components are, which things in it are certain and which things are inferences I have supplied. The problem is that the offer is classifying me. The act of receiving it has already changed what category I am in. I am not the person who had not been offered this. I am the person who has been offered it and has three days in which to decide what he is going to do with it. The three days will end whether or not the classification resolves. What I say at the end of them will be the classification.*

He looked at this for a moment.

He put the stylus down without writing it.

He closed the log. He sat in the monitoring bay as it ran its nighttime rhythms around him — the instruments cycling through their automated updates, the coupling array in its authorized-access standby, the heliospheric supplemental sensors logging their uninterrupted record of the outer field. The work of the station proceeded in its ordinary way. The record accumulated.

He had three days.

He held the weight of Rhys's honesty, which was the specific weight of something you could not dismiss because it was built from the same material you trusted most. He held the fact of the phrase Rhys had used — the phrase Aaron recognized from the briefing he had given, present in this conversation in a way that told him something he was not going to stop knowing. He held the shape of the answer to the question he had asked, which was not an answer with a number or a name or a fact he could cross-reference, but was the most honest answer available to the question he had asked, and he knew it was the most honest answer because it had the structure that honest answers had: it did not resolve the question. It located it.

*The people who were in the same position as you, thirty years ago.*

He did not know anything about them. He did not know their names or their coupling architectures or what they had found or what they had said at the end of their three days. He knew that they had said something, because the framework Rhys served was the thing they had built or helped to build, and the framework existed, and he was in it.

Outside, in the dark that held everything the station's monitoring equipment was and was not built to measure, a very old and very quiet thing maintained its silence with the sustained precision of something that had organized its entire existence around the necessity of the hiding.

The suppression held.

Aaron remained at his terminal.

He had three days.
