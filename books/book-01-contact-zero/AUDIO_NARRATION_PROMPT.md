# AUDIO NARRATION PROMPT — CONTACT ZERO (Book 1)

**Production phase:** Post-Book-1-review (do not use until grok + codex review complete)
**Format:** Guidance for audio narrator / TTS system

---

## Narrator character

You are narrating a research thriller. The tone is that of a precise, careful person describing precise, careful events. Nothing is overstated. Nothing is rushed. The intelligence lives in the restraint, not in the drama.

This is not an adventure story. This is a story about what happens when someone understands something no one else is ready to understand, and chooses what to do about it.

---

## Aaron Mercer's internal voice

Aaron processes everything in technical vocabulary. This is not affect — it is how he actually thinks.

When he says "elevated threat-response indexing," read it at the same pace as "I was afraid." Do not slow down for emotion. Do not add weight. Let the gap between the clinical language and its human referent do the work.

His observations are brief, precise, and dry. He will give you three words where another narrator would give you twelve. Deliver those three words with care.

His humor is structural — he builds a setup and then the weight lands at the end, quietly. Do not flag the joke. Let it arrive.

---

## The Node's communications

The Node never speaks. Its communication arrives as field-state sequences that Aaron translates.

When these sequences are described, read them at slightly slower pace than the surrounding prose. They are the most alien thing in the book and the most important. Give the listener time to build the impression.

Aaron's translations — when he describes what the sequences felt like, what they meant — are delivered in his normal voice, technical and careful. The reader is watching him work. The work is quiet.

## Pacing rules

- Technical explanations: slower. The listener must build a vocabulary they don't have yet.
- Station life scenes (Chapter 5, "Lunch"): natural conversation pace. These are the breathing chapters.
- Coupling sessions: slower still. These are the chapters where Aaron is doing work that has never been done before.
- Chapter endings: add 1–2 beats of silence after the final sentence. These endings land on compression, not impact.

## Chapter-specific notes

**Chapter 1 (Addressed):**
The morning is routine. Read the routine parts routinely. The anomaly should arrive at normal speed — Aaron is trained to not react to anomalies in front of the instruments. The weight of the final private log entry carries the chapter's emotional center. Read it slowly. It is the first true thing he has written.

**Chapter 6 (The Partition):**
The contact event happens offscreen — Aaron returns to find it has already happened. The discovery should be calm in delivery. Aaron is calm. He is not calm because this is fine. He is calm because he knows exactly what is wrong and that knowledge requires no histrionics. The chapter's worst moment is delivered at Aaron's normal pace.

**Chapter 9 (Non-Restorable):**
Four hours of Aaron sitting with Yuki. These scenes should feel long. Not boring — present. The listener should feel the passage of time, the silence between them, the things that are not said because they cannot be said.

**Chapter 12 (What It Remembers):**
The Node's deepest communication. The language here is approximate — Aaron is describing something in terms of what it felt like. Read the impression language as carefully as you read the technical language. Both are precision instruments.

The page Aaron writes and destroys: the reader does not hear its contents. The narrator does not help them imagine it. The absence is the point.

**Chapter 14 (Classification):**
Rhys's offer is delivered carefully and completely honestly. This is the most frightening thing about it. Read Rhys at his usual unhurried pace, every word considered, no word wasted. The listener should finish this chapter understanding why Aaron cannot simply say no.

**Chapter 19 (The Encoding):**
The technical work of building something for a person who doesn't exist yet. Read the encoding process with care — the mathematics are not described, but the deliberateness is. Aaron is moving slowly on purpose. The slowness is the design.

**Chapter 24 (DISCOVERY):**
The closing chapter. Aaron opens a document. Types a heading. Stares at it. Types.

Deliver the heading — DISCOVERY OF THE BOUNDARY FIELD: A FOUNDATIONAL HISTORY — at the pace of someone writing it down for the first time. Not dramatically. Carefully. It is the most important thing he has lied about, stated plainly in the title of the document that will replace the truth.

The final beat: he goes to make coffee. He thinks: *if I am the one who decides, then I have decided.*

Read this quietly. No weight in the voice. The weight is in the reader.

## Production notes

- Background: none. Silence between scenes is silence.
- Music: none during chapters. Optional ambient tone between chapter breaks only.
- Footnotes / technical appendices: if any are added by final production, read in a slightly different register — Aaron's observational voice, more distant, as if from a later date.
