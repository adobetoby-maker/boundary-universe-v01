# Chapter 22 — Taren’s Choice

Taren Holt received the offer in a room with no windows.

He considered that on-brand.

The room belonged to Asterion Security, but the two people waiting inside did not.

One was Deputy Director Mina Orr from the academy’s external oversight office.

The other was a man Taren knew as Gabriel Holt.

Uncle Gabriel to the family.

Director Holt to the Continuity Directorate.

Taren stopped in the doorway.

Gabriel looked older than he had during the siege.

Not weaker.

Just more human than Taren preferred.

That complicated anger.

“Sit,” Orr said.

Taren did.

No one offered coffee.

Hostile process.

Gabriel placed a folder on the table.

Paper.

Real paper.

Taren almost laughed.

“Is the Directorate legally required to become theatrical when asking teenagers to ruin their lives?”

Gabriel’s mouth tightened.

“You are eighteen.”

“Recent development.”

Orr looked between them.

“This meeting is recorded. Mr. Holt requested counsel and declined it.”

Taren nodded.

“I know enough lawyers to make that feel worse.”

Gabriel opened the folder.

Inside were three documents.

First: reinstatement to full student status at Asterion.

Second: recommendation that no criminal referral be pursued for his pre-siege intelligence activity because he had been a minor when recruited and later materially assisted in stopping the Directorate attack.

Third: a confidential cooperation agreement.

Taren read the first paragraph.

Then the second.

He stopped joking.

The agreement did not ask him to spy on Kade.

That would have been easy to reject.

It asked him to provide “contextual risk reporting” on emerging cross-academy Consensus structures, international student networks, and foreign institutional influence.

No theft.

No covert access.

No sabotage.

Just observations.

Relationships.

Who trusted whom.

Who deferred to whom.

Who could become strategically important.

The exact kind of information Taren had once convinced himself was harmless.

Gabriel said, “This is not the old assignment.”

Taren looked at him.

“No. It has better nouns.”

“You would not access private systems.”

“I did not start with private systems last time.”

“You would not be asked to deceive Mercer.”

“I lied by omission for months.”

“You were fifteen.”

“Sixteen.”

Gabriel’s face changed.

“You remember everything except what helps.”

Taren almost smiled.

Family.

Orr said, “The offer is real. So are the consequences if you decline. The academy cannot guarantee full reinstatement before the investigation concludes. Travel restrictions remain. Your security clearance remains suspended. Several partner academies have already declined your participation.”

Taren looked at the first document.

Asterion full status.

Classes without escort.

House movement without check-ins.

International travel.

A future that did not require everyone to remember his worst decision before they remembered his name.

Gabriel said quietly, “You can help prevent another blind spot.”

That sentence hurt because part of it was true.

The Directorate had not invented risk.

Governments did need to understand Consensus.

Foreign institutions did have agendas.

People could exploit trust.

Taren knew that better than most.

“What happens to my reports?” he asked.

Gabriel answered too quickly.

“Strategic review.”

“Who gets them?”

“Restricted analysts.”

“Which analysts?”

Gabriel paused.

There.

Taren leaned back.

“Same building?”

“Taren.”

“Same Directorate?”

“The Directorate is under restructuring.”

“That is not an answer.”

Orr did not intervene.

Good.

Gabriel finally said, “Some of the same personnel remain.”

Taren looked at the cooperation agreement again.

Contextual risk reporting.

Harmless words.

People reduced to leverage maps.

He remembered House Trial.

His handler asking who would follow Kade if Kade made an unsafe call.

At the time, Taren had answered.

Because the question sounded operational.

He had not understood that someone wanted to know where the social control points were.

He understood now.

“Do I get to tell the people I am reporting on?”

Gabriel’s expression hardened.

“No.”

Taren closed the folder.

“Then no.”

Orr looked at him.

“Take time.”

“I did.”

“You have had the document for four minutes.”

“I have had the question for two years.”

Gabriel’s hands tightened on the table.

“You are throwing away protections I fought to secure.”

Taren felt that.

The easiest version of his uncle was a villain.

Gabriel had never been easy.

He had helped Taren get into Asterion.

Taken him hiking.

Remembered birthdays.

Taught him how to read a room.

Then used that same skill as recruitment criteria.

Two truths.

Again.

“I know,” Taren said.

Gabriel looked at him sharply.

Taren corrected himself.

“I believe you fought for them.”

“That is not enough?”

“No.”

This time the word stayed.

Not joke.

Not shield.

Choice.

---

Kade learned about the offer from Mara.

This annoyed both of them.

Mara found him after Vector Analysis and held up a message from Taren.

**I declined something stupid. Please do not make it heroic. Coffee later?**

Kade read it.

“What did he decline?”

“He did not say.”

“Why did he tell you first?”

“He sent it to both of us.”

Kade checked his tablet.

The message was there.

He had missed it beneath seven public requests and a Helix data notice.

That was worse.

Mara looked at him.

“Do not make missing one message into abandonment.”

“I was not.”

“You were beginning.”

“Profiling.”

“Yes.”

Kade sighed.

They met Taren in a small café near the harbor where Asterion students went when they wanted to pretend the island had ordinary businesses.

Taren arrived without an escort.

Kade noticed.

Taren noticed him noticing.

“Temporary privilege,” he said. “Oversight office approved independent transit for the meeting.”

Kade sat.

Mara sat across from Taren.

No one ordered for him.

Good.

Taren told them the offer.

Not every legal detail.

Enough.

Kade’s anger came fast.

“They wanted you to report on us again.”

Taren held up one finger.

“Careful.”

Kade stopped.

Taren continued.

“They wanted me to report on international Consensus networks. You would be part of that. So would Aya, Jonas, Neema, Arjun, faculty, maybe others.”

“Same thing.”

“No.”

Kade disliked the correction.

It mattered anyway.

Mara asked, “What did they offer in exchange?”

Taren told them.

Full status recommendation.

No criminal referral recommendation.

Travel review.

Security restrictions lifted early.

Kade felt the temptation on Taren’s behalf.

He hated that too.

“You said no.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Taren looked at him.

“You know why.”

Kade almost answered for him.

Did not.

Taren rubbed both hands around his coffee cup.

“Because the last time I told myself observation was not betrayal unless I touched the system.”

He looked at Mara.

“I learned what you eat when you are stressed. Who Kade listens to after he stops listening to faculty. When Eli lies about being fine. Which people Sera can move through a room without raising her voice.”

Kade’s skin crawled.

Taren saw.

“I know.”

Then he winced.

“Sorry. Bad phrase.”

Mara almost smiled.

Taren continued.

“None of that felt classified. It still became useful to people who wanted leverage.”

Kade looked down.

“And now?”

“Now I am tired of being good at something that makes people less free when I use it badly.”

That was not a clean redemption sentence.

Good.

It sounded like work.

Mara asked, “What does declining cost?”

Taren’s smile came back.

There it was.

“Probably my semester.”

Kade looked up.

“What?”

“Without the cooperation agreement, oversight will not accelerate reinstatement. I remain restricted until the full investigation closes. That may be months.”

“Can Sen change it?”

Taren stared at him.

Kade heard himself.

Right.

Fix it.

Again.

He exhaled.

“Sorry.”

Taren looked almost disappointed.

“I had a good speech ready.”

“Save it.”

“Cruel.”

“Accurate.”

Mara ignored them.

“Are you appealing?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Taren nodded.

He had not refused the Directorate in order to become passive.

Also good.

---

The cost arrived faster than expected.

Two days later the international academy consortium published its next cross-campus research roster.

Aya.

Mara.

Eli.

Julian.

Kade.

Sera as response observer.

Taren: **NOT ELIGIBLE — ACTIVE SECURITY REVIEW**

Taren read it in Meridian Commons.

Nobody pretended not to.

Eli said, “That is garbage.”

Sera said, “It is predictable.”

Julian said, “Those are not mutually exclusive.”

Kade looked at Taren.

Old instinct: make it move.

He did not.

Instead he asked, “What are you doing?”

Taren looked at the roster.

“Appealing the security designation.”

“Need help?”

Taren thought.

“Yes.”

Kade blinked.

Not the answer he expected.

Taren continued.

“I need statements about what I did during the siege and after. Not friendship statements. Operational facts.”

Kade nodded.

“That I can do.”

Mara said, “Same.”

Julian offered too.

Sera did not immediately.

Everyone noticed.

She looked at Taren.

“I will write what I saw. Including the things I still do not trust.”

Taren nodded.

“Please.”

That was better than loyalty.

It was evidence.

---

That night Taren called his mother.

Kade did not hear the call.

He heard about it later because Taren told him.

His mother cried.

Not because he had refused the Directorate.

Because Gabriel had called her first.

Family systems moved faster than governance.

She asked whether Taren was ruining his future for people who might never forgive him.

Taren said probably.

Then she asked whether the people mattered that much.

He did not answer quickly.

Kade asked what he finally said.

Taren looked embarrassed.

“I said that is not why I did it.”

“Why then?”

Taren looked toward the Meridian bell hanging over the common-room door.

“Because I want my future to belong to the person who lives it.”

Kade did not make a joke.

Taren noticed.

“Please never become emotionally mature around me again.”

“Temporary condition.”

“Good.”

The bell rang for dinner.

Taren stood.

His status band still glowed restricted yellow.

Nothing visible had improved.

That mattered.

He had made the right choice and still paid for it.

Kade followed him into the dining hall.

Trust had not been repaired.

But for the first time since the betrayal, Taren had chosen a harder future without asking anyone to reward him for it.

That was enough for one chapter.
