## A — Character first

### Best qualities
- Strongest sense that Northline is a real school populated by real kids rather than merely Kade's launch pad.
- Darius scene does several jobs simultaneously: humor, social intelligence, alternative-school context, and proof that Kade notices what people hide.
- Ms. Alvarez/Kade relationship has the most texture.
- Kade's defensive humor clearly reads as armor rather than generic snark.
- Best foundation for the novel's belonging theme.

### Risks
- Takes longest to reveal the speculative hook.
- A few explanatory paragraphs could be compressed for audio.
- The reader may initially categorize the novel as contemporary school fiction for slightly too long.

## B — Mystery first

### Best qualities
- Strongest first line and fastest genre signal.
- Opening inside the anomalous exam creates immediate questions.
- Flashback makes the reader interpret ordinary Northline scenes under a layer of impending strangeness.
- `Where does the system actually end?` is a memorable expression of the series physics and theme.
- Strongest thriller propulsion into Chapter 2.

### Risks
- The flashback structure spends some of the suspense twice.
- Northline becomes slightly more backstory than lived experience.
- Early mention of Aaron risks highlighting the father mystery before the story has earned it.
- Less room for the reader simply to like Kade before the plot claims him.

## C — Audio first

### Best qualities
- Strongest spoken cadence.
- Short paragraphs and controlled repetition make the chapter very easy to follow at 1x–1.5x playback.
- Darius, Ms. Alvarez and Kade are distinguishable by rhythm without needing exaggerated voice acting.
- Best line-level clarity during the test sequence.
- Repeated concepts (`two names`, `school loved answers that came out clean`, `if you laughed first`) produce useful audiobook memory anchors.

### Risks
- Slightly less visual/textural richness than A.
- The deliberately spare style could become monotonous over a 20-hour book if used at full intensity everywhere.
- A few aphoristic lines feel polished enough to call attention to the prose rather than disappear into Kade's POV.

# Editorial recommendation

Do **not** choose one unchanged.

Build the canonical Chapter 1 using approximately:

- **45% A:** scene order, Northline immersion, Darius, Ms. Alvarez, Kade's emotional characterization.
- **35% C:** sentence cadence, audio clarity, dialogue rhythm, restrained exposition.
- **20% B:** sharper anomaly language, earlier sense that something about the exam is wrong, and the final escalation.

## Recommended canonical structure

1. Open in Room Four, not inside the exam. The book's unique emotional hook is the kid the normal system discarded; we should meet that kid before learning he is extraordinary.
2. Keep the Darius attempted walkout nearly intact from A/C. It demonstrates Kade's real gift before any supernatural/physics gift: he models people and systems.
3. Keep Ms. Alvarez's challenge that Kade decides things do not matter before he can discover whether he can succeed. This directly seeds his series arc.
4. Give Resonance only enough exposition to establish that elite academies are culturally enormous and unimaginable to Kade.
5. During the test, borrow B's language around the hidden question but do not literally show a secret text prompt unless later canon establishes the test software can intentionally communicate that way. Prefer the interface behaving unexpectedly.
6. Use C's spoken cadence through the escalating simulations.
7. End with the national cascade and a proctor frightened by `000`, followed by Kade realizing for the first time that zero may not mean failure.

## Length note

These are **comparison drafts**, intentionally written before spending full polishing effort. The selected/hybrid canonical pass should be expanded and tuned to the architecture target of approximately **5,300 words**, with additional Northline texture, one more meaningful peer interaction, and slightly more breathing room around Kade choosing to genuinely try on the exam.
