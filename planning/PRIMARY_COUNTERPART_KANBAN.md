# PRIMARY COUNTERPART / SIDEKICK KANBAN

**Status:** PROVISIONAL planning layer
**Branch:** `planning/universe-kanban`
**Purpose:** give every origin protagonist one primary counterpart who becomes foundational in Book 1, develops independent agency across the trilogy, and enters the ensemble era as a person rather than an accessory.

---

## NORTH STAR

Each origin lead should have one primary counterpart introduced early enough that the relationship becomes part of the trilogy's emotional foundation, but late enough that readers first understand how the protagonist survives alone.

The counterpart is not a mascot, servant, comic appendage, or permanent subordinate.

They must eventually become capable of disagreeing with the lead, operating without the lead, and making decisions the lead cannot overrule.

Kade / Eli is the current benchmark pattern: Kade sees systems and boundary relationships; Eli measures, models, builds, and makes those ideas usable. Mara remains too independent and central to reduce to the counterpart slot.

---

# 1. INTRODUCTION TIMING RULE

## Default timing
Introduce the primary counterpart during **Book 1, roughly the first 20–30%**.

Recommended emotional rhythm:
1. Opening chapters establish the protagonist's wound, competence, worldview, and solo survival strategy.
2. Counterpart enters before the protagonist has fully adapted to the new story world.
3. Initial interaction creates friction, mismatch, obligation, rivalry, or unwanted usefulness.
4. By the midpoint, each has solved at least one problem the other could not solve alone.
5. By the Book 1 climax, they choose to rely on one another in a costly way.
6. By the Book 1 ending, the relationship is foundational but not complete.

### Hard gate
Every primary counterpart must be firmly established **before the end of Origin Book 1**.

Do not wait until Book 2 to introduce the person who is supposed to carry the trilogy's central partnership.

---

# 2. VARY THE ENTRY PATTERN

Do not introduce all five counterparts the same way.

Assign a different entry model to each origin line.

Possible models:
- **Existing friend** — knows the lead before the ability/world changes.
- **Assigned partner** — school, guild, military, medical, legal, expedition, or professional pairing.
- **Rival-to-partner** — initially competes against the lead and becomes indispensable.
- **Handler / observer** — starts with divided loyalties and must become a real friend.
- **Accidental ally** — trapped in the same crisis with no institutional reason to cooperate.
- **Local expert** — lead enters an unfamiliar world and needs someone who understands the ordinary rules.
- **Rescue inversion** — counterpart is introduced as someone the lead thinks they must save, then proves more capable in the relevant domain.
- **Reluctant mentor-peer** — one has expertise but not authority or emotional maturity over the other.

### Gate
Across the five origin trilogies, no more than two counterparts should share the same basic introduction pattern.

---

# 3. COUNTERPART CHARACTER CARD

Every origin counterpart gets a full card before the trilogy bible is attested.

Required fields:
- name / age / home culture / profession or school role;
- relationship to protagonist at introduction;
- first appearance chapter target;
- introduction pattern;
- pre-power or ordinary-world competence;
- ability / specialist discipline;
- why this ability complements rather than duplicates the lead;
- emotional wound;
- false belief;
- independent ambition;
- what they want that has nothing to do with the protagonist;
- moral line they will not cross for the protagonist;
- thing the protagonist repeatedly misunderstands about them;
- thing they understand about the protagonist before the protagonist does;
- Book 1 relationship turn;
- Book 2 divergence/conflict;
- Book 3 mature partnership state;
- ensemble-era role;
- reason they may sometimes work better with another origin lead than with their original partner.

### Hard gate
A counterpart fails design if removing the protagonist causes their entire life, ambition, and plot relevance to disappear.

---

# 4. POWER / METHOD PAIRING RULE

The lead and counterpart should differ primarily in **method**.

Preferred pattern:
- lead has the disruptive, rare, dangerous, or worldview-breaking interface;
- counterpart has a specialized, practical, measurable, culturally normal, or complementary discipline;
- lead changes what is possible;
- counterpart makes that possibility survivable, usable, testable, teachable, or ethically constrained.

Examples for design only — NOT CANON:
- Probability lead + Causality Anchor counterpart.
- Threshold Walker lead + Pattern Forger/map-maker counterpart.
- Vital Weaver lead + Identity Locker / diagnostic specialist counterpart.
- Memory Architect lead + Echo / evidence-verification counterpart.
- Temporal Perception lead + Momentum/physiology specialist counterpart.

### Gate
The counterpart must solve at least one Book 1 crisis using a capability the protagonist fundamentally lacks.

---

# 5. TRILOGY RELATIONSHIP LOOP

## Book 1 — FOUNDATION
Required beats:
- protagonist is understood alone first;
- counterpart enters by 20–30%;
- first useful collaboration occurs before full trust;
- one failure caused by misunderstanding each other's method;
- one ordinary-life bonding scene unrelated to the central mystery;
- one argument where both are partly correct;
- one costly voluntary reliance beat;
- end state: "we work together because we choose to," not permanent dependency.

## Book 2 — DIVERGENCE
Required beats:
- success creates different opportunities for each person;
- counterpart receives an independent path that could take them away;
- protagonist cannot assume access to their time, skills, secrets, body, loyalty, or future;
- counterpart disagrees on method or ethics in a way the plot validates at least partly;
- at least one major sequence separates them and proves both function independently;
- relationship survives disagreement without artificial breakup machinery.

## Book 3 — EQUALITY
Required beats:
- counterpart has independent authority / expertise / status;
- protagonist asks rather than assumes;
- counterpart leads at least one major operation;
- each can refuse the other without ending the relationship;
- final trilogy victory requires both, but neither is merely an extension of the other;
- end state prepares them to enter the ensemble era without being fused into one narrative unit.

---

# 6. ENSEMBLE REMIX RULE

When the five origin leads converge, do not preserve five permanent two-person pods.

The original partnerships are emotional home bases, not tactical handcuffs.

During the ensemble saga:
- remix lead/counterpart pairings by mission;
- let counterparts form friendships and rivalries with other leads;
- let leads discover another protagonist's counterpart understands something their own counterpart cannot;
- allow at least one counterpart to become a major ensemble POV/lead;
- give at least one counterpart command of a mission containing multiple origin leads;
- never require every counterpart to travel on every ensemble adventure.

### Band-growth target
By the middle of the ensemble saga, readers should think in terms of a **network of relationships**, not five hero-sidekick pairs.

# 7. CURRENT ORIGIN CARDS

## O1 — Kade Mercer / Asterion
**Primary counterpart benchmark:** Eli Navarro
**Status:** ESTABLISHED

### Why it works
- introduced early in Book 1 after Kade's isolation is established;
- Eli has engineering/tactical competence Kade lacks;
- independent civilian stabilization ambition;
- relationship is uncomplicated friendship rather than romance;
- Eli can challenge Kade's assumptions with data;
- Kade must respect Eli's consent around experiments and risk;
- Eli remains useful when Kade's rare architecture is irrelevant.

### Development rule
Do not retroactively narrow Eli into "Kade's sidekick." Preserve engineering fellowship, civilian stabilization, machine/signal interpretation, and eventual independent ensemble leadership opportunities.

# 8. REUSABLE COUNTERPART KANBAN

Each counterpart card moves through:

`OPEN → ENTRY MODEL → CHARACTER CARD → ABILITY/METHOD PAIR → BOOK 1 PLACEMENT → TRILOGY ARC → ENSEMBLE ROLE → ATTESTED`

### Definition of ATTESTED
- introduction timing is known;
- introduction pattern is not repetitive across the portfolio;
- independent ambition is concrete;
- protagonist cannot replace their specialty;
- they solve at least one crisis without the protagonist;
- Book 2 creates divergence rather than permanent orbit;
- Book 3 ends in chosen equality;
- ensemble role includes at least one relationship outside the original pair;
- character remains compelling if crossover material is removed.

---

# 9. PORTFOLIO AUDIT

Before Origin Lead 2 Book 1 prose begins, verify:
- Lead 2 counterpart is fully attested.

Before any later origin Book 1 prose begins, verify:
- every active origin has a counterpart plan;
- entry models remain varied;
- no ability pairing duplicates Kade/Eli's exact dynamic;
- no counterpart is secretly only a romance vehicle;
- at least one counterpart across the five origins is positioned to outgrow the "sidekick" label and become a major ensemble lead.
