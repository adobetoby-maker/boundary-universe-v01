# CRAFT TECHNIQUE INTEGRATION — BOUNDARY UNIVERSE

**Status:** ACTIVE production guidance
**Purpose:** capture transferable storytelling techniques from progression-SF/fantasy exemplars without imitating protected expression, characters, settings, or scene designs.

## 1. TRANSFERABLE WARFORMED-STYLE STRENGTHS

### W-001 — Visible Progression Delta
When a character improves, show the reader a clear before/after difference in what they can perceive, decide, execute, endure, or coordinate.

The delta may be:
- measurable performance;
- a changed tactical option;
- a failed technique becoming viable;
- reduced cost;
- better team interoperability;
- improved judgment rather than raw power.

Never rely only on another character saying the protagonist improved.

### W-002 — Action Geography Before Speed
Before fast combat/rescue/field action, establish:
- objective;
- physical layout;
- important actors;
- constraints;
- failure condition.

Once geography is legible, sentences may accelerate.

### W-003 — Distinct Team Competence
Every recurring team member needs a recognizable problem-solving identity. In a major team scene, the reader should know why each person is present.

Avoid interchangeable support characters.

### W-004 — Competence Surprise / Catharsis
Progression payoff should occasionally let a previously underestimated character reveal earned competence in public.

The moment must be seeded by training, choices, and prior limitation. It cannot be an unearned power reveal.

### W-005 — Training Must Change Story State
No training bout or tournament scene exists only because progression fiction is expected to contain one.

Every such scene must change at least one of:
- capability;
- relationship;
- status;
- knowledge;
- strategy;
- future opportunity;
- cost/injury;
- institutional pressure.

Prefer changing two.

## 2. WARFORMED FAILURE MODES TO AVOID

### WA-001 — No Filler Fights
If the outcome, lesson, relationship, or stakes are unchanged, cut or compress the fight.

### WA-002 — No Secrecy as Plot Glue
A secret may be withheld only when the text can identify a believable reason, risk, duty, trauma, jurisdiction, or consent boundary.

If a safe two-minute conversation would solve the conflict and there is no reason not to have it, let the characters talk.

### WA-003 — Drama Must Produce State Change
Repeated emotional conversations cannot merely restate the same tension. Each recurrence must alter trust, information, boundaries, choices, or future behavior.

### WA-004 — Slice of Life Has a Job
Ordinary meals, travel, jokes, classes, calls home, and dorm scenes are valuable because they create attachment and contrast. Compress them when they repeat an already established relationship state.

## 3. SANDERSON-STYLE STRUCTURAL TOOLS

### S-001 — Chapter Promise → Progress → Payoff
Every chapter identifies, implicitly or explicitly:
1. what question/problem/emotional pressure this chapter is about;
2. visible progress or complication;
3. a satisfying local payoff, reversal, or consequence;
4. a handoff into the next chapter.

A chapter may intentionally defer the larger answer, but it must not feel stationary.

### S-002 — Progress Signposts
Readers should regularly be able to answer: **what is different now?**

Signposts may be:
- new information;
- changed relationship behavior;
- rank/certification/role change;
- a new limitation;
- a solved subproblem;
- a new destination;
- a decision that closes one option and opens another.

### S-003 — Try / Fail / Learn / Reframe
Major abilities and strategies should often develop through:
- attempt;
- meaningful failure or partial success;
- diagnosis;
- revised model;
- later payoff.

Do not let a single lecture substitute for earned mastery.

### S-004 — Limitation-First Ability Design
When adding a new capability, define what it cannot do, what it costs, and what failure looks like before using it as a solution.

Limitations create plot.

### S-005 — Hard / Soft Boundary Balance
Keep the local ability mechanics hard enough that readers can predict solutions fairly while preserving soft/mysterious cosmology above them.

Characters may understand how to operate an interface long before they understand why the Boundary Field exists or what the Quieters are.

### S-006 — Strategic Worldbuilding
Worldbuilding enters prose through decisions, conflict, ordinary use, institutions, and consequences.

Do not pause the story to display research that does not change what a character does.

### S-007 — Character Triad
For every major recurring character, track:
- **Motivation:** what they want now;
- **Personality:** how they pursue it / how they sound;
- **Values:** what they will or will not trade to get it.

This is especially important when multiple characters share the same academy/team plot.

### S-008 — Convergence Payoff
Large finales should combine previously separate promises: character, progression, mystery, relationship, and world stakes.

The climax should feel surprising in arrangement but familiar in ingredients.

No late unseeded universal solution.

## 4. BOUNDARY-UNIVERSE CHAPTER GATE

Before a chapter is attested, verify:
- chapter Promise → Progress → Payoff is legible;
- at least one visible story-state delta exists;
- action geography is legible before speed;
- any progression has an observable delta and a cost/limit;
- no fight/test is filler;
- no secret exists only to delay a conversation;
- repeated emotional material changes state rather than repeating;
- supporting cast have distinct useful roles;
- worldbuilding changes decisions rather than pausing them;
- Pass 5 Story Completeness confirms context, causality, aftermath, and handoff.

## 5. NON-IMITATION RULE

These are abstract craft tools only. Do not reproduce protected characters, prose, scene choreography, terminology, distinctive devices, or plot sequences from any comparison author. The Boundary Universe must remain original under Canon Rule 1.
